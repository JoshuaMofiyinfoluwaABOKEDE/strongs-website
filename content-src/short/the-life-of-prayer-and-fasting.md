# Prayer in Scripture | What prayer is and how Jesus taught us to pray.
## What prayer is | Matthew 6:5-13; Philippians 4:6-7; Luke 18:1
Prayer is talking with God as a child talks with a father. Jesus taught us to begin, "Our Father which art in heaven" (Matthew 6:9). Prayer is not a performance for people (Matthew 6:5) or a machine that works by many words (Matthew 6:7). It is relationship.

The Lord's Prayer gives a pattern:

- **Worship**: "Hallowed be thy name."
- **Submission**: "Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done."
- **Provision**: "Give us this day our daily bread."
- **Forgiveness**: "Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors."
- **Protection**: "Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil."

Jesus "spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint" (Luke 18:1). Prayer is the believer's first resort, not the last. "Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer... let your requests be made known unto God" (Philippians 4:6).
## A daily prayer habit | Mark 1:35; Daniel 6:10; Psalm 5:3
Jesus rose "a great while before day" to pray in a solitary place (Mark 1:35). Daniel prayed three times a day, "as he did aforetime", even when it was dangerous (Daniel 6:10). Habits sustain prayer when feelings fade.

Building a habit:

1. **Set a time.** Morning is best for many: "in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee" (Psalm 5:3).
2. **Set a place.** A corner, a chair, a walk.
3. **Start small.** Ten honest minutes daily is better than one hour once a month.
4. **Pray the Bible.** Read a psalm, then pray its words back to God.
5. **Keep a list.** Write names and needs; note answers.
6. **Use ACTS** as a guide: Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication.

When you miss a day, do not give up. Return tomorrow. God is not an angry taskmaster but a Father waiting to meet you.

# Fasting rightly | Why and how believers fast.
## Fasting in the Bible | Matthew 6:16-18; Isaiah 58:3-7; Acts 13:2-3
Jesus said, "when ye fast", not "if ye fast" (Matthew 6:16). Fasting is going without food (or sometimes other things) for a period in order to seek God.

In Scripture people fasted:

- In **repentance** (Jonah 3:5-10, Nineveh).
- For **guidance** (Acts 13:2-3, sending Barnabas and Saul).
- In **danger** (Esther 4:16).
- In **mourning** and seeking God's mercy (Nehemiah 1:4).

But fasting can be done wrongly. In Isaiah 58 the people fasted while oppressing workers and quarrelling. God said the fast He chooses is to "loose the bands of wickedness... deal thy bread to the hungry" (Isaiah 58:6-7). Fasting does not twist God's arm. It humbles us, clears our hearts and expresses dependence on Him. Jesus also warned against fasting to be seen (Matthew 6:16-18).
## How to fast wisely | Matthew 4:1-4; Joel 2:12-13; Daniel 10:2-3
Practical guidance for fasting:

- **Have a purpose.** Write down why you are fasting.
- **Choose a type.** A partial fast (as Daniel ate "no pleasant bread", Daniel 10:3), skipping a meal, a day fast with water, or a set period.
- **Start small** if you are new: one meal, then a day.
- **Replace food time with prayer and Scripture.** Otherwise it is only a diet.
- **Be wise about health.** Drink water. Anyone pregnant, nursing, diabetic, on medication or with health problems should seek medical advice and choose a non-food fast instead.
- **Break the fast gently** with light food.
- **Keep it private** unless fasting together as a church.

"Turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting... and rend your heart, and not your garments" (Joel 2:12-13). The heart is what matters.

# Intercession for the harvest | Praying for others and for souls.
## Standing in the gap | Ezekiel 22:30; Genesis 18:23-33; 1 Timothy 2:1-4
God said, "I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land" (Ezekiel 22:30). An **intercessor** stands between God and people, pleading for them.

Abraham interceded for Sodom (Genesis 18). Moses pleaded for Israel after the golden calf (Exodus 32:11-14). Jesus "ever liveth to make intercession" for us (Hebrews 7:25).

Paul urges "supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks... for all men; for kings, and for all that are in authority" (1 Timothy 2:1-2), because God "will have all men to be saved" (verse 4). Intercession includes:

- Family and friends who do not know Christ.
- Leaders in church and nation.
- Missionaries and evangelists.
- The persecuted church.
- Your school, workplace and community.
## Praying for the harvest | Matthew 9:36-38; Romans 10:1; Colossians 4:2-4
Seeing the crowds "as sheep having no shepherd", Jesus said, "The harvest truly is plenteous, but the labourers are few; pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest, that he will send forth labourers" (Matthew 9:37-38).

Every great move of evangelism has been carried by prayer. Before outreach and crusades, STRONGS teams pray because only God can open hearts.

How to pray for the harvest:

- **Pray for specific people by name**, as Paul did for Israel (Romans 10:1).
- **Pray for labourers**: that God would send workers, including you.
- **Pray for open doors and boldness** (Colossians 4:3; Acts 4:29).
- **Pray for the Spirit's conviction** (John 16:8).
- **Pray for new converts** to stand and grow.

Start a "list of ten": ten people you will pray for daily until they come to Christ. Then look for chances to speak to them.

@assignment Practical assignment: a prayer and fasting plan
Write a one-page personal plan (300 to 500 words) and follow it for one week:

1. Your daily prayer time and place, and how you will use it (for example, ACTS).
2. One fast you will keep this week: its purpose, type and length, and any health precautions.
3. Your "list of ten" people you will pray for by name.
4. At the end of the week, a short reflection: what you learned, what was hard, and any answers to prayer you noticed.
