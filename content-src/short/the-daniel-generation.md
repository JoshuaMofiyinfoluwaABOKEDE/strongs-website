# Purpose in the heart | Deciding who you are before the pressure comes.
## Taken to Babylon | Daniel 1:1-7; Jeremiah 29:4-7
Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah were young men, probably teenagers, taken from Jerusalem to Babylon. They were chosen because they were "skilful in all wisdom, and cunning in knowledge, and understanding science" (Daniel 1:4). They were put through three years of Babylonian education and given new names honouring Babylon's gods (Daniel 1:7).

Babylon wanted to change their **language, learning, diet and identity**. Many young people today face something similar: schools, media and cities that shape how they think and who they become.

God had told the exiles through Jeremiah to "seek the peace of the city... and pray unto the LORD for it" (Jeremiah 29:7). Daniel did not run from Babylon or hate it. He studied its learning, served it well, and blessed it, while refusing to let it own his heart.

That is the Daniel generation: young people who **engage their world with excellence**, without losing their faith.
## Daniel purposed in his heart | Daniel 1:8-16; Proverbs 4:23
"But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank" (Daniel 1:8).

Notice:

- **He decided early**, in his heart, before the moment of pressure.
- **He chose his battles.** He accepted the Babylonian name and education but drew a line at defiling himself.
- **He was respectful.** He "requested of the prince of the eunuchs" (v. 8) and proposed a ten-day test (v. 12). No rudeness, no rebellion.
- **God gave him favour** (v. 9) and the test succeeded (v. 15).

Your convictions must be settled before the party, the exam malpractice offer, the temptation online, or the pressure from friends. "Keep thy heart with all diligence" (Proverbs 4:23).

Write down your convictions: what you will and won't do, and why, from Scripture.

# Excellence in learning | Studying as worship.
## Ten times better | Daniel 1:17-20; Colossians 3:23; Proverbs 22:29
"As for these four children, God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom... And in all matters of wisdom and understanding, that the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm" (Daniel 1:17, 20).

God gave them knowledge, **and** they studied for three years. Grace and hard work go together.

Excellence in learning is worship: "whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23). A Christian student should be known for:

- **Diligence**: attending classes, doing assignments, studying ahead.
- **Honesty**: no exam malpractice, plagiarism or "sorting" lecturers. God's favour never needs cheating.
- **Curiosity**: asking questions, reading beyond the syllabus.
- **Humility**: helping classmates, respecting teachers.

"Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings" (Proverbs 22:29). Daniel literally did.
## Habits of a good student | Proverbs 6:6-11; Ecclesiastes 9:10; James 1:5
"Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise" (Proverbs 6:6). The ant prepares in summer for winter.

Practical study habits:

- **Plan your week**: a timetable for classes, study, rest, church and family.
- **Study in short, focused blocks** (e.g. 25 to 45 minutes) with short breaks. Put your phone away.
- **Review regularly**: go over notes within a day of each class, then weekly. Cramming the night before is the least effective way to learn.
- **Test yourself**: past questions, flashcards, explaining a topic aloud to someone else.
- **Join or form a study group** with serious, honest students.
- **Ask for help early** when you don't understand.
- **Sleep and eat well**; the brain needs rest to remember.
- **Pray** for wisdom: "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God" (James 1:5). Then open your books.

# Faithful under pressure | Standing when it costs something.
## The fiery furnace | Daniel 3:13-30; 1 Peter 4:12-14
Nebuchadnezzar set up a golden image and commanded everyone to bow. Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refused. Threatened with the furnace, they answered: "our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. **But if not**, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods" (Daniel 3:17-18).

Their faith did not depend on the outcome. They trusted God's power **and** accepted His will. In the fire, a fourth figure walked with them, "like the Son of God" (v. 25), and they came out unharmed.

Today's furnaces may be:

- Pressure to cheat or give bribes.
- Mockery for your faith or purity.
- Losing friends or opportunities for refusing to compromise.

"Think it not strange concerning the fiery trial... if ye be reproached for the name of Christ, happy are ye" (1 Peter 4:12-14). God is with you in the fire.
## The lions' den and daily faithfulness | Daniel 6:1-23; Daniel 6:10; Galatians 6:9
Decades later, Daniel was a senior official. His enemies "could find none occasion nor fault; forasmuch as he was faithful" (Daniel 6:4). They could only attack his faith, so they got a law passed against prayer.

Daniel's response: "he went into his house; and his windows being open in his chamber toward Jerusalem, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, **as he did aforetime**" (Daniel 6:10).

The key words are *as he did aforetime*. Daniel's courage in the crisis grew from **daily habits** built over a lifetime. He was thrown to the lions, and God shut their mouths (6:22).

Lessons:

- Be so faithful in your work that the only charge against you is your faith.
- Build daily habits of prayer and the Word now.
- Stay faithful over the long run: Daniel served under several kings across about seventy years.

"Let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not" (Galatians 6:9).

@assignment Practical assignment: my Daniel plan
Write your personal "Daniel plan" (400 to 600 words):

1. **My convictions**: five things you have purposed in your heart, each with a Bible reference.
2. **My study plan**: a weekly timetable and three study habits you will build this term.
3. **My furnace**: one area where you face pressure to compromise, and how you will stand.
4. **My daily habit**: your plan for prayer and Bible reading.
5. **My accountability**: one person you will share this plan with.

Follow it for three weeks, then add a 150-word reflection.
