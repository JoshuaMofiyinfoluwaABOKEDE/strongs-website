@about Many Nigerian ministries have long sent teams to villages, preaching in markets and compounds and planting churches where none existed. This course focuses on the evangelism side of rural ministry: preparing and leading village outreach teams, preaching and storytelling in local languages, confronting fear of spirits with the power of Christ, and ensuring that converts are gathered, taught and left with a living church. It complements the Rural and Village Ministry course.

# Preparing the team | Before the journey.
## Called and sent | Luke 10:1-12; Mark 6:7-13; Romans 10:14-15
"The Lord appointed other seventy also, and sent them two and two before his face into every city and place, whither he himself would come" (Luke 10:1). "How shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach, except they be sent?" (Romans 10:14-15).

Village evangelism often happens through **short-term teams** (a weekend or a week) linked to **long-term workers** or local churches who stay.

Preparing the team:

- **Pray** together for weeks before going.
- **Choose** members who are spiritually mature, teachable and able to endure hardship.
- **Train**: gospel outline, storytelling, counselling, cultural respect, health and safety.
- **Assign roles**: leader, preachers, counsellors, music, children's ministry, health team, logistics, prayer.
- **Research** the community: language, leadership, religion, needs, local churches.
- **Prepare materials**: Bibles and portions in the local language, audio players, tracts, decision cards, first-aid kit, water.
## Respect, safety and endurance | 1 Corinthians 9:19-23; 2 Timothy 2:3; Proverbs 22:3
Paul became "all things to all men" (1 Corinthians 9:22) and told Timothy to "endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ" (2 Timothy 2:3).

**Respect**:

- Greet the traditional ruler and elders first.
- Dress modestly and appropriately.
- Eat what is offered where possible; thank your hosts.
- Never mock local customs or beliefs; speak truth with love.
- Avoid political or tribal topics.

**Safety**:

- Check security conditions with local contacts and authorities before travelling.
- Travel in daylight; keep the team together.
- Carry drinking water, basic medicines and a first-aid kit.
- Have a communication plan and an emergency contact.

**Endurance**: bad roads, heat, mosquitoes, simple sleeping places and opposition are normal. Joy in hardship is itself a witness.

# Proclaiming Christ | Preaching, storytelling and power.
## Open-air preaching and storytelling | Mark 4:33-34; Acts 14:15-17; Nehemiah 8:8
In Lystra, Paul preached to rural people about the living God "which made heaven, and earth" who "gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness" (Acts 14:15-17). He used their world as a bridge.

Village preaching:

- **Gather** people with music, a drama or a film in the evening; market days are good for daytime meetings.
- **Preach in the local language**, or with a skilled interpreter.
- **Use stories** from the Bible in sequence: creation, the fall, the flood or Abraham's sacrifice, the Passover, the prophets' promise, Jesus' life, death and resurrection, and the call to respond.
- **Use pictures** from farm life: seed, harvest, rain, the shepherd, the lost goat.
- **Keep it clear and short**, with repetition.
- **Ask questions** and let listeners retell stories.
- **Give a clear invitation**, without pressure.

Audio Bibles and recorded stories in local languages can keep speaking after the team leaves.
## Christ's power over fear | Colossians 2:15; Acts 19:18-20; 1 John 4:4
Many villagers live with fear of spirits, curses, witchcraft and ancestral obligations. The gospel announces that Christ, "having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it" (Colossians 2:15). "Greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world" (1 John 4:4).

In Ephesus, new believers "confessed, and shewed their deeds", and those who practised magic burned their books publicly: "So mightily grew the word of God and prevailed" (Acts 19:18-20).

In ministry:

- **Preach Christ's victory**, not fear of demons.
- **Pray** for the oppressed and sick with faith and compassion.
- **Call converts** to renounce idols and occult practices and trust Christ alone (1 Thessalonians 1:9).
- **Avoid sensationalism**: don't name or shame people as "witches"; such accusations have caused terrible harm, especially to children and elderly women.
- **Teach** new believers that in Christ they are free and safe.

# Gathering and leaving a church | Fruit that remains.
## Gathering converts | Acts 16:13-15; Acts 2:41-42; John 15:16
"I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain" (John 15:16).

After decisions:

- **Counsel** each person; record names, compounds and contacts.
- **Gather** them the next day for a simple teaching meeting.
- **Teach basics**: who Jesus is, assurance, prayer, turning from idols, meeting together, baptism.
- **Find a host**: a convert's compound (like Lydia's home, Acts 16:15) can become the meeting place.
- **Identify** potential leaders among the converts.
- **Connect** with the nearest Bible-believing church, which can provide oversight.
- **Leave** Scripture and audio resources in the local language.
## Follow-up from a distance | 1 Thessalonians 3:1-10; Acts 15:36; Titus 1:5
When Paul could not stay in Thessalonica, he sent Timothy "to establish you, and to comfort you concerning your faith" (1 Thessalonians 3:2), and rejoiced at his good report.

Keeping the fruit:

- **Return visits** on a schedule: after a week, a month, three months.
- **A local or nearby worker** assigned to the village.
- **Phone contact** where possible: calls, voice notes in the local language.
- **Train local leaders** with simple, reproducible studies.
- **Encourage** the new church to reach the next village.
- **Report and pray**: share news with supporting churches and keep praying.

Paul left Titus to "set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city" (Titus 1:5). Aim for a church that can stand and grow.
