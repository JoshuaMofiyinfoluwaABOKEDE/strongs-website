# Posture and breath | The foundation of every good voice.
## Posture for singing | Psalm 150:6; 1 Corinthians 6:19-20
"Let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD" (Psalm 150:6). Your body is your instrument, and it is the temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19). Caring for it is part of worship.

Good singing posture:

- **Feet** shoulder-width apart, weight balanced, knees soft (not locked).
- **Spine** long, as if a string gently lifts the crown of your head.
- **Shoulders** relaxed and down, not raised.
- **Chest** comfortably open, not puffed out.
- **Head** level; chin neither lifted nor pressed down.
- **Jaw, tongue and face** loose.

When sitting, sit forward on the chair with both feet flat and the back long.

Try this: stand against a wall with heels, hips, shoulders and head touching lightly. Step away and keep that alignment, then relax any stiffness. Poor posture squeezes the breath and tightens the throat. Good posture lets the voice flow freely.
## Breathing and support | Genesis 2:7; Ezekiel 37:9-10
God breathed into Adam "the breath of life" (Genesis 2:7). Breath powers the voice.

Singers use **low, deep breathing** (often called diaphragmatic breathing):

- Place a hand on your stomach just below the ribs.
- Breathe in slowly through the nose or mouth. Your **stomach and lower ribs expand** outward; your shoulders stay still.
- Breathe out on a long "sss". Try to keep the ribs open and let the stomach move in slowly.

**Breath support** means controlling the out-breath so the sound is steady.

Daily exercises (five minutes):

1. **Hiss**: breathe in for 4 counts, hiss out for 8, then 12, then 16.
2. **Pulse**: "ha-ha-ha" short bursts, feeling the stomach bounce.
3. **Sustain**: sing one comfortable note on "ah" for as long as it stays steady.

Don't gasp or lift your shoulders. Breathe early, at natural pauses in the phrase.

# Pitch and ear training | Singing in tune and hearing music clearly.
## Matching pitch | Psalm 33:3; 1 Chronicles 15:22
"Play skilfully with a loud noise" (Psalm 33:3). Chenaniah led the singing "because he was skilful" (1 Chronicles 15:22). Skill grows with practice.

**Pitch** is how high or low a note is. Singing **in tune** means matching the correct pitch.

Exercises:

- **Hum a note** played on a keyboard (or a phone piano app), then sing it on "ah". Does it match?
- **Slide** (siren) slowly up and down from your note until you find it.
- **Record yourself** and listen back. Many people sing flat without knowing.
- **Sing with a partner** who matches pitch well.

Common reasons for singing out of tune: not listening, tension, poor breath support, and singing outside your comfortable range. Most people who think they are "tone-deaf" can learn to sing in tune with patient practice.
## Training your ear | Nehemiah 12:42; 2 Chronicles 5:13
At the dedication of the temple the singers and trumpeters were "as one, to make one sound" (2 Chronicles 5:13). Unity in sound needs good ears.

Ear-training exercises:

- **Sol-fa scale**: sing *do re mi fa sol la ti do* up and down slowly with a keyboard.
- **Intervals**: learn the sound of common steps using songs you know. *Do* to *mi* (a third) and *do* to *sol* (a fifth) appear in countless hymns.
- **Echo singing**: someone sings a short phrase; you sing it back.
- **Hear the harmony**: listen to a choir recording and try to follow the alto or tenor line.
- **Hold your part**: sing a simple melody while someone else sings a different note.

Ten minutes a day, steadily, will sharpen your ear more than an occasional long session.

# Tone and resonance | Producing a clear, pleasant sound.
## Free and open tone | Psalm 98:4-6
A good tone is clear, free and pleasant, not pushed, breathy or strained. "Make a loud noise, and rejoice, and sing praise" (Psalm 98:4) does not mean shouting.

Keys to free tone:

- **Relax the jaw**: let it drop gently; don't clench.
- **Lift the soft palate**: feel the beginning of a yawn at the back of the mouth.
- **Keep the tongue forward**, tip resting behind the lower teeth.
- **Open the throat** without forcing.
- **Balance breath and sound**: too much air gives a breathy tone; too little and too much pressure gives a pressed, harsh tone.

Exercise: sigh gently from high to low on "hah". Then sing a five-note scale on "ah", keeping the same easy feeling.
## Resonance and vowels | Psalm 47:6-7
**Resonance** is the amplification and colouring of sound in the throat, mouth and nasal spaces. It lets a voice carry without strain.

Exercises:

- **Humming** on "mm" with lips closed lightly. Feel the buzz on the lips and nose. Then open to "mah".
- **"Ng"** (as in *sing*) slides up and down, then open to "ah".
- **"Nee-nay-nah-noh-noo"** on one note, keeping the buzz forward.

**Vowels** carry tone. Choirs sound unified when they shape vowels the same way. The five pure vowels **ee, eh, ah, oh, oo** should be clear and open. Avoid spreading "ah" into a wide smile or swallowing it at the back.

"Sing ye praises with understanding" (Psalm 47:7): good tone and clear vowels help people understand and join the praise.

# Range and agility | Extending your voice safely and moving it freely.
## Finding and extending your range | Proverbs 19:2
Every voice has a natural **range**. Choirs usually group voices as soprano and alto (higher and lower women's voices) and tenor and bass (higher and lower men's voices).

To find your range, slide down from a comfortable middle note to your lowest clear note, then up to your highest easy note. Your **comfortable range** is the part you can sing without strain.

To extend range safely:

- **Warm up first**, always.
- Use **sirens** on "oo" or "ng", sliding gently through the break between chest and head voice.
- Sing **five-note scales** moving up by half steps, stopping before strain.
- Use **lighter sound** as you go higher; don't push chest voice up.
- Progress over **weeks and months**, not in one day.

"He that hasteth with his feet sinneth" (Proverbs 19:2). Forcing high notes damages voices. Patience builds them.
## Agility and flexibility | Psalm 96:1-2
**Agility** is moving the voice quickly and accurately between notes, needed for runs and lively songs.

Exercises:

- **Lip trills** (lips buzzing like a motorbike) on scales. They balance breath and sound.
- **Staccato** "ha-ha-ha" on a five-note scale.
- **Arpeggios**: *do-mi-sol-do-sol-mi-do* on "ah".
- **Runs**: start slowly with a nine-note scale up and down; increase speed only when accurate.

Use a metronome app. Accuracy first, then speed.

Remember the purpose: "Sing unto the LORD a new song... shew forth his salvation from day to day" (Psalm 96:1-2). Vocal runs should serve the song and the message, not show off.

# Diction | Singing words clearly so people understand.
## Clear consonants and vowels | 1 Corinthians 14:15; Colossians 3:16
"I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the understanding also" (1 Corinthians 14:15). If the congregation cannot understand the words, the message is lost.

**Diction** is how clearly words are pronounced in singing.

- **Vowels** carry the tone; sustain them on long notes.
- **Consonants** carry the meaning; make them crisp and quick.
- **Final consonants** ("Lord", "God", "light") must be heard, together, at the same moment in a choir.
- Don't sing **through** consonants on long notes: in "love", hold "lo-" and put the "v" at the end.
- For **diphthongs** (two vowel sounds, as in "my" = "mah-ee"), hold the first vowel and add the second at the end.

Exercise: speak the lyrics slowly and clearly, then whisper them clearly, then sing them with the same clarity.
## Singing in different languages | Acts 2:6-11; Revelation 7:9-10
At Pentecost people heard "every man in his own language" (Acts 2:6). In heaven worship rises from "all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" (Revelation 7:9).

Many Nigerian choirs sing in English, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and other languages. Each needs care:

- **Learn the pronunciation** from a fluent speaker.
- **Tonal languages** like Yoruba and Igbo change meaning with pitch. Composers try to fit melody to the tones; singers should not distort them carelessly.
- **Understand the meaning** of what you sing, so you sing it with conviction.
- **Write a pronunciation guide** for choir members who don't speak the language.

Speaking, then whispering, then singing works here too. Clear words, sung with understanding, edify the church.

# Vocal health | Looking after your voice for a lifetime.
## Healthy voice habits | 1 Corinthians 6:19-20; 3 John 1:2
"I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health" (3 John 1:2). Your voice is a gift to steward.

Daily habits:

- **Drink water** through the day. Hydration keeps vocal folds healthy.
- **Warm up** before singing and **cool down** gently after (humming, slides).
- **Rest**: sleep well; voice fatigue often comes from tiredness.
- **Avoid shouting** and screaming at events, matches and markets.
- **Don't whisper forcefully** when hoarse; it strains too.
- **Limit throat clearing**; swallow or sip water instead.
- **Avoid smoking** and smoky environments.
- **Be careful with** very spicy food, alcohol and heavy meals right before singing if they affect you.
- **Use a microphone** properly instead of forcing volume.
## When the voice is unwell | Proverbs 14:15; Mark 6:31
Signs of strain: hoarseness, pain when singing, loss of high notes, a breathy or cracked voice, or a voice that tires very quickly.

What to do:

- **Rest your voice**. Speak little and gently; don't sing through pain.
- **Stay hydrated**; steam inhalation may soothe.
- **Tell your choir leader** rather than pushing through a performance.
- **See a doctor** (an ENT specialist if possible) if hoarseness lasts more than two weeks, if singing or speaking is painful, if you cough blood, or if your voice changes suddenly. Do not rely on home remedies for persistent problems.

Jesus told His disciples, "Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while" (Mark 6:31). Rest is not laziness; it protects the voice God gave you so you can keep serving for years.
