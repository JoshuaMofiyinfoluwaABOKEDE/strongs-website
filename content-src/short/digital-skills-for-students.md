# Documents | Word processing for assignments, letters and reports.
## Creating clear documents | Habakkuk 2:2; 1 Corinthians 14:40
"Write the vision, and make it plain" (Habakkuk 2:2). Most schoolwork, job applications and ministry reports are typed documents. You can use **Microsoft Word**, **Google Docs** (free, works on phones), or **LibreOffice Writer** (free).

Key skills:

- **Headings**: use the built-in Heading 1, Heading 2 styles rather than just making text bold. They create structure and allow an automatic table of contents.
- **Fonts**: one clear font (e.g. Calibri, Arial or Times New Roman) at 11 or 12 point for body text.
- **Line spacing**: 1.15 to 1.5 for readability (or as your school requires).
- **Margins**: usually 2.5 cm (1 inch).
- **Lists**: bullets for unordered items; numbers for steps.
- **Page numbers** and a **header/footer** with your name or title.
- **Tables** for data, with a header row.
- **Images** with captions; don't stretch them.
- **Save** with clear file names: `Surname_Course_Assignment1.docx`.
## Editing, sharing and formats | Proverbs 27:17; Luke 14:28
Good documents are revised, shared and saved safely.

- **Spelling and grammar check**: use it, but read carefully too.
- **Track changes and comments**: let others suggest edits you can accept or reject. Useful for group work and editing.
- **Sharing**: in Google Docs, share a link as "viewer", "commenter" or "editor". Be careful who can edit.
- **Version history**: Google Docs saves versions automatically; in Word, save new versions (v1, v2).
- **PDF**: export to PDF when sending a final document so the layout doesn't change on other devices.
- **Backups**: save important work in the cloud (Google Drive, OneDrive) **and** on another device or flash drive. A lost phone shouldn't mean a lost project.
- **Keyboard shortcuts** save time: Ctrl+C (copy), Ctrl+V (paste), Ctrl+Z (undo), Ctrl+S (save), Ctrl+F (find), Ctrl+B (bold).

"Count the cost" (Luke 14:28): an hour backing up is cheaper than losing a semester's work.

# Spreadsheets | Organising data and doing calculations.
## Spreadsheet basics | Numbers 1:1-3; Luke 16:10
God told Moses to "take ye the sum of all the congregation... by their families" (Numbers 1:2). Counting and recording well is part of faithful stewardship. "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much" (Luke 16:10).

A **spreadsheet** (Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc) is a grid of **cells**. Columns have letters (A, B, C), rows have numbers (1, 2, 3); a cell's address is like **B3**.

Uses: attendance lists, budgets, grades, contact lists, inventories, outreach records.

Good habits:

- **One header row** with clear column names (Name, Phone, Date, Amount).
- **One kind of data per column** (don't mix dates and text).
- **One record per row**.
- **Freeze the header row** so it stays visible while scrolling.
- **Format** numbers as currency (₦), dates as dates, phone numbers as text (so the leading 0 isn't lost).
- **Sort and filter** to find information quickly.
## Formulas and charts | Luke 14:28-30; Proverbs 27:23-24
Formulas start with **=**. Common ones:

- `=SUM(B2:B20)`: adds the numbers in B2 to B20.
- `=AVERAGE(C2:C20)`: the average.
- `=COUNT(B2:B20)`: how many numbers.
- `=MAX()` and `=MIN()`: highest and lowest.
- `=B2*C2`: multiply (e.g. quantity × price).
- `=IF(D2>=50,"Pass","Fail")`: a simple decision.

Copy a formula down a column by dragging the small square at the bottom-right of the cell.

**Charts**: select your data and insert a column, bar, line or pie chart. Give it a title and labels. Use charts to show trends (attendance over months) or comparisons (spending by category).

Example budget columns: Item | Quantity | Unit price | Total (`=B2*C2`) | then `=SUM(D2:D15)` at the bottom.

"Know the state of thy flocks" (Proverbs 27:23). Spreadsheets help you see the true state of things.

# Email and online safety | Professional communication and protecting yourself.
## Professional email | Colossians 4:6; Proverbs 15:23
"Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt" (Colossians 4:6). Email is still how schools, employers and scholarship bodies communicate.

- **Use a professional address**: firstname.lastname@gmail.com, not coolboy2009@...
- **Clear subject line**: "Application for Admission: Ade Ojo" or "Assignment 2 Submission: Biology 101".
- **Greeting**: "Dear Dr Okafor," or "Dear Admissions Office,".
- **Short paragraphs**: who you are, why you are writing, what you need.
- **Polite close**: "Thank you for your time. Yours sincerely, [full name], [phone]."
- **Attachments**: PDF, clearly named; mention them in the email.
- **Proofread** before sending; check the recipient address.
- **Reply** within a day or two; don't use text-speak ("pls", "u").
- **Reply all** only when everyone needs the reply.
## Staying safe online | Matthew 10:16; Proverbs 14:15
"Be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves" (Matthew 10:16). "The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going" (Proverbs 14:15).

Online safety basics:

- **Strong passwords**: long phrases, different for each account; use a password manager if possible.
- **Two-step verification** on email and social media.
- **Never share** OTPs, PINs, BVN or passwords with anyone, even someone claiming to be from your bank or school.
- **Beware of scams**: "You've won a scholarship/prize", job offers asking for payment, "urgent" messages from "friends" asking for money. Verify by calling the person directly.
- **Phishing links**: check the web address before logging in; don't click suspicious links.
- **Update** your phone and apps.
- **Privacy**: limit who sees your posts and location.
- **Digital footprint**: employers and universities may look at what you post.
- **Be kind** and avoid sharing false news; check before forwarding.

# Research skills | Finding, judging and using information well.
## Finding good information | Acts 17:11; Proverbs 18:17
The Bereans "searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so" (Acts 17:11). Good research checks claims.

Where to search:

- **Search engines** (Google): use specific words, quotation marks for exact phrases ("youth unemployment Nigeria"), and `site:` (e.g. `site:gov.ng`) for particular sites.
- **Google Scholar** for academic articles.
- **Libraries** (school, public, digital libraries).
- **Official sources**: government agencies, universities, international bodies (e.g. WHO, UNESCO, the National Bureau of Statistics).

Judging sources (ask **CRAAP** questions):

- **Currency**: is it recent?
- **Relevance**: does it answer my question?
- **Authority**: who wrote it? Are they qualified?
- **Accuracy**: is it supported by evidence? Do other good sources agree?
- **Purpose**: is it informing, selling, or pushing an agenda?

"He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him" (Proverbs 18:17). Check more than one source.
## Using sources honestly | Exodus 20:15; Exodus 20:16; Proverbs 12:22
**Plagiarism** is presenting someone else's words or ideas as your own. It is a form of stealing and lying (Exodus 20:15-16), and schools punish it severely.

Using sources honestly:

- **Quote** exact words in quotation marks, with the source.
- **Paraphrase** in your own words and sentence structure, still citing the source.
- **Cite** every source in the format your school uses (e.g. APA or Harvard): author, year, title, publisher or website, and link.
- **Keep a list** of sources as you research, so you don't lose track.
- **Use reference tools** like Zotero (free) or the citation tool in Word or Google Docs.

**AI tools** (chatbots) can help brainstorm or explain, but:

- They can produce errors and invented sources; **check everything**.
- Follow your school's rules on AI use.
- Never submit AI-written work as your own if it isn't allowed.

Honest work builds real skill and a good name.
