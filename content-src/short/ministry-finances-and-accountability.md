# Principles of stewardship | God's money, our trust.
## Stewards, not owners | 1 Corinthians 4:2; Luke 16:10-13; Haggai 2:8
"The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the LORD of hosts" (Haggai 2:8). Money given to God's work belongs to Him; leaders are **stewards**. "It is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:2).

Jesus linked faithfulness with money to spiritual trust: "If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?" (Luke 16:11).

Principles:

- **Faithfulness**: use money for its intended purpose.
- **Honesty**: no hidden spending, no inflated figures.
- **Accountability**: no one handles money alone.
- **Wisdom**: plan, save, avoid waste and unwise debt.
- **Generosity**: the ministry itself should give, including to the poor and to missions.
- **Contentment**: "not greedy of filthy lucre" (1 Timothy 3:3).

Judas "had the bag, and bare what was put therein" and stole from it (John 12:6). Even among the twelve, money needed oversight.
## Transparency protects | 2 Corinthians 8:16-24; Acts 5:1-11; Proverbs 11:1
When Paul collected money for the poor in Jerusalem, he sent **several trusted brothers** with it, "Avoiding this, that no man should blame us in this abundance which is administered by us: Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men" (2 Corinthians 8:20-21).

Paul did not say, "Trust me, I'm an apostle." He set up a system others could see.

Transparency:

- **Protects leaders** from temptation and false accusation.
- **Builds trust**, so people give more freely.
- **Honours God**, whose name is shamed by financial scandals.

Ananias and Sapphira lied about money and fell dead (Acts 5:1-11). God takes integrity in giving and handling money seriously.

# Budgets and controls | Planning and protecting funds.
## Budgeting | Luke 14:28-30; Proverbs 21:5; Proverbs 27:23-24
"Which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost?" (Luke 14:28).

A **budget** is a plan for income and spending over a period (usually a year).

Steps:

1. **Review** last year's actual income and spending.
2. **Estimate income** realistically: tithes, offerings, pledges, project giving, other sources.
3. **List expense categories**: ministry programmes, missions and evangelism, welfare, staff pay, rent and utilities, maintenance, equipment, administration, savings/reserve.
4. **Align with vision**: spend on what matters most.
5. **Approve** the budget through the proper body (board, council or members).
6. **Track monthly**: compare actual to budget and adjust.
7. **Keep a reserve** for emergencies (a few months of essential costs where possible).

Separate **restricted funds** (given for a specific purpose, such as a building or missions) and spend them only on that purpose.
## Financial controls | 2 Kings 12:9-15; Ezra 8:24-34; Nehemiah 13:13
When King Joash repaired the temple, the money chest was opened and counted by "the king's scribe and the high priest" together (2 Kings 12:10). Ezra weighed out the silver and gold to twelve priests, and it was weighed again on arrival, "by number and by weight of every one" (Ezra 8:24-34). Nehemiah appointed treasurers who "were counted faithful" (Nehemiah 13:13).

Essential controls:

- **Two or more people** always count offerings together, record totals and sign.
- **Bank promptly**; avoid keeping large cash.
- **Bank accounts in the ministry's name**, never a leader's personal account.
- **Two signatories** for withdrawals and transfers.
- **Approval process** for spending: requests, budget check, approval by someone other than the requester.
- **Receipts** for every payment.
- **Separation of duties**: the person who records money is not the only one who handles it.
- **Petty cash** limits with records.
- **Conflict of interest** rules: declare when buying from relatives' businesses.

# Records, reporting and fair pay | Showing faithfulness.
## Records, reports and audits | Luke 16:2; Romans 13:7; Proverbs 27:23
"Give an account of thy stewardship" (Luke 16:2).

**Records**:

- A **cash book** or accounting software recording every income and expense.
- **Offering slips** signed by counters.
- **Receipts and invoices** filed by month.
- **Bank statements** reconciled monthly with records.
- **Asset register**.

**Reports**:

- **Monthly** to the board or finance committee: income, expenses, budget comparison, bank balances.
- **Annually** to members and partners: a clear summary with explanations.
- **Project reports** to donors for restricted gifts.

**Audit**: an independent review of the accounts each year by a qualified accountant who is not part of the leadership. Act on the auditor's recommendations.

Simple, regular reporting prevents suspicion and catches mistakes early.
## Fair pay and financial temptations | 1 Timothy 5:17-18; 1 Timothy 6:6-10; Jeremiah 22:13
"The labourer is worthy of his reward" (1 Timothy 5:18). "Woe unto him that... useth his neighbour's service without wages" (Jeremiah 22:13).

**Fair pay**:

- Pay pastors and staff **fairly and on time**, with written agreements.
- Meet **legal obligations**: PAYE tax and pension where applicable.
- Avoid **extremes**: neither neglecting workers nor lavish packages that shame the gospel.
- Decisions on the senior leader's pay should be made by others (a board), not by the leader alone.

**Temptations to avoid**:

- **Mixing** personal and ministry money.
- **Manipulative fundraising**: pressure, guilt, or promises of guaranteed wealth for giving.
- **Debt** without a sound repayment plan.
- **Secret** arrangements and unexplained spending.
- **Love of money**: "the root of all evil" (1 Timothy 6:10).

"Godliness with contentment is great gain" (1 Timothy 6:6).
