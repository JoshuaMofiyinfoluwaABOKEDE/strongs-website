# The campus mission field | Students, culture and opportunity.
## Why campuses matter | Acts 19:8-10; 2 Timothy 2:2; Daniel 1:3-4
Paul "disputed daily in the school of one Tyrannus" for two years, "so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word" (Acts 19:9-10). Students come from many places and go back to many places: reach them and you reach regions and professions.

In Nigeria, campus fellowships have produced many pastors, missionaries and Christian professionals. William Kumuyi's ministry, which became the Deeper Life Bible Church, began in 1973 as a small Bible study group in Lagos among university people; its focus on careful Bible study and holy living spread widely.

Campus students are at a stage of:

- **Big questions**: identity, purpose, truth, God.
- **New freedom**, often away from home for the first time.
- **Pressure**: academics, money, relationships, cultism, fraud, substances.
- **Openness** to new ideas and friendships.

It is a time of decision. Many who come to Christ in school remain faithful for life.
## Understanding campus culture | 1 Chronicles 12:32; Acts 17:16-23; 1 Corinthians 9:22
Paul in Athens "his spirit was stirred in him" as he saw the city, and he engaged people "in the market daily with them that met with him", including philosophers (Acts 17:16-17).

Understanding your campus:

- **Who** are the students? Departments, faiths, backgrounds, languages.
- **Where** do they gather? Hostels, cafeterias, libraries, sports, clubs.
- **What** do they talk about and worry about?
- **What** worldviews are common? Nominal Christianity, Islam, traditional religion, secularism, "success" gospel, scepticism.
- **What** pressures exist? Cult groups, sexual exploitation, exam malpractice, financial hardship.
- **What** rules govern religious activities on campus?

Pray over your campus map, hostel by hostel. Like the men of Issachar, understand the times so you know what to do (1 Chronicles 12:32).

# Sharing Christ with students | Friendship, conversation and answers.
## Friendship and conversation | John 1:40-46; John 4:7-26; Colossians 4:5-6
Andrew found his brother Simon; Philip found Nathanael and said, "Come and see" (John 1:41, 46). Most students come to Christ through **friends**.

Friendship evangelism:

- **Be a real friend**: help with assignments, share meals, care in sickness.
- **Live visibly for Christ**: honesty in exams, purity, kindness, joy.
- **Pray** for five friends by name.
- **Ask questions**: "What do you think about God?", "What gives you hope for the future?"
- **Share your story** briefly and honestly.
- **Invite**: "Come and see" a Bible study, fellowship or outreach event.
- **Share the gospel clearly** when there is an opening (Romans Road or a simple outline).

**Hostel and classroom witness**: respect roommates' space; don't preach at people at night when they want to sleep; avoid disrupting lectures. Earn the right to speak. "Walk in wisdom toward them that are without" (Colossians 4:5).
## Answering students' questions | 1 Peter 3:15; Acts 17:22-31; Jude 1:22
"Be ready always to give an answer... with meekness and fear" (1 Peter 3:15). "Of some have compassion, making a difference" (Jude 1:22).

Questions students often ask:

- **"Is the Bible reliable?"** It has strong manuscript support, fits archaeology and history in many details, and its central claims about Jesus were made by eyewitnesses.
- **"Can science and faith go together?"** Many scientists have been Christians; science studies how creation works; faith knows the Creator.
- **"Why does God allow suffering?"** Admit the difficulty; God entered our suffering in Christ and will end it.
- **"Aren't all religions the same?"** Jesus uniquely claims to be the way (John 14:6) and rose from the dead.
- **"Isn't Christianity for weak people?"** Everyone needs grace; Christ calls the strong and weak alike to humility.
- **"Church people are hypocrites."** Some are; Jesus is not. He calls us to follow Him.

Listen first. Answer honestly. Admit what you don't know and find out. Keep pointing back to Jesus.

# Campus outreach and discipleship | Events, follow-up and growth.
## Outreach events | Acts 5:42; Acts 2:14-41; Luke 14:21-23
"They ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ" (Acts 5:42).

Campus outreach ideas:

- **Freshers' welcome week**: help new students, give welcome packs, invite them to fellowship.
- **Hostel outreach**: small teams, room-to-room with permission, short conversations, tracts.
- **Open-air preaching** where allowed.
- **Gospel concerts, drama and film nights**.
- **Question forums**: "Ask anything about God" nights with panels.
- **Exam-season outreaches**: study sessions, prayer, encouragement cards.
- **Service projects**: tutorials, blood donation drives, hospital visits.
- **Social media campaigns** with testimonies and short videos.

Plan with permission from school authorities, coordinate with other fellowships, and always have counsellors and follow-up ready.
## Discipling students | 1 Thessalonians 2:7-12; Colossians 2:6-7; 2 Timothy 2:2
New student believers face strong pressures. "As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him: Rooted and built up in him" (Colossians 2:6-7).

Discipleship plan:

- **Contact within 48 hours**; meet in person.
- **Assign** a mature student as a discipler.
- **Basic lessons**: assurance, prayer, the Word, the Holy Spirit, fellowship, witnessing, holiness.
- **Bible study groups** by hostel or department.
- **Connect** to a local church as well as the fellowship.
- **Help with academics**; a failing student is under pressure to compromise.
- **Train** them to reach their own friends.

Kumuyi's ministry is known for structured, systematic Bible study. Students grow strong when they learn to study Scripture for themselves, not only to hear sermons. Then pass it on: "commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2).
