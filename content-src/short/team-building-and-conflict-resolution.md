# Healthy teams | Many members, one body.
## One body, many members | 1 Corinthians 12:12-27; Ecclesiastes 4:9-12; Nehemiah 4:6
"For as the body is one, and hath many members... so also is Christ" (1 Corinthians 12:12). "The eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee" (verse 21). "Two are better than one... a threefold cord is not quickly broken" (Ecclesiastes 4:9, 12).

Nehemiah's builders finished the wall because "the people had a mind to work" (Nehemiah 4:6).

Marks of a healthy team:

- **Shared purpose**: everyone knows and owns the goal.
- **Trust**: members are honest and reliable.
- **Clear roles**: everyone knows their part.
- **Different gifts valued**: no one is despised.
- **Good communication**: information flows; issues are raised early.
- **Accountability**: commitments are kept.
- **Care**: members pray for and support one another.
- **Celebration**: wins are shared.
## Building trust and clarity | Philippians 2:1-4; Amos 3:3; Acts 13:1-3
"Can two walk together, except they be agreed?" (Amos 3:3). "Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves" (Philippians 2:3).

Building the team:

- **Pray together** regularly. The Antioch leaders "ministered to the Lord, and fasted" together (Acts 13:2).
- **Agree on vision and values**, written down.
- **Define roles** and decision rights.
- **Hold regular meetings** with an agenda, honest discussion and clear actions.
- **Know each other**: share testimonies, eat together, celebrate.
- **Be reliable**: do what you say.
- **Admit mistakes** quickly; leaders go first.
- **Assume the best** about teammates (1 Corinthians 13:7).
- **Give and receive feedback** kindly.

Trust is built slowly and lost quickly. Guard it.

# Communication and conflict | Why conflict happens and how to start well.
## Understanding conflict | James 4:1-2; Acts 15:36-40; Acts 6:1
"From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members?" (James 4:1). Some conflict comes from sin: pride, envy, selfish ambition.

Other conflict comes from honest differences. Paul and Barnabas disagreed sharply over John Mark (Acts 15:39) and parted; later Paul spoke well of Mark (2 Timothy 4:11). In Acts 6 a complaint about neglected widows exposed a real problem that needed a new structure.

Common sources of team conflict:

- Unclear roles or expectations.
- Poor communication; rumours.
- Different personalities and working styles.
- Disagreements about direction or methods.
- Competition for recognition or resources.
- Unresolved hurts.

Conflict is not always bad. Handled well, it can reveal problems, sharpen ideas and deepen relationships. Handled badly, it divides churches.
## Speaking and listening | James 1:19-20; Proverbs 15:1; Ephesians 4:15, 29
"Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God" (James 1:19-20). "A soft answer turneth away wrath" (Proverbs 15:1). "Speaking the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15).

Communication habits that prevent and calm conflict:

- **Listen to understand**, not to reply. Repeat back what you heard: "So you feel that..."
- **Use 'I' statements**: "I felt left out when the decision was made without me", not "You always ignore me."
- **Focus on the issue**, not the person.
- **Avoid exaggerations** ("always", "never").
- **Choose the right time and place**: privately, calmly, not by angry message.
- **Watch your words**: "Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying" (Ephesians 4:29).
- **Don't gossip**: speak *to* people, not *about* them.

# Resolving conflict | Biblical steps to peace.
## The biblical process | Matthew 7:3-5; Matthew 18:15-17; Matthew 5:23-24
Jesus gave clear steps:

1. **Examine yourself first**: "first cast out the beam out of thine own eye" (Matthew 7:5). What is my part?
2. **Go directly and privately**: "If thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother" (Matthew 18:15).
3. **Take one or two others** if that fails (18:16): wise, neutral people to help.
4. **Tell it to the church** (leaders) if still unresolved (18:17).

Also: if you know someone has something against you, go to them, even before worship (Matthew 5:23-24).

Don't skip steps. Most conflicts can be resolved at step 1 or 2 if done humbly. Going to others first (gossip) or straight to public confrontation usually makes things worse.
## Forgiveness, mediation and restoration | Ephesians 4:31-32; Philippians 4:2-3; Colossians 3:13
Paul pleaded with two women in the Philippian church, Euodias and Syntyche, "that they be of the same mind in the Lord", and asked a "true yokefellow" to help them (Philippians 4:2-3). Mediation is biblical.

**Forgiveness**: "forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (Ephesians 4:32). Forgiveness releases the debt; it does not always mean trust is instantly restored or that there are no consequences, but it ends revenge and bitterness.

**Mediation** steps (for a neutral leader):

- Meet each side separately first to listen.
- Bring them together with ground rules (respect, no interruptions).
- Let each explain their view and feelings; summarise fairly.
- Identify shared goals and the real issues.
- Explore solutions together; agree on specific steps.
- Pray; follow up later.

**Restoration**: aim to "gain thy brother", not just win. Celebrate reconciliation. Where serious sin or abuse is involved, follow proper church processes and, where necessary, the law; reconciliation must never put victims at risk.
