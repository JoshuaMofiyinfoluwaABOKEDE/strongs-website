# Faithful stewards | Leadership as service and stewardship.
## The towel and the basin | Mark 10:42-45; John 13:1-17
The disciples argued about who would be greatest. Jesus answered: "whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister: And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all. For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many" (Mark 10:43-45).

On the night before the cross, Jesus "riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel" and washed His disciples' feet (John 13:4-5). Then He said, "I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you" (John 13:15).

The world's leaders "exercise lordship". Christ's leaders serve. Servant leadership does not mean weakness or having no authority. It means using authority for the good of others, not for status, money or applause. The question is not "How can they serve me?" but "How can I serve them and God's purpose?"
## Stewards who must give account | 1 Corinthians 4:1-2; Luke 12:42-48; Hebrews 13:17
Paul calls leaders "ministers of Christ, and stewards of the mysteries of God. Moreover it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:1-2).

A **steward** manages what belongs to someone else. Leaders steward:

- **People** entrusted to them.
- **Time**, opportunities and influence.
- **Money and resources** of the ministry.
- **The message** of the gospel.

Jesus described the "faithful and wise steward" who gives others "their portion of meat in due season" (Luke 12:42), and warned that "unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required" (verse 48).

Leaders "watch for your souls, as they that must give account" (Hebrews 13:17). Leadership is not ownership. One day every leader will stand before the Owner.

# Character before gifting | Who you are matters more than what you can do.
## The qualifications of a leader | 1 Timothy 3:1-7; Titus 1:6-9
When Paul listed qualifications for church leaders, almost all concern **character**: "blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behaviour, given to hospitality, apt to teach; Not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre; but patient, not a brawler, not covetous" (1 Timothy 3:2-3).

Only one item, "apt to teach", is a skill. The rest are about **who the leader is**: self-controlled, honest, peaceable, generous, faithful at home, respected outside the church.

Why? Because leaders are examples. People follow what leaders **are** more than what they say. A gifted leader with poor character will eventually damage people and the name of Christ.

Ask yourself honestly:

- Am I the same person in private as in public?
- How do I handle money?
- How do I treat people who can do nothing for me?
- How do I respond to criticism?
## Guarding your heart | Proverbs 4:23; 1 Corinthians 10:12; 1 Timothy 4:16
"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life" (Proverbs 4:23). "Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine" (1 Timothy 4:16).

Common ways leaders fall:

- **Pride**: thinking we are indispensable or above correction.
- **Money**: careless or dishonest handling of funds.
- **Sexual sin**: often begins with small compromises and secrecy.
- **Prayerlessness**: running on gifting while the spiritual life dries up.
- **Isolation**: no one close enough to warn us.

Guards:

- A daily walk with God in prayer and the Word.
- **Accountability**: trusted people who can ask hard questions.
- **Clear boundaries** with money and relationships (e.g. two people count offerings; avoid compromising situations).
- **Rest** and family time.
- Quick **confession** and repentance.

"Let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall" (1 Corinthians 10:12).

# Planning and delegation | Leading a team to get things done.
## Vision and planning | Nehemiah 2:11-18; Proverbs 16:3; Proverbs 21:5
Nehemiah heard of Jerusalem's broken walls, **prayed** (Nehemiah 1), **surveyed** the walls by night (2:13-15), **shared the vision** ("Come, and let us build up the wall", 2:17), and organised the people to build. The wall was finished in 52 days (6:15).

Planning steps for any project:

1. **Pray** and seek counsel.
2. **Define the goal** clearly: what will success look like? (Make it specific and measurable.)
3. **List the tasks** needed.
4. **Set a timeline** with deadlines.
5. **Count the cost**: money, people, materials (Luke 14:28).
6. **Assign responsibilities**.
7. **Review progress** regularly and adjust.

"Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established" (Proverbs 16:3). Planning is not unspiritual; it is faithful stewardship.
## Delegation | Exodus 18:13-26; Acts 6:1-7
Moses judged the people from morning to evening until Jethro said, "The thing that thou doest is not good. Thou wilt surely wear away... for this thing is too heavy for thee" (Exodus 18:17-18). Jethro advised him to choose "able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness" and share the load.

The apostles did the same in Acts 6: they appointed seven men to serve tables so they could give themselves "to prayer, and to the ministry of the word" (verse 4). The result: "the word of God increased" (verse 7).

How to delegate well:

- **Choose faithful people** with the right character and ability.
- **Explain clearly**: the goal, the deadline, the limits of their authority.
- **Provide resources** and training.
- **Trust them**; don't micromanage.
- **Check in** at agreed times.
- **Give credit** publicly; correct privately.

Delegation multiplies ministry and develops new leaders.

# Shepherding people | Caring for those you lead.
## The heart of a shepherd | John 10:11-14; 1 Peter 5:1-4; Ezekiel 34:2-4
"I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep... and know my sheep, and am known of mine" (John 10:11, 14).

Peter urged leaders: "Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind; Neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock" (1 Peter 5:2-3).

God condemned shepherds who fed themselves but not the flock: "The diseased have ye not strengthened, neither have ye healed that which was sick... neither have ye sought that which was lost" (Ezekiel 34:4).

Shepherding means:

- **Knowing** your people by name and situation.
- **Feeding** them with the Word.
- **Protecting** them from false teaching and harm.
- **Seeking** those who drift away.
- **Caring** for the weak and hurting.
## Patience, conflict and encouragement | Galatians 6:1-2; 1 Thessalonians 5:14; Matthew 18:15
"Warn them that are unruly, comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, be patient toward all men" (1 Thessalonians 5:14). Different people need different care.

When there is **conflict**:

- Go **privately** first (Matthew 18:15).
- **Listen** to all sides before judging (Proverbs 18:17).
- Aim for **reconciliation**, not winning.
- Restore those who fall "in the spirit of meekness" (Galatians 6:1).
- Involve others only when needed, and keep confidences.

**Encourage** often. Paul's letters are full of thanks and praise for his co-workers (Romans 16). Notice effort, thank people by name, celebrate growth.

Shepherding is slow work. Results may take years. But "when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away" (1 Peter 5:4).

@assignment Practical assignment: a leadership project plan
Choose a real project you lead or help lead (a fellowship meeting, outreach, Bible Club, choir programme, or class project). Write a 400 to 600 word plan that includes:

1. The goal, stated specifically.
2. A list of tasks with a timeline.
3. The people you will delegate to, and how you will brief and support them.
4. How you will care for the team spiritually during the project.
5. One area of your own character you will guard during it, and how.

After the project (or a month of working on it), add a 200-word review.
