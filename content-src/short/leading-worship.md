# The worshipper's life | Leading others begins with your own walk with God.
## Worship is more than music | John 4:23-24; Romans 12:1; Isaiah 29:13
Jesus said, "the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him" (John 4:23). Worship is the response of our whole life to who God is. Paul calls it presenting our bodies "a living sacrifice" (Romans 12:1).

Music is one powerful expression of worship, but not the whole. God warned of people who "draw near me with their mouth... but have removed their heart far from me" (Isaiah 29:13).

So a worship leader must first be a **worshipper**:

- A daily life of prayer and the Word.
- Obedience in private, not only zeal in public.
- Humility before God and people.
- Love for the congregation you lead.

You cannot lead people where you have not been. Before you ask the church to lift their hands, lift your heart in your room.
## The leader's character | 1 Samuel 16:7; 1 Timothy 4:12; Psalm 24:3-4
"Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? or who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart" (Psalm 24:3-4). God "looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7).

Marks of a godly worship leader:

- **Purity**: a clean life matters more than a powerful voice.
- **Humility**: you point to Christ, not to yourself.
- **Teachability**: you accept correction from pastors and team.
- **Faithfulness**: you prepare, arrive on time and keep commitments.
- **Servanthood**: you serve the congregation and the team, not your own preferences.
- **Example**: "be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity" (1 Timothy 4:12).

Gifting can draw a crowd; character keeps a ministry. Many talented musicians have fallen because they neglected the secret place.

# Songs that teach | Choosing songs that are biblical and help people worship.
## Truth in song | Colossians 3:16; Psalm 47:7; Ephesians 5:19
"Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs" (Colossians 3:16). Songs **teach**. People often remember the theology of their songs more than their sermons.

Test every song:

- **Is it true?** Do the lyrics agree with Scripture? Watch for vague, self-centred or unbiblical lines.
- **Is it about God?** Does it focus on who He is and what He has done in Christ, not only on our feelings?
- **Is it clear?** Will the congregation understand the words?
- **Is it singable?** Is the range comfortable and the melody learnable?
- **Does it fit the moment?** Praise, confession, thanksgiving, response to the Word.

"Sing ye praises with understanding" (Psalm 47:7). A song with a great beat and weak words is a poor teacher.
## Variety and the church's song | Psalm 96:1; Revelation 5:9-13; Psalm 136:1
The Bible includes many kinds of songs: shouts of praise, quiet trust, laments, confession, and thanksgiving. Psalm 136 repeats a refrain every verse; Psalm 96 calls for "a new song".

Healthy variety:

- **Old and new**: hymns carry the faith of past generations; new songs express today's worship. Use both.
- **Local and global**: songs in English, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Pidgin and others, so everyone can join. Heaven's worship includes "every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation" (Revelation 5:9).
- **Fast and slow**: celebration and reflection.
- **Different themes**: God's holiness, Christ's cross, the Spirit, the church's mission, the hope of His return.

Build a **song list** for your church: songs you know well, grouped by theme and key. Introduce new songs slowly, perhaps one a month, and repeat them so people learn them.

# Planning a set | Putting songs together with purpose and flow.
## Flow and purpose | 1 Chronicles 16:4-36; Psalm 100:4
"Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise" (Psalm 100:4). Many leaders plan a set that moves from **praise** (celebrating God's works), through **worship** (adoring who He is), to **response** (surrender, prayer, or preparing for the Word).

Planning steps:

1. **Pray and talk with your pastor** about the theme of the service and sermon.
2. **Choose a purpose** for the set (e.g. "God's faithfulness").
3. **Select songs** that serve that purpose, usually three to six.
4. **Order them** for flow: tempo, theme and energy.
5. **Check keys** so transitions are smooth and the range fits the congregation (most can sing roughly from A below middle C to the D an octave above).
6. **Plan transitions**: a short prayer, a Scripture reading, an instrumental link, or a key change.
7. **Plan timing** and stay within it.

David appointed Levites "to record, and to thank and praise the LORD" (1 Chronicles 16:4). Planning is spiritual work.
## Scripture, prayer and flexibility | Acts 2:42; Acts 16:25
Worship sets are stronger when they include more than songs:

- **Scripture reading** between songs (a psalm, or a verse that connects the theme).
- **Short prayers** of thanksgiving or confession.
- **Brief exhortation**, not a sermon: one or two sentences that explain why we sing the next song.
- **Silence**: a moment to reflect.

Prepare well, but stay **flexible**. The Holy Spirit may lead you to repeat a song, extend a time of prayer, or move on early. Paul and Silas sang at midnight in prison (Acts 16:25); worship is not bound to a programme.

Flexibility is not an excuse for poor preparation. A well-prepared team can follow the Spirit's leading because they are not struggling with the basics. Agree on signals with the band (e.g. a hand sign for "repeat the chorus").

# Leading people | Leading a congregation and a team on the day.
## On the platform | 1 Corinthians 14:15-16; Nehemiah 8:6
When Ezra blessed the LORD, "all the people answered, Amen, Amen, with lifting up their hands" (Nehemiah 8:6). A leader's job is to help the **congregation** worship, not to perform for them.

On the platform:

- **Start clearly**: announce or introduce the first song; give the starting line.
- **Make eye contact** and look at the people, not only at your lyrics.
- **Sing the melody clearly**, at a comfortable pitch.
- **Give cues**: sing or speak the first words of the next line when needed.
- **Use simple, warm words**: "Let's sing this together"; avoid long speeches.
- **Watch the congregation**: are they singing? If not, a song may be too high, too new or too long.
- **Model worship**: let your posture and expression invite participation, without drawing attention to yourself.

Paul asks how the "unlearned" can say Amen if they cannot understand (1 Corinthians 14:16). Lead so that everyone can join.
## Leading a team | Nehemiah 4:16-21; Philippians 2:3-4
Nehemiah organised builders and guards so that "every one... had his sword girded by his side, and so builded", with the trumpeter beside him (Nehemiah 4:18). Good leaders organise people well.

Leading a worship team:

- **Share the plan early**: songs, keys, order and recordings, at least a few days ahead.
- **Rehearse** before the service, not only on the platform.
- **Assign roles**: lead vocal, harmonies, instruments, sound.
- **Communicate kindly and clearly**. Correct in private, praise in public.
- **Pray together** before every service.
- **Care for your team**: know their lives, not just their skills.
- **Develop others**: give newer members opportunities to grow; train your replacement.
- **Work with the sound team**: a good sound check prevents many problems.

"Let each esteem other better than themselves" (Philippians 2:3). A humble leader builds a team that serves together for years.

@assignment Practical assignment: plan and lead a worship set
Plan a 20 to 25 minute worship set for a church service, fellowship or STRONGS meeting.

Hand in: (1) the theme and the passage or sermon it connects to; (2) the song list with keys, order and a one-line reason for each song; (3) planned Scripture readings, prayers and transitions; and (4) after leading it (or rehearsing it with a team if you have no opportunity to lead), a 250 to 400 word reflection on what went well, what you would change, and any feedback from your pastor or team.
