# The campus as a mission field | Vision and identity.
## Why campus ministry matters | Acts 19:8-10; Daniel 1:17-20; Matthew 9:37-38
In Ephesus, Paul taught daily "in the school of one Tyrannus. And this continued by the space of two years; so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word" (Acts 19:9-10). A lecture hall became a base for reaching a whole region.

Campuses gather future teachers, doctors, engineers, lawyers, pastors, politicians and parents. Students are at a decisive age: forming beliefs, habits and relationships that last a lifetime. Students reached today will carry the gospel to every sphere tomorrow.

A campus fellowship exists to:

- **Win** students to Christ.
- **Build** them in the Word and prayer.
- **Send** them as disciples into every career and community.

Like Daniel and his friends, Christian students should be marked by excellence and integrity in their studies as well as faith.
## Vision and identity | Proverbs 29:18; Philippians 1:27; 1 Corinthians 1:10
"Where there is no vision, the people perish" (Proverbs 29:18). Each executive should agree on a **vision** for the session: "This session, we will help every member study the Bible daily and reach every hostel with the gospel."

Know your identity:

- Is the fellowship **interdenominational**? Then major on the essentials of the gospel and avoid divisive debates over secondary issues (1 Corinthians 1:10).
- What is your relationship with the **school authorities**? Register properly and follow rules.
- What is your relationship with **local churches**? Encourage members to belong to a church; don't compete with churches.
- What are your **doctrinal basics**? Have a clear statement of faith.

"Stand fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together for the faith of the gospel" (Philippians 1:27).

# Organising the fellowship | Executives, units and meetings.
## The executive team | Exodus 18:21-23; Acts 6:3; 1 Corinthians 12:12-27
Jethro advised Moses to choose "able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness" and share the work (Exodus 18:21).

Typical executive roles:

- **President/Coordinator**: overall leadership, vision, representing the fellowship.
- **Vice-president(s)**: support; often oversee units.
- **General secretary**: records, minutes, correspondence.
- **Bible study secretary**: Bible study programme and teachers.
- **Prayer secretary**: prayer meetings and intercession.
- **Evangelism secretary**: outreaches and follow-up.
- **Music/choir coordinator**.
- **Treasurer/financial secretary**: money and records (two people to count and sign).
- **Welfare and sisters'/brothers' coordinators**.
- **Media and publicity**.

Choose by **character and faithfulness**, not popularity. Write **role descriptions**. Hold a **weekly executive meeting** with prayer, planning and accountability.
## Meetings that build | Acts 2:42; Hebrews 10:24-25; 1 Corinthians 14:26
The early church "continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers" (Acts 2:42).

A weekly rhythm might include:

- **Sunday fellowship service** (if your fellowship holds one) or a main meeting.
- **Bible study** in small groups (by level, department or hostel).
- **Prayer meeting**.
- **Unit meetings**: choir, drama, evangelism, media, ushering, welfare.
- **Executive meeting**.

Make meetings **purposeful**: start and end on time, teach the Word, involve many members, and keep announcements short. "Let all things be done unto edifying" (1 Corinthians 14:26).

**Avoid overload**: students must study. Better a few strong meetings than many tired ones.

# Outreach, academics and handover | Reaching students and finishing well.
## Reaching the campus | Acts 5:42; Colossians 4:5-6; John 1:40-42
"Daily in the temple, and in every house, they ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ" (Acts 5:42). Andrew "first findeth his own brother Simon" and brought him to Jesus (John 1:41).

Campus outreach ideas:

- **Hostel-to-hostel** and room-to-room evangelism (with respect for rules and privacy).
- **Freshers' welcome**: help new students with registration, orientation and accommodation; invite them to fellowship.
- **Campus crusades**, concerts, drama and film nights.
- **Discussion forums** on questions students ask (purpose, suffering, science and faith).
- **Tracts and social media** campaigns.
- **Service projects**: tutorials, exam preparation, blood drives, care for sick students.
- **Friendship evangelism**: each member praying for and reaching five friends.

**Follow-up**: record contacts, visit within 48 hours, assign each new believer to a small group, and connect them with a church.
## Excellence and handover | Daniel 1:17-20; Colossians 3:23; 2 Timothy 2:2
Fellowship leaders must not fail their courses for the sake of ministry. Daniel and his friends were "ten times better" in learning (Daniel 1:20). Poor grades damage your witness and future.

Balance:

- **Plan your week**: classes, study, rest, ministry.
- **Delegate**: don't do everything yourself.
- **Say no** to extra commitments during exams.
- **Study with others**; help weaker members academically.
- **Keep your devotional life** strong; it sustains everything else.

**Handover**: every executive serves for a season. Prepare the next set:

- Identify and train potential leaders throughout your tenure (2 Timothy 2:2).
- Involve them in planning and decisions.
- Prepare a **handover file**: constitution, vision, calendar, contacts (school authorities, churches, patrons), finances and accounts, inventory, minutes, lessons learned.
- Hold a proper **handover meeting**, pray for the new executive, and stay available as an adviser without interfering.

@assignment Practical assignment: a fellowship session plan
Write a session plan (600 to 900 words) for a campus fellowship you lead or belong to:

1. A vision statement for the session and the fellowship's identity (interdenominational status, statement of faith, relationships).
2. An executive structure with brief role descriptions and a weekly meeting rhythm.
3. A Bible study and prayer programme for the session.
4. One detailed outreach plan with a follow-up system.
5. A personal plan for balancing ministry and academics.
6. An outline of your handover file.
