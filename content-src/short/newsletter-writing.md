# Planning a newsletter | Purpose, readers and rhythm.
## Why newsletters matter | Philippians 1:3-7; Colossians 4:7-9
Paul's letters often included news and personal updates. He sent Tychicus to the Colossians so "that he might know your estate, and comfort your hearts" (Colossians 4:8). He thanked the Philippians "for your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now" (Philippians 1:5).

A newsletter does the same: it keeps people **informed, connected and involved**. Types include:

- **Prayer letters** from missionaries and ministries.
- **Church or fellowship bulletins**.
- **Partner updates** for donors and volunteers.
- **Email newsletters** for a community, school or business.

Before writing, decide:

- **Purpose**: inform, encourage, ask for prayer, raise support, invite?
- **Readers**: partners, members, parents, students?
- **Frequency**: monthly or quarterly is common. Consistency matters more than frequency.
- **Format**: email, PDF, WhatsApp broadcast, or print.
## A content calendar | Ecclesiastes 3:1; Proverbs 21:5
"To every thing there is a season" (Ecclesiastes 3:1). A **content calendar** plans what goes in each issue.

Typical newsletter sections:

- **Lead story**: one main story with a photo (an outreach, a testimony, a milestone).
- **Short updates**: two to four brief items.
- **Prayer points**: specific and current.
- **Upcoming events** with dates and links.
- **A call to action**: pray, give, volunteer, attend, share.
- **Thanks**: to partners, volunteers or hosts.

Plan for the year: Easter, Christmas, camp seasons, school calendars, annual events. Keep a running list of story ideas as they happen, with photos and notes.

Set a production schedule: gather content by day X, write by day Y, review by day Z, send on the same day each month.

# Writing that gets read | Stories, updates and clear actions.
## The lead story | Acts 14:27; Psalm 107:2
When Paul and Barnabas returned, "they rehearsed all that God had done with them" (Acts 14:27). Readers want to know what God is doing, through **people** and **stories**.

Writing the lead story:

- **Start with a person or moment**, not with general statements. Not "Our outreach was successful", but "Halima had never heard the gospel in Hausa until Saturday afternoon at the motor park."
- **Keep it short**: 200 to 400 words for email.
- **Show impact** with one or two honest numbers ("74 people gave their lives to Christ; 52 have been visited by our follow-up team").
- **Include a good photo** with a caption, with consent.
- **End with meaning** and a link to the call to action.

Be truthful. Don't exaggerate numbers or stories. Readers trust what is honest and specific.
## Clear, warm and actionable | Colossians 4:6; Philippians 4:6
Newsletter writing style:

- **Write to one reader**: "you", warm and personal.
- **Short paragraphs** and **headings** so readers can scan.
- **Plain language**: avoid jargon and long church phrases.
- **Specific prayer points**: not "Pray for our ministry", but "Pray for the 52 new believers as they join local churches this month."
- **One main call to action**, clearly stated and easy to do: a button, link, account number, or reply.
- **Gratitude**: thank readers sincerely and specifically.
- **Brevity**: say less, better. Link to more for those who want it.

"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God" (Philippians 4:6). Your newsletter can help readers do that for your work.

Proofread names, dates and figures carefully. One wrong date can cause confusion.

# Design, sending and results | Getting the newsletter to readers.
## Subject lines and design | Proverbs 25:25; Habakkuk 2:2
"As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country" (Proverbs 25:25). The **subject line** decides whether an email is opened.

Subject lines:

- Short (under about 50 characters) and specific.
- Promise a story or value: "Halima's first Hausa Bible", "52 new believers, and how you can pray".
- Avoid all capitals, many exclamation marks or words that look like spam ("FREE!!!", "URGENT").

**Design**:

- One column for phones; most readers use them.
- Your logo and consistent colours at the top.
- A clear lead image, headings, short paragraphs.
- Buttons for links.
- Contact details and social links at the bottom.
- For print or PDF: A4, clear fonts, enough white space.
## Tools, consent and measuring | Romans 13:1; Matthew 7:12
**Tools**: email services such as Mailchimp, MailerLite or Brevo have free plans, templates and sign-up forms. For smaller lists, a well-formatted email or PDF on WhatsApp can work.

**Consent and privacy**:

- Only add people who have **agreed** to receive the newsletter.
- Include a clear **unsubscribe** link and honour it.
- Use **BCC** (or a newsletter tool) so you don't expose everyone's email addresses.
- Protect personal data; don't share your list. Nigeria's data protection law (the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023) and similar laws elsewhere expect this. "Be subject unto the higher powers" (Romans 13:1).
- Get permission before featuring people's names and photos.

**Measuring**: newsletter tools show **open rates** and **click rates**. Watch trends: which subject lines and stories work? Ask readers for feedback. Then improve the next issue.

@assignment Practical assignment: write a complete newsletter
Plan and write one complete newsletter for a real ministry, church, fellowship or organisation (or for STRONGS). Hand in:

1. A one-paragraph plan: purpose, readers, frequency and format.
2. A three-month content calendar.
3. The full newsletter: subject line, lead story (200 to 400 words with a captioned photo), two short updates, specific prayer points, one call to action, and thanks.
4. A 150-word note on how you will handle consent, privacy and measuring results.
