# What is news? | News values and finding stories.
## Truth-telling as a calling | Exodus 23:1-2; Proverbs 12:17; Zechariah 8:16
"Thou shalt not raise a false report... Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil" (Exodus 23:1-2). "He that speaketh truth sheweth forth righteousness: but a false witness deceit" (Proverbs 12:17). "Speak ye every man the truth to his neighbour" (Zechariah 8:16).

Journalism at its best is **truth-telling in public**: informing people so they can make decisions, holding power to account, and giving a voice to the voiceless. In an age of rumours and fake news, careful reporting is a service to God and neighbour.

**News values**: what makes a story newsworthy?

- **Timeliness**: it's new.
- **Proximity**: it's close to your audience.
- **Impact**: it affects many people or matters deeply.
- **Prominence**: well-known people or institutions are involved.
- **Conflict** or **controversy**.
- **Human interest**: emotion, unusual stories.
- **Novelty**: the unexpected.
## Finding stories | Nehemiah 1:2-4; Proverbs 25:2
When Nehemiah "asked them concerning the Jews that had escaped... and concerning Jerusalem", he learned that "the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down" (Nehemiah 1:2-3). Good reporters **ask questions** and **listen**.

Where stories come from:

- **Your beat**: a regular area you cover (campus, health, local government, churches, sport).
- **Events**: meetings, launches, protests, outreaches, results.
- **Documents**: budgets, reports, court records, official announcements.
- **People**: students, traders, officials, workers, elders. Ask, "What's happening that people should know?"
- **Follow-ups**: what happened after the story everyone forgot?
- **Data**: numbers that reveal a trend.

Keep a notebook of story ideas and contacts. "It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter" (Proverbs 25:2).

# Gathering and verifying | Interviews, sources and facts.
## Interviewing sources | Proverbs 18:13; Proverbs 20:5; James 1:19
"He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him" (Proverbs 18:13).

Interviewing:

- **Identify yourself** and your publication; explain the story.
- **Prepare questions**, but listen and follow up.
- Ask **open questions**: "What happened?", "Why did you decide...?", "What happens next?"
- Ask for **specifics**: names, numbers, dates, places.
- **Record accurately**: notes and, with permission, audio. Read back key quotes if unsure.
- Clarify **terms**: on the record (can be quoted and named), on background (can use the information but not name the source), off the record (not to be published). Agree before the interview.
- Interview **several sides**, especially in a dispute.
- Thank them; get contact details for follow-up questions.
## Verification and attribution | Deuteronomy 19:15; Acts 17:11; Proverbs 14:15
"At the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established" (Deuteronomy 19:15). Journalists apply the same principle.

**Verification**:

- Confirm important facts with **at least two independent sources**.
- Check **documents** and official records.
- Be sceptical of viral posts, screenshots and forwarded messages; trace them to the original source.
- Check images with a **reverse image search** to see if they are old or from elsewhere.
- Check names, titles, numbers and dates carefully.
- If you can't verify something, **don't publish it** as fact.

**Attribution**: tell readers where information came from.

- "said the Vice-Chancellor, Prof. Ada Okeke."
- "according to the state Ministry of Health's report released on Monday."
- Use **"said"** as the neutral verb; avoid loaded verbs like "admitted" or "claimed" unless accurate.

Separate **fact** from **opinion**. News stories report; opinion pieces argue.

# Writing the story and ethics | News, features and responsibility.
## The inverted pyramid and features | Habakkuk 2:2; Luke 1:1-4
**News stories** use the **inverted pyramid**: most important information first, less important details later. Readers can stop at any point and still have the key facts.

The **lead** (first sentence or two) answers as many of the **5 Ws and H** as possible: **who, what, when, where, why, how**.

> The University of Ibadan will reopen its main library on Monday after a three-month renovation funded by alumni, the librarian, Dr Tunde Bello, said on Thursday.

Then: key details, quotes, background, reactions, what happens next. Keep paragraphs short (one or two sentences).

**Features** are longer, more narrative stories: profiles, in-depth reports, human-interest pieces. They can start with a scene or person (a **delayed lead**), use description and quotes, and explore context and meaning. Luke's careful research "from the very first" (Luke 1:3) is a model of patient, ordered reporting.
## Ethics and care | Matthew 7:12; Proverbs 31:8-9; Micah 6:8
"Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction... plead the cause of the poor and needy" (Proverbs 31:8-9). "Do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God" (Micah 6:8).

Core ethics (found in most journalism codes):

- **Seek truth and report it**: accuracy, fairness, context.
- **Minimise harm**: show compassion for victims, grieving families, children and vulnerable people. Don't publish names of minors involved in crimes or victims of sexual violence. Avoid graphic images that strip dignity.
- **Act independently**: refuse bribes and "brown envelopes"; declare conflicts of interest.
- **Be accountable**: correct mistakes promptly and openly.
- **Give the right of reply**: anyone accused should have a chance to respond before publication.
- **Avoid plagiarism**: credit others' reporting.
- **Know the law**: defamation, privacy and contempt of court rules exist; check with an editor.

"All things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them" (Matthew 7:12).

@assignment Practical assignment: a news story and a feature
Report on something real in your campus, church or community.

1. Write a 400-word news story in the inverted pyramid with a strong lead, at least two named sources, and accurate attribution.
2. Write an 800-word feature (a profile or human-interest story) with a delayed lead, description and quotes.
3. Hand in your interview notes, a list of how you verified three key facts, and a 150-word note on any ethical decisions you made (consent, privacy, right of reply).
