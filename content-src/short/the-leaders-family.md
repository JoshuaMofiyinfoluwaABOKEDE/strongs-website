# The home as first ministry | Ruling your own house well.
## Ruling well at home | 1 Timothy 3:4-5; Joshua 24:15; Deuteronomy 6:6-7
A leader must be "one that ruleth well his own house, having his children in subjection with all gravity; (For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?)" (1 Timothy 3:4-5).

The home is the first place where a leader's faith is seen and tested. Joshua declared, "as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15). God's words were to be taught "diligently unto thy children... when thou sittest in thine house" (Deuteronomy 6:7).

"Ruling well" does not mean harsh control or perfect children. It means:

- **Loving leadership**: serving the family as Christ serves the church.
- **Spiritual nurture**: prayer and the Word at home.
- **Order and discipline** with gentleness.
- **Presence**: time and attention, not only provision.

A ministry that destroys the leader's family has a broken foundation.
## Warnings from Scripture | 1 Samuel 2:12-17, 22-25; 1 Samuel 8:1-5; 2 Samuel 13:21
Eli was a priest, but "the sons of Eli were sons of Belial; they knew not the LORD" (1 Samuel 2:12). God judged Eli because "his sons made themselves vile, and he restrained them not" (1 Samuel 3:13).

Samuel was a great prophet, yet "his sons walked not in his ways, but turned aside after lucre, and took bribes" (1 Samuel 8:3). Israel used it as a reason to ask for a king.

David was "very wroth" when Amnon sinned against Tamar (2 Samuel 13:21), but did nothing, and the family fell into violence.

Lessons:

- Public success does not guarantee family health.
- **Neglect** and **failure to correct** have consequences.
- Busy leaders can lose touch with their own children.

These warnings are not meant to condemn, but to wake us: our families deserve our best, not our leftovers.

# Marriage and children | Love, partnership and parenting in ministry.
## A marriage that lasts | Ephesians 5:25-33; 1 Peter 3:7; Ecclesiastes 9:9
"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it" (Ephesians 5:25). Wives and husbands are "heirs together of the grace of life" (1 Peter 3:7). "Live joyfully with the wife whom thou lovest" (Ecclesiastes 9:9).

Ministry pressures on marriage:

- **Time**: endless meetings, calls and travel.
- **Emotional drain**: carrying others' burdens leaves little for home.
- **Expectations**: congregations may expect the spouse to be an unpaid co-pastor.
- **Criticism** of the leader that wounds the spouse.
- **Money** pressures.
- **Temptation** from close ministry relationships.

Protecting the marriage:

- **Weekly time together** (a "date"), even simple.
- **Daily communication**: share hearts, not just schedules.
- **Pray together**.
- **Honour your spouse publicly** and never criticise them from the pulpit.
- **Agree together** on ministry commitments and the spouse's role; respect their own calling and gifts.
- **Guard purity** and avoid emotional affairs.
- **Seek help early**: marriage counselling is wise, not shameful.
## Raising children in a ministry home | Ephesians 6:4; Proverbs 22:6; Colossians 3:21
"Ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord" (Ephesians 6:4). "Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged" (Colossians 3:21).

Children of leaders often face pressure: constant watching, unrealistic expectations, sharing their parents with everyone, hearing criticism of their parents.

Helping them:

- **Let them be children**, not models for the congregation.
- **Give them time**: attend their events; have regular one-on-one time.
- **Protect their privacy**: don't use them as sermon illustrations without permission.
- **Make faith real at home**: family devotions that are short, warm and honest; let them ask questions.
- **Discipline with love**, consistently, and never to protect your reputation.
- **Don't force ministry roles** on them; let their own calling grow.
- **Apologise** when you're wrong.
- **Shield them** from church conflict and gossip.

Children who see Christ in their parents at home are likely to love Him themselves.

# Boundaries and rhythms | Time, money, privacy and singleness.
## Time, money and privacy | Mark 6:31; Exodus 20:8-10; 1 Timothy 5:8
Jesus told His busy disciples, "Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while" (Mark 6:31). God commanded a Sabbath (Exodus 20:8-10). "If any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith" (1 Timothy 5:8).

**Time boundaries**:

- A weekly **day off** guarded like an appointment.
- **Family time** on the calendar: meals, outings, holidays.
- Limits on evening meetings and late calls; true emergencies excepted.
- **Phone-free** times at home.

**Money**:

- Provide for the family; budget and save.
- Agree together on giving and spending.
- Avoid putting the family under pressure from unpaid ministry costs.
- Plan for children's education and retirement.

**Privacy**:

- The home is not a public office. Set visiting hours where needed.
- Keep family matters private.
- Teach the church to respect the family's space.
## Single leaders and healthy support | 1 Corinthians 7:32-35; Ecclesiastes 4:9-10; Psalm 68:6
Paul valued singleness as an opportunity for undistracted devotion (1 Corinthians 7:32-35). Single leaders also need a "family": "God setteth the solitary in families" (Psalm 68:6).

For single leaders:

- **Build community**: close friends, mentors and spiritual family.
- **Keep healthy rhythms**: rest, meals, exercise.
- **Guard purity** with boundaries and accountability.
- **Avoid overwork**; singleness is not unlimited availability.
- **Seek God** about marriage without pressure from others; wait wisely.

For every leader's household:

- Have **friends outside the congregation** where you can be yourselves.
- **Accountability** for family health, not only ministry results.
- **Counselling** when needed.
- **Annual review** as a family: how are we really doing?

"Two are better than one... if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow" (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10).

@assignment Practical assignment: a family rhythm and boundary plan
Write a 400 to 700 word plan (with your spouse if married):

1. Your weekly rhythm: day off, family or personal time, devotions.
2. Boundaries for time, phone use, visitors and privacy.
3. A financial plan for the household.
4. For married leaders: three ways you will invest in your marriage. For parents: how you will spend time with each child and protect them from ministry pressure. For single leaders: your support network and boundaries.
5. Who you will ask to hold you accountable for your home life.
