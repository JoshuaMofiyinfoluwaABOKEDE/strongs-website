# Why follow-up matters | Babies need care, and so do new believers.
## Born again, but newborn | 1 Peter 2:2; 1 Thessalonians 2:7-8; Acts 15:36
Peter calls new believers "newborn babes" who need "the sincere milk of the word" (1 Peter 2:2). A healthy baby left alone will not survive. A new believer left alone often falls back.

Paul knew this. He cared for the Thessalonians "even as a nurse cherisheth her children", giving them "not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls" (1 Thessalonians 2:7-8). After his first missionary journey he said, "Let us go again and visit our brethren in every city... and see how they do" (Acts 15:36).

New believers face:

- **Doubt**: "Did anything really happen?"
- **Opposition** from family, friends or old companions.
- **Temptation** to return to old habits.
- **Ignorance**: they don't yet know how to pray, read the Bible or find a church.

Follow-up is the bridge between a decision and a disciple. Jesus commanded us not only to make converts but to "teach them to observe all things" (Matthew 28:20).
## The goal: disciples, not decisions | Matthew 28:19-20; Colossians 1:28; Luke 14:27
The Great Commission says "teach all nations" (literally *make disciples*), "baptizing them... teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:19-20).

A **disciple** is a learner and follower of Jesus who:

- Trusts Christ for salvation.
- Is growing in obedience and holiness.
- Is part of a church family.
- Is learning to share faith with others.

Jesus did not lower the cost: "whosoever doth not bear his cross, and come after me, cannot be my disciple" (Luke 14:27). Follow-up is not keeping people happy; it is helping them follow Christ wholeheartedly.

Paul's aim was "that we may present every man perfect (mature) in Christ Jesus" (Colossians 1:28). Counting hands at a crusade is not the finish line. The finish line is a mature believer making other disciples.

# The first weeks | Practical care right after a decision.
## The first 48 hours and the first visit | Acts 9:10-19; Proverbs 27:23; Hebrews 3:13
After Saul's conversion, Ananias visited him within days, laid hands on him, and called him "Brother Saul" (Acts 9:17). That first visit shaped Paul's life.

In the first **48 hours**:

- **Call or message**: "Hello ___, it's ___ from the outreach. I'm so glad you gave your life to Jesus. How are you?"
- **Arrange a visit** in a public place or at their home with another believer.

During the first visit:

- Listen to their story.
- Review the gospel and **assurance** (1 John 5:11-13).
- Teach them to **pray** simply and to **read** a short passage daily (start with the Gospel of John).
- Invite them to church and offer to go with them.
- Pray together and agree on the next meeting.

"Exhort one another daily... lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin" (Hebrews 3:13). Consistency matters more than eloquence.
## Basic lessons for new believers | 1 John 1:9; John 15:5; Hebrews 10:24-25
Over the first four to six weeks, teach short, simple lessons, one per meeting:

1. **Assurance of salvation** (1 John 5:11-13).
2. **Forgiveness and confession** when we sin (1 John 1:9).
3. **Prayer**: talking with God daily (Matthew 6:9-13).
4. **The Bible**: God's word for daily life (Psalm 119:105).
5. **The Holy Spirit**: God living in us (John 14:16-17).
6. **Church and fellowship** (Hebrews 10:24-25).
7. **Witnessing**: telling others (Acts 1:8).
8. **Baptism**: obeying Christ publicly (Matthew 28:19).

Use questions, not lectures: "What does this verse say? What does it mean for you this week?" Give a memory verse each time. Celebrate small steps. "Without me ye can do nothing" (John 15:5): keep pointing them to Christ, not to yourself.

# Discipling one person | Walking with someone over months.
## The Jesus pattern | Mark 3:14; 2 Timothy 2:2; 1 Corinthians 11:1
Jesus chose twelve "that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach" (Mark 3:14). Discipleship is first **being with** someone.

Paul followed the same pattern. "Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ" (1 Corinthians 11:1). He invested in Timothy and told him to pass it on: "the things that thou hast heard of me... commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2). That verse contains four generations: Paul, Timothy, faithful men, others.

Discipling one person means:

- **Regular meetings**: weekly if possible, for months.
- **The Word together**: read and discuss a book of the Bible.
- **Prayer together**: teach by praying.
- **Life together**: invite them into your ordinary life, ministry and outreach.
- **Accountability**: ask honest questions with love.

You don't need to be perfect, only a step ahead and honest.
## Helping a disciple grow | Hebrews 5:12-14; Galatians 6:1-2; Ephesians 4:15
Growth is not always smooth. Expect:

- **Slow seasons**: some disciples stall. Be patient; keep praying and showing up.
- **Falls**: "if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness" (Galatians 6:1). Restore, don't reject.
- **Hard questions**: welcome them. Study the answers together.
- **Life problems**: school, money, family. "Bear ye one another's burdens" (Galatians 6:2), and connect them with others who can help.

Move from milk to meat (Hebrews 5:12-14): from basic lessons to studying whole books, serving, and sharing the gospel.

Watch for these signs of growth: a daily walk with God, victory over old sins, love for other believers, faithfulness in church, and a desire to win others. When you see them, start encouraging your disciple to disciple someone else. "Speaking the truth in love" we "grow up into him in all things" (Ephesians 4:15).

# Rooted in the church | No believer grows alone.
## Why church matters for new believers | Acts 2:41-47; Hebrews 10:24-25; Ephesians 2:19-22
After Pentecost, those who received the word "were baptized" and "continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers" (Acts 2:41-42). Conversion led straight into church life.

The church gives a new believer:

- **Teaching** from faithful leaders.
- **Fellowship** and friendship.
- **Worship** and the Lord's Supper.
- **Accountability** and pastoral care.
- **A place to serve** with their gifts.

"Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is" (Hebrews 10:25). A coal removed from the fire soon goes cold.

STRONGS is an interdenominational ministry. Our follow-up connects new believers to a **sound, Bible-believing local church** near where they live, preferably one they already have links with. We do not compete with churches; we serve them.
## Making the connection | Romans 16:1-2; Acts 9:26-28; 1 Corinthians 12:18-27
When Saul came to Jerusalem, the disciples were afraid of him, "But Barnabas took him, and brought him to the apostles" (Acts 9:27). New believers often need a Barnabas.

Practical steps:

- **Ask** if they have a church or family links to one.
- **Recommend** a nearby Bible-believing church and **go with them** the first time.
- **Introduce** them to the pastor or a leader, as Paul commended Phoebe to the church in Rome (Romans 16:1-2).
- **Help them join** a house fellowship, Bible study or youth group.
- **Encourage baptism** and membership classes.
- **Keep in touch** for several months.

Every member matters in Christ's body (1 Corinthians 12:18-27). Help your new believer find their place and begin to serve. Then your follow-up has done its work: a disciple rooted in a church, growing and bearing fruit.
