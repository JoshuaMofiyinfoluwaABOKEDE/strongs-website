# What makes leadership spiritual | Influence that comes from God.
## Leadership is influence under God | Romans 13:1; 1 Corinthians 2:1-5; Acts 6:3
J. Oswald Sanders famously summarised leadership as **influence**: the ability of one person to move others. But in God's work, the question is **where that influence comes from**.

**Natural leadership** relies on personality, education, confidence, charisma and position. These are gifts from God and can be useful, but by themselves they produce only natural results.

**Spiritual leadership** is influence that flows from a life filled with and governed by the Holy Spirit. When the apostles chose the seven, they looked for men "of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom" (Acts 6:3). Paul's power at Corinth was "not with enticing words of man's wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power" (1 Corinthians 2:4).

A spiritual leader moves people **toward God**, not merely toward the leader's goals. The test is: are people becoming more like Christ because of my leadership?
## Called, not self-appointed | Hebrews 5:4; Jeremiah 23:21; Galatians 1:15-16
"No man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron" (Hebrews 5:4). God complained through Jeremiah of prophets who ran without being sent (Jeremiah 23:21).

Spurgeon taught his students to test their call: an intense, lasting desire for the work; some aptness to teach; evidence of fruit; and the confirmation of God's people. Gbile Akanni has often warned against ministries built on ambition rather than on God's commission.

Signs of a genuine call:

- A burden that will not go away, tested in prayer over time.
- Character that matches the work.
- Gifts that others recognise and confirm.
- Fruit in small, unseen service first.
- Willingness to wait for God's timing, as Moses, David and Paul did.

The called leader can endure hardship, because the work is not his own idea.

# The qualities God looks for | Character and spiritual life.
## Character before charisma | 1 Timothy 3:1-7; 1 Samuel 16:7; Titus 1:7-9
When Samuel looked at Eliab's height, God said, "the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7).

Qualities Scripture stresses for leaders:

- **Discipline**: "temperate", self-controlled (Titus 1:8). A leader who cannot rule himself cannot lead others.
- **Vision**: seeing what God wants to do, and faith to pursue it.
- **Wisdom**: applying truth rightly in real situations.
- **Decision**: willingness to decide after prayer and counsel.
- **Courage**: facing opposition (Joshua 1:9).
- **Humility**: the greatest leaders in Scripture were servants.
- **Integrity**: "blameless" in reputation (1 Timothy 3:2).
- **Patience** and **sympathy** with weak people.
- **Faithfulness** over a long time.

These qualities grow through the Spirit's work and through testing. No leader has them all perfectly; every leader must keep growing.
## Filled with the Spirit | Ephesians 5:18; Acts 4:8-13; Galatians 5:22-25
"Be filled with the Spirit" (Ephesians 5:18) is a command for every believer, and an absolute necessity for leaders. When Peter, "filled with the Holy Ghost", spoke before the council, they "marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus" (Acts 4:8, 13).

Being Spirit-filled shows itself in:

- **The fruit of the Spirit** (Galatians 5:22-23), especially love, patience and self-control.
- **Boldness** in witness (Acts 4:31).
- **Wisdom** in decisions (Acts 15:28: "it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us").
- **Sensitivity** to God's leading (Acts 16:6-10).

How leaders keep being filled:

- Daily surrender: "walk in the Spirit" (Galatians 5:25).
- Confession of known sin.
- Feeding on the Word.
- Prayer and dependence.
- Obedience to what God has already shown.

Gifts without the Spirit's fruit have ruined many ministries. The Spirit's presence is the leader's greatest need.

# The cost and the finish | Paying the price and finishing well.
## The cost of leadership | Mark 10:38-39; 2 Corinthians 11:23-28; 2 Corinthians 4:8-10
Jesus asked James and John, who wanted the highest seats: "Can ye drink of the cup that I drink of?" (Mark 10:38). Leadership has a cost.

Sanders described several costs that every spiritual leader pays:

- **Self-sacrifice**: time, comfort, money and preferences.
- **Loneliness**: leaders often walk ahead and carry burdens others cannot see.
- **Fatigue**: the work is demanding (2 Corinthians 11:27-28: "the care of all the churches").
- **Criticism**: leaders will be misunderstood and opposed.
- **Rejection**: even Jesus was rejected by many.
- **Pressure and perplexity**: hard decisions with no easy answers.

Paul summarised: "We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair; Persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed" (2 Corinthians 4:8-9). The cost is real, but so is God's sustaining grace.
## Perils and finishing well | 2 Timothy 4:6-8; 1 Corinthians 9:27; 2 Chronicles 26:16
King Uzziah was "marvellously helped, till he was strong. But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction" (2 Chronicles 26:15-16). Success can be more dangerous than struggle.

Perils of leaders:

- **Pride** and self-importance.
- **Jealousy** of other leaders' success.
- **Popularity** that makes us fear people more than God.
- **Indispensability**: thinking the work depends on us.
- **Elation and depression** that swing with results.
- **Moral compromise** in private.

Paul feared becoming "a castaway" after preaching to others (1 Corinthians 9:27), so he disciplined himself. At the end he could say: "I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith" (2 Timothy 4:7).

Finishing well requires a daily walk with God, accountability, rest, humility, and preparing others to continue the work.

@assignment Practical assignment: my leadership covenant
Write a personal leadership covenant (500 to 700 words):

1. Your understanding of God's call on your life and how it has been confirmed.
2. An honest assessment of yourself against five qualities from Module 2, with one area for growth.
3. The costs of leadership you are facing or expect to face, and how you will bear them.
4. Three perils you are most vulnerable to, with practical safeguards (including who will hold you accountable).
5. A short prayer of commitment.

Share it with a mentor or pastor and include their comments if possible.
