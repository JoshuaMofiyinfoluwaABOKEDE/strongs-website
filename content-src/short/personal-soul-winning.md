# The gospel we preach | Knowing the message before we share it.
## What is the gospel? | 1 Corinthians 15:1-4; Romans 1:16; Mark 1:15
The word *gospel* means "good news". Paul summarises it: "Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; and that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).

The gospel is **news about what God has done**, not advice about what we should do. It is "the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth" (Romans 1:16). Our job is not to make it powerful but to tell it faithfully.

The gospel has a call: "repent ye, and believe the gospel" (Mark 1:15). Good news invites a response.

What the gospel is not:

- Not "come to Jesus and all your problems will disappear".
- Not "be good and God will accept you".
- Not "join our church".

It is the message of a holy God, sinful people, a crucified and risen Saviour, and the call to repent and believe.
## A simple outline | Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Romans 5:8; Romans 10:9-10
Many soul winners use a simple outline such as the **Romans Road**:

1. **God's standard and our sin**: "all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).
2. **The penalty**: "the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord" (Romans 6:23).
3. **God's provision**: "God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8).
4. **Our response**: "if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved" (Romans 10:9).

Learn these four verses by heart, with their references. Practise explaining them in your own words in under five minutes, in English and in your local language. You can also use your **testimony**: what your life was like before Christ, how you came to Him, and how He has changed you.

# Starting conversations | Moving naturally from everyday talk to the gospel.
## Seeing people as Jesus did | John 4:1-26; Matthew 9:36; Colossians 4:5-6
Jesus met a Samaritan woman at a well (John 4). He was tired, she was an outsider, and the culture said they should not speak. Yet He began with something ordinary, "Give me to drink", and moved step by step to "living water" and finally to "I that speak unto thee am he."

Lessons from the well:

- **Notice people.** Jesus "saw the multitudes" and was moved with compassion (Matthew 9:36).
- **Cross barriers** of tribe, status and comfort.
- **Start where they are**: weather, work, studies, news, a need.
- **Ask questions** and listen.
- **Move toward spiritual things** without forcing.

Paul said, "Walk in wisdom toward them that are without... Let your speech be alway with grace" (Colossians 4:5-6). Friendly, honest conversation opens doors that preaching at people closes.
## Bridges into the gospel | Acts 17:22-31; Acts 8:30-35; 1 Peter 3:15
Useful bridge questions:

- "Do you go to church anywhere? What do you think about God?"
- "If you died tonight, are you sure you would go to heaven? Why?"
- "Can I share what changed my life?"
- "Can I pray for you about anything?" Then, after praying: "Can I share something that has given me peace?"

In Athens Paul started with their altar "TO THE UNKNOWN GOD" and led to Christ's resurrection (Acts 17:22-31). Philip asked the Ethiopian, "Understandest thou what thou readest?" (Acts 8:30).

**Tracts** help: give one, ask a question about it, or leave it with someone you cannot talk to long.

Be sensitive. If a person is not open, be kind, leave a tract, and pray. Not every conversation ends in a decision, but every faithful conversation is seed sown.

# Questions and objections | Answering honestly and gently.
## Common questions | 1 Peter 3:15; Jude 1:22; John 14:6
"Be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear" (1 Peter 3:15). Some common questions:

- **"Aren't all religions the same?"** Religions differ deeply about God, sin and salvation. Jesus said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6). Only Christianity says salvation is a gift through a risen Saviour.
- **"I'm a good person."** Compare yourself with God's standard, not with others. Have you ever lied, hated, or put something before God? (Romans 3:23)
- **"I will come to Christ later."** "Now is the day of salvation" (2 Corinthians 6:2). Tomorrow is not promised.
- **"Christians are hypocrites."** Some are, and that is sad. But look at Christ, not at failing people.
- **"Why does God allow suffering?"** Admit it is hard. God entered our suffering at the cross and promises to end it (Revelation 21:4).
## How to respond well | 2 Timothy 2:24-26; Proverbs 15:1; Acts 26:28-29
How you answer matters as much as what you say.

- **Listen fully** before answering. Ask, "What makes you say that?"
- **Stay calm.** "A soft answer turneth away wrath" (Proverbs 15:1).
- **Be honest.** If you don't know, say, "I'll find out and get back to you." Then do.
- **Return to Christ.** Don't get stuck on side issues. Gently bring the conversation back to who Jesus is and what He did.
- **Don't try to win the argument.** You may win the argument and lose the person.
- **Respect their freedom.** Agrippa said, "Almost thou persuadest me" (Acts 26:28). Paul did not force him; he simply wished that he would be like him, apart from the chains.

The Spirit convicts; we only witness (John 16:8). Pray that God would give "them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth" (2 Timothy 2:25).

# Leading to Christ, and field outreach | Helping someone respond, then going out together.
## Leading someone to Christ | Acts 16:30-31; John 1:12; 1 John 5:11-13
When the jailer asked, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?" Paul answered, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved" (Acts 16:30-31).

When a person is ready:

1. **Check understanding**: "Do you understand that you have sinned and that Jesus died and rose for you?"
2. **Ask about willingness**: "Would you like to turn from sin and trust Jesus as your Saviour and Lord now?"
3. **Pray together**: let them pray in their own words, or guide them in a simple prayer of repentance and faith.
4. **Give assurance** from God's Word: John 1:12 and 1 John 5:11-13.
5. **Take the next steps**: get their phone number, invite them to church or a Bible Club, give them a Gospel of John or a follow-up booklet, and arrange to meet again within a few days.

Never pressure anyone. A decision made under pressure is not real faith.
## Going out on outreach | Luke 10:1-11; Mark 6:7; Acts 5:42
Jesus "sent them two and two before his face into every city" (Luke 10:1). Outreach works best in teams.

Before you go:

- **Pray** as a team.
- **Prepare** tracts, Gospels, decision cards and a pen.
- **Plan** the area and time, and agree on safety rules (go in pairs, stay in public places, return together).

During outreach:

- Greet people warmly; be polite to those who refuse.
- One person talks while the other prays silently.
- Record every decision and contact clearly.

After outreach:

- Meet to report and pray for those you met.
- Hand contacts to the follow-up team that day.
- Visit or call new believers within 48 hours.

The early church "ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ" daily, in the temple and "in every house" (Acts 5:42).

@assignment Practical assignment: a witnessing report
Over two weeks, share the gospel with at least three people: one friend or family member, one colleague or classmate, and one person on outreach.

Hand in a 300 to 500 word report covering: who you spoke to (first names only), how you started each conversation, which verses you used, the questions they asked and how you answered, how each conversation ended, and what follow-up you arranged. Finish with what you will do differently next time.
