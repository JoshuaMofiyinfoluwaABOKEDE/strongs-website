# Foundations of corporate prayer | Why God's people pray together.
## A house of prayer | Isaiah 56:7; Matthew 21:13; Acts 1:14
God said, "mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people" (Isaiah 56:7). Jesus quoted it when He cleansed the temple (Matthew 21:13).

The church began in a prayer meeting: "These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication" (Acts 1:14). Ten days of prayer preceded Pentecost.

Throughout Acts, corporate prayer:

- Preceded the Spirit's coming (Acts 2:1-4).
- Was a continual practice (Acts 2:42).
- Answered persecution with boldness (Acts 4:23-31).
- Opened prison doors (Acts 12:5-17).
- Sent out missionaries (Acts 13:1-3).

Jesus promised special presence when believers gather: "if two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them... For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them" (Matthew 18:19-20).
## The prayer leader's heart | Colossians 4:12; Exodus 17:11-12; Romans 8:26-27
Epaphras was "always labouring fervently for you in prayers" (Colossians 4:12). Prayer leaders must first be people of prayer.

Qualities of a prayer leader:

- **A personal prayer life**: you can't lead others where you don't go.
- **Grounded in Scripture**: prayers shaped by God's Word and will.
- **Humble**: prayer is not a stage for spiritual display (Matthew 6:5).
- **Discerning**: sensitive to the Holy Spirit (Romans 8:26-27) and to people.
- **Orderly**: "Let all things be done decently and in order" (1 Corinthians 14:40).
- **Submitted** to church leadership.
- **Encouraging**: helping others pray, especially beginners.

Like Aaron and Hur holding Moses' hands (Exodus 17:12), prayer ministry often works unseen, holding up leaders and the work.

# Leading prayer meetings | Meetings with life and focus.
## Shaping a prayer meeting | Acts 4:23-31; Psalm 100:4; Philippians 4:6
In Acts 4 the believers' prayer had a shape: they **worshipped** God as Creator ("Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven, and earth"), **quoted Scripture** (Psalm 2), **acknowledged the situation**, **asked** for boldness and signs, and God answered (Acts 4:24-31).

A prayer meeting plan (60 to 90 minutes):

1. **Worship and thanksgiving** (Psalm 100:4).
2. **Scripture**: read a passage that shapes the prayers.
3. **Confession**: personal and corporate.
4. **Focused intercession** in segments: the church, leaders, the sick and needy, the lost, missions, the nation.
5. **Response**: listening, testimonies of answers, commitment.

Variety keeps people engaged:

- Short sentence prayers by many people.
- Praying in pairs or small groups.
- Praying through a psalm together.
- Prayer walks around a campus or community.
- Using a map, pictures or lists for missions.
- Silent prayer.
## Practical leadership in the meeting | 1 Corinthians 14:26-33; Matthew 6:7; Ecclesiastes 5:2
"God is not the author of confusion, but of peace" (1 Corinthians 14:33). Jesus warned against "vain repetitions" and thinking we are heard "for their much speaking" (Matthew 6:7).

Leading well:

- **Start and end on time.**
- **Explain the focus** of each segment clearly and briefly.
- **Give specific points**: names, needs, verses.
- **Keep your own prayers short** so others pray.
- **Encourage beginners**; don't let a few dominate.
- **Guide gently** when prayers wander or become sermons or gossip.
- **Weigh prophecies and words** humbly and in line with Scripture and church order (1 Corinthians 14:29).
- **Manage volume and energy**: fervency is good, but chaos does not edify visitors (1 Corinthians 14:23).
- **Record answers** and share them: faith grows.

# Intercessory teams and prayer strategy | Organising sustained prayer.
## Building intercessory teams | Ezekiel 22:30; Isaiah 62:6-7; Proverbs 11:13
"I have set watchmen upon thy walls, O Jerusalem, which shall never hold their peace day nor night" (Isaiah 62:6). God seeks those who will "stand in the gap" (Ezekiel 22:30).

Organising intercession:

- **Recruit** faithful, mature believers; train them.
- **Prayer chains**: a structured way to pass urgent requests (e.g. WhatsApp group or call tree) with clear rules.
- **Watches or rotas**: people commit to pray at set times so prayer covers the week.
- **Request system**: a simple way for members to submit requests, marked as public or confidential.
- **Confidentiality**: "a talebearer revealeth secrets" (Proverbs 11:13). Share sensitive requests only with consent and only with those who need to know.
- **Accountability**: intercessors serve under church leadership; avoid "super-spiritual" groups that become critical or independent.
- **Care**: intercessors carry heavy burdens; pray for them and give them rest.

Vigils and fasting events: plan them well (safety, transport, food for breaking fasts, breaks, health considerations).
## Praying for the harvest and the nation | Matthew 9:37-38; 1 Timothy 2:1-4; Jeremiah 29:7
"Pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest, that he will send forth labourers into his harvest" (Matthew 9:38). "I exhort therefore, that... supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; For kings, and for all that are in authority" (1 Timothy 2:1-2). "Seek the peace of the city... and pray unto the LORD for it" (Jeremiah 29:7).

A **prayer strategy for an outreach or crusade**:

- **Months before**: pray for the area, for open hearts, for workers, for permits and resources.
- **Weeks before**: prayer walks in the community; lists of people to pray for by name.
- **During**: a prayer team praying throughout every session, separate from the platform.
- **After**: pray for new believers, follow-up workers and churches receiving them.

**Praying for the nation**: leaders at every level, security, justice, the economy, the church, unity, and revival. Pray with Scripture, not merely with news headlines.
