# Understanding young people | The generation we serve.
## The young in Scripture | 1 Timothy 4:12; Ecclesiastes 12:1; Psalm 119:9
God has often used young people: Joseph (a teenager with dreams), Samuel (a boy who heard God), David (a youth who faced Goliath), Josiah (king at eight, seeking God at sixteen, 2 Chronicles 34:3), Daniel and his friends, Mary, and Timothy.

"Let no man despise thy youth; but be thou an example of the believers" (1 Timothy 4:12). "Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth" (Ecclesiastes 12:1). "Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word" (Psalm 119:9).

Youth ministry exists to help young people **know Christ, grow in Him, and serve Him** now, not only "in the future". Young people are not the church of tomorrow; they are part of the church today.
## Their world | 1 Chronicles 12:32; John 17:15; Romans 12:2
The men of Issachar "had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do" (1 Chronicles 12:32). Youth leaders must understand young people's world.

Pressures many Nigerian young people face:

- **Academic pressure**: exams, admission, strikes, unemployment after graduation.
- **Economic hardship** and pressure to "make it" quickly, including fraud and get-rich schemes.
- **Identity** questions shaped by social media.
- **Sexual pressure**, pornography and unhealthy relationships.
- **Drugs and alcohol**, including codeine and tramadol misuse.
- **Mental health** struggles: anxiety, depression, loneliness.
- **Family problems**: conflict, absent parents, poverty.
- **Doubts** about faith, often unspoken.
- **Desire to emigrate** ("japa") and uncertainty about the future.

Listen before you speak. Ask them what they face. Jesus prayed not that His disciples would be taken out of the world, but kept from evil in it (John 17:15).

# Building the ministry | Purpose, programmes and teaching.
## Purpose and plan | Proverbs 29:18; Habakkuk 2:2; Luke 2:52
"Where there is no vision, the people perish" (Proverbs 29:18). Jesus "increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man" (Luke 2:52): intellectual, physical, spiritual and social growth.

Write a **purpose statement**: "Our youth ministry exists to help young people follow Jesus wholeheartedly, grow in His Word, care for one another, and serve Him in school, work and the world."

Build around five purposes:

- **Worship**: meeting God together.
- **Teaching**: grounding them in the Word.
- **Fellowship**: real friendships and belonging.
- **Service**: using their gifts in church and community.
- **Evangelism**: reaching their friends.

Make a **termly plan**: weekly meetings, small groups, a retreat or camp, an outreach, a service project, a social event, and parents' meetings. Keep it simple and consistent.
## Teaching that connects | Luke 24:13-32; Acts 17:22-31; Colossians 4:6
On the road to Emmaus, Jesus walked with two discouraged disciples, asked questions, listened, then "beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27).

Teaching young people:

- **Be biblical**: open the Word; don't replace it with motivational talks.
- **Be relevant**: connect Scripture to their real questions (identity, relationships, purpose, money, doubt, the future).
- **Be honest**: welcome hard questions; say "I don't know, let's find out" when needed.
- **Be interactive**: questions, discussion, small groups, testimonies, drama, music.
- **Be short and clear**: one main point.
- **Be practical**: give specific next steps.
- **Use their language and media**, without being fake.

Series ideas: "Who Am I?", "Love, Sex and Dating God's Way", "Faith and Doubt", "Purpose and Career", "Money and Integrity", "Social Media and Your Soul".

# Discipling, protecting and raising leaders | Relationships, safety and leadership.
## Mentoring and small groups | 1 Thessalonians 2:7-8; Titus 2:3-8; Proverbs 27:17
Paul shared "not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls" (1 Thessalonians 2:8). Titus 2 describes older believers teaching younger ones: older women the younger women, and Titus himself as "a pattern of good works" to young men (Titus 2:4, 7).

Young people grow most through **relationships**:

- **Small groups** of six to ten, led by trusted leaders, meeting weekly for Bible study, prayer and honest sharing.
- **Same-gender mentoring** for sensitive issues.
- **Being present**: attend their events, check in when they miss meetings, celebrate milestones.
- **Partner with parents**: keep them informed and supported.
- **Involve the wider church**: intergenerational relationships matter.

Programmes attract young people; relationships keep them.
## Safeguarding and raising leaders | Matthew 18:6; Mark 9:42; 1 Timothy 4:12
Jesus said whoever causes a little one to stumble, "it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck" (Matthew 18:6). Protecting young people is serious.

**Safeguarding basics**:

- **Screen** volunteers: references, known character, time in the church.
- **Two-adult rule**: avoid one adult alone with one young person in private; meet in visible places.
- **Appropriate boundaries** in touch, messages and social media. Communicate in groups or with parents' knowledge.
- **Parental consent** for trips, camps and photos.
- **Report concerns** of abuse to designated leaders and, where required, to the authorities. Never cover up.
- **A written policy** that every worker signs.

**Raising leaders**: give young people real roles: leading worship, teaching younger children, planning events, media, outreach, prayer. Train them, give feedback, and gradually hand over responsibility. Timothy was young and led; so can they.

@assignment Practical assignment: a youth ministry plan
Write a youth ministry plan (600 to 900 words) for a church, fellowship or school group:

1. A purpose statement and a description of the young people you serve, with three key pressures they face (ideally from conversations with them).
2. A termly calendar covering worship, teaching, fellowship, service and evangelism.
3. One complete meeting plan (welcome, activity, teaching outline, discussion questions, prayer).
4. A one-page safeguarding policy.
5. A plan to give five young people real leadership roles.
