# Know your reader | Briefs, audiences and the one action.
## Audience and the one action | Acts 17:22-23; 1 Corinthians 9:22
Paul began in Athens with what his audience already cared about: their altar "TO THE UNKNOWN GOD" (Acts 17:23). Good copy starts with the **reader**, not the writer.

Before writing, prepare a **creative brief**:

- **Who** is the reader? Age, situation, language, what they care about, what worries them.
- **What do they need or want?** (Not what we want to say.)
- **What is the one action** we want them to take? Register, attend, buy, call, give, download, share.
- **Why should they?** The main benefit.
- **Why believe us?** Proof: testimonies, numbers, credentials, guarantees.
- **Where** will they see it? WhatsApp, a poster, a website, radio, Instagram?
- **Tone**: warm, urgent, playful, professional?

One piece of copy, **one main action**. Too many asks means no action.
## Features and benefits | Matthew 11:28-30; John 10:10
Jesus didn't list features; He offered benefits: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). "I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly" (John 10:10).

- A **feature** is what something is or has.
- A **benefit** is what it does for the reader.

Examples:

- Feature: "A 4-week online course." Benefit: "Learn to study the Bible confidently in just 2 hours a week, at your own pace."
- Feature: "Our bakery uses fresh ingredients." Benefit: "Bread your family will ask for again, soft and fresh every morning."
- Feature: "Free crusade, 6pm, Stadium." Benefit: "An evening of hope, healing prayer and good news for your whole family."

Ask "So what?" after each feature until you reach a benefit the reader cares about. But don't promise benefits you can't deliver.

# Headlines and structures | Proven ways to grab attention and move readers.
## Headlines and calls to action | Proverbs 25:11; Joshua 24:15
Most people read only the **headline**. Make it count.

Headline approaches:

- **Benefit**: "Speak with Confidence in 5 Weeks"
- **Question**: "Struggling to Pray Every Day?"
- **How to**: "How to Start a Small Poultry Business with ₦50,000"
- **News**: "New: Free Evening Classes for Final-Year Students"
- **Specific numbers**: "3 Mistakes That Kill Church Flyers"

**Calls to action (CTAs)**:

- Use **verbs**: Register, Join, Call, Book, Download, Give.
- Make it **specific and easy**: "WhatsApp 0813 789 0055 to book your seat."
- Add **urgency** only if it's true: "Registration closes Friday."
- **Reduce risk**: "Free", "No experience needed", "Cancel anytime" (if true).

Joshua's "choose you this day" (Joshua 24:15) is a clear, urgent, personal call to action.
## AIDA and PAS | Nehemiah 2:17-18; Acts 2:37-38
Two classic structures:

**AIDA**:

- **Attention**: a headline or image that stops the reader.
- **Interest**: a fact, story or question that keeps them reading.
- **Desire**: benefits and proof that make them want it.
- **Action**: a clear call to act.

**PAS**:

- **Problem**: name a problem the reader feels.
- **Agitate**: show why it matters or what it costs.
- **Solution**: present your offer as the answer, then call to action.

Nehemiah used something like PAS: "Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lieth waste" (problem and agitation), "come, and let us build up the wall" (solution and action). The people answered, "Let us rise up and build" (Nehemiah 2:17-18).

At Pentecost, the crowd asked "what shall we do?" and Peter answered with a clear action: "Repent, and be baptized" (Acts 2:37-38).

# Channels and honesty | Writing for each medium, testing, and integrity.
## Writing for different channels | 1 Corinthians 14:8-9; Habakkuk 2:2
"Make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it" (Habakkuk 2:2). Each channel has different space and attention.

- **Flyers and posters**: headline, one benefit line, key details (date, time, venue), CTA. Fewer than 40 words of body text.
- **WhatsApp broadcasts**: short, friendly, one link or number. Use line breaks. Avoid long forwards.
- **Social media ads**: a strong first line, one benefit, one CTA; the image carries much of the message.
- **Websites / landing pages**: a clear headline and sub-headline, benefits in short sections, proof (testimonies), FAQs, and a repeated CTA button.
- **Email**: a compelling subject line, a personal greeting, short paragraphs, one CTA button.
- **Radio**: say the key detail (phone number, date) at least twice; write for the ear.
- **Fundraising appeals**: a real story, the specific need, what a gift will do ("₦5,000 provides Bibles for 5 new believers"), and thanks.

Write, then **cut**. Short copy is harder and better.
## Honest persuasion and testing | Proverbs 11:1; Proverbs 12:22; 2 Corinthians 8:20-21
"Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men" (2 Corinthians 8:21). "Lying lips are abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 12:22).

Honesty rules for copy:

- **No false claims**: don't promise healing, wealth, admission or results you can't guarantee.
- **No fake urgency** or fake scarcity ("Only 3 seats left!" when there are 300).
- **No fake reviews or testimonies.** Use real ones, with permission.
- **Clear prices** and terms; no hidden charges.
- **Accurate images**: don't show what isn't true.
- **Respect dignity** in fundraising: don't exploit images of poverty or suffering.
- **Follow advertising rules** (in Nigeria, the Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria sets standards for adverts).

**Testing**: write two versions of a headline or subject line (A/B test). Measure results (clicks, sign-ups, calls) and keep what works. Ask a few real readers what they understood.

@assignment Practical assignment: a campaign copy set
Choose a real event, product, course or appeal (for a church, ministry, STRONGS programme or business).

1. Write a one-page creative brief.
2. Write copy for three channels: a flyer, a WhatsApp broadcast message, and either a landing page or a fundraising email.
3. Use AIDA or PAS in at least one piece, and label the parts.
4. Write two alternative headlines or subject lines for an A/B test.
5. Add a 150-word note confirming every claim is true and explaining how you would measure results.
