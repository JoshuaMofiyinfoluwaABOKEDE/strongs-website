# Filming | Getting good pictures and sound.
## Camera basics | Habakkuk 2:2; Acts 2:6
Video can carry the gospel to people who will never sit in our meetings. At Pentecost "every man heard them speak in his own language" (Acts 2:6). Today a sermon clip can travel across the world in a day.

You can start with a **smartphone**. What matters most:

- **Steady shots**: use a tripod or rest the phone on something. Shaky video tires viewers.
- **Horizontal (landscape)** for YouTube and screens; **vertical** for WhatsApp status, Reels and TikTok. Decide before you film.
- **Framing**: use the **rule of thirds** (imagine a 3×3 grid; place eyes on the top line). Leave a little headroom; don't cut people at the joints.
- **Shot types**: wide (shows the setting), medium (waist up), close-up (face, emotion). Mix them.
- **Focus and exposure**: tap the screen on the subject's face to lock focus and brightness.
- **Clean the lens** before filming.
- **Settings**: 1080p at 30 frames per second is a good standard.
## Light and sound | Genesis 1:3-4; 1 Corinthians 14:7-9
"God said, Let there be light: and there was light" (Genesis 1:3). Light makes or breaks video.

**Lighting**:

- Face the subject toward a window or soft light; avoid strong light **behind** them (they become a silhouette).
- Avoid harsh overhead light; add a lamp or ring light in front, slightly to the side.
- Keep lighting consistent through a recording.

**Sound** matters even more than picture. People will watch a slightly blurry video with clear sound, but not a sharp video they cannot hear. "If the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself?" (1 Corinthians 14:8).

- Use an external **lavalier (clip-on) microphone** or a wireless mic if possible.
- Record in a **quiet place**; switch off fans and generators where possible, or move away from them.
- Keep the mic close to the speaker.
- **Monitor with headphones** and do a short test recording first.
- At services, take an audio feed from the **sound desk** for the best result.

# Editing | Turning footage into a clear, purposeful video.
## The editing process | Ecclesiastes 3:1; Proverbs 10:19
"In the multitude of words there wanteth not sin" (Proverbs 10:19). Editing is mostly **cutting**: keeping what serves the message and removing the rest.

Tools: CapCut, InShot or VN on phones; DaVinci Resolve (free) or Adobe Premiere Pro on computers.

Steps:

1. **Organise**: name and sort your clips in folders.
2. **Watch everything** and note the best moments.
3. **Rough cut**: put the main clips in order.
4. **Fine cut**: trim pauses, mistakes and repetition. Add B-roll (supporting footage) to cover cuts and show what is being described.
5. **Sound**: balance levels, reduce noise, add background music quietly under speech.
6. **Titles and captions**: names, Scripture references and **subtitles**. Many people watch on silent.
7. **Colour**: correct brightness and colour so clips match.
8. **Export** in the right size and format.
## Short clips and ethics | Exodus 20:15; Proverbs 12:22
Short clips (30 to 90 seconds) from a sermon or testimony often reach more people than the full video.

Making a good clip:

- **One idea** that makes sense on its own.
- **A strong first three seconds**: start with the key line, not the introduction.
- **Captions** in large, readable text.
- **Vertical format** for status and Reels.
- **A clear end**: Scripture reference, ministry name, where to watch the full message.

**Ethics**:

- **Don't take words out of context** so a preacher seems to say something they did not mean. "Lying lips are abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 12:22).
- **Music**: use royalty-free music or music you have permission to use. Copyrighted songs can get videos blocked, and using them without permission is wrong (Exodus 20:15).
- **Consent**: get permission before publishing close-ups of people, especially children, people at the altar, or people receiving prayer.

# Livestreaming | Taking services and events online live.
## Setting up a stream | Luke 14:28-30; Proverbs 24:27
"For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost?" (Luke 14:28). Plan your stream before you go live.

A simple setup:

- **Camera**: a phone on a tripod, or a camera with an HDMI output.
- **Audio**: a feed from the church sound desk into the phone or computer (via an audio interface or adapter). Do not rely on the camera's built-in mic in a loud hall.
- **Encoder**: a phone app (YouTube or Facebook Live) for simple streams, or free software like **OBS Studio** on a laptop for multiple cameras, titles and slides.
- **Internet**: a stable connection with **upload speed** of at least about 5 Mbps for 720p; more for 1080p. Test it. Have a backup (a second network's mobile data).
- **Power**: full batteries, power banks, and a plan for power cuts (UPS or inverter).
- **Platform**: YouTube, Facebook, or both via a multistreaming service.
## Running the stream | 1 Corinthians 14:40; Proverbs 27:23
On the day:

1. **Arrive early** and set up at least an hour before.
2. **Test**: picture, sound levels, internet speed, and a private test stream.
3. **Prepare graphics**: a "starting soon" slide, lower-thirds (names), Scripture slides, and an end slide.
4. **Go live 5 to 10 minutes early** with the starting slide and music.
5. **Monitor** the stream on a second device with headphones (with a delay).
6. **Moderate comments**: welcome viewers, share Scripture references, answer questions, remove spam.
7. **Protect privacy**: avoid showing the offering count, the altar ministry of people who haven't consented, or children's close-ups.
8. **End cleanly** with a closing slide and links.
9. **Review** afterwards: what worked, what failed, viewer numbers and comments.

"Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks" (Proverbs 27:23). Online viewers are people too; care for them and follow up.

# Event coverage | Covering crusades, outreaches and programmes.
## Planning coverage | Nehemiah 4:16; Acts 14:27
At a crusade or major event, a media team may handle photos, video, livestream and social media at once. Plan like Nehemiah, who divided his workers so the task was covered (Nehemiah 4:16).

A **coverage plan**:

- **Shot list**: what must be captured (arrival, worship, preaching, the appeal, counselling, testimonies, crowd, team, partners).
- **Roles**: photographer, videographer(s), livestream operator, social media person, team lead.
- **Positions**: where each person stands; safe, clear sightlines that don't block the congregation.
- **Schedule**: when each part happens.
- **Equipment list**: cameras, batteries, memory cards, chargers, power banks, rain covers.
- **Backups**: copy footage to two places the same day.

After the event, the footage supports the **report** (Acts 14:27) and future promotion.
## Coverage with dignity | Matthew 6:1-4; Matthew 7:12
Jesus warned against doing good deeds "to be seen of men" (Matthew 6:1). Coverage should glorify God, not people.

Guidelines:

- **Be discreet** during prayer and the altar call; avoid flash and moving in front of people.
- **Ask consent** before close-up photos or interviews. Use consent forms for testimonies.
- **Protect children**: no close-ups without a parent's permission; don't publish their names or schools.
- **Respect the vulnerable**: don't publish images of sick, poor or distressed people in ways that shame them.
- **Don't stage** fake responses or healings. Show what really happened.
- **Honour the team and partners** in captions and credits.
- **Dress appropriately** and behave as ministers, not tourists.

"All things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them" (Matthew 7:12).
