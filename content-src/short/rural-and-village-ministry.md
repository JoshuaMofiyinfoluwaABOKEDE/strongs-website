# Understanding rural ministry | Villages, culture and calling.
## Jesus in the villages | Matthew 9:35-36; Mark 6:6; Luke 8:1
"Jesus went about all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every sickness" (Matthew 9:35). "He went round about the villages, teaching" (Mark 6:6).

Rural communities matter to God. In many parts of Nigeria, villages:

- Hold many people with **little access** to the gospel or to sound teaching.
- Have **struggling churches** with untrained leaders or no pastor.
- Face **poverty**, poor roads, limited healthcare and schooling.
- Are shaped by **traditional religion**, festivals and strong family ties.
- Are increasingly affected by **migration** of young people to cities, and in some areas by **insecurity**.

Village ministry requires compassion, patience, humility and long-term commitment. Jesus "was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd" (Matthew 9:36).
## Culture and community | 1 Corinthians 9:19-23; Acts 17:22-28; Ruth 1:16
Paul said, "I am made all things to all men, that I might by all means save some" (1 Corinthians 9:22). In Athens he studied their culture and found a starting point (Acts 17:23).

Understanding village culture:

- **Community over individual**: decisions are often made with family and elders.
- **Respect for age and authority**: greet properly, honour elders and chiefs.
- **Oral culture**: stories, proverbs, songs and conversation matter more than print.
- **Daily rhythms** follow farming seasons, market days and festivals.
- **Hospitality** is important; accepting it builds relationship.
- **Spiritual world**: concerns about ancestors, spirits, curses, witchcraft and protection are real to many people.

Learn before you teach. Ask questions, observe and listen. Distinguish what is merely **cultural** (dress, food, greetings) from what is **contrary to the gospel** (idolatry, occult practices). Respect the first; address the second with truth and love.

# Entering and communicating | Arriving well and speaking the people's language.
## Entering a community | Luke 10:5-9; Genesis 23:3-16; Romans 13:7
Jesus told the seventy: "into whatsoever house ye enter, first say, Peace be to this house... And in the same house remain, eating and drinking such things as they give... heal the sick that are therein, and say unto them, The kingdom of God is come nigh unto you" (Luke 10:5-9).

Abraham, a stranger in Hebron, dealt respectfully and openly with the local leaders (Genesis 23).

Steps for entering:

1. **Pray** and research the community.
2. **Connect** through a local believer, church or contact.
3. **Visit the traditional ruler (Oba, Obi, Emir, chief or village head)** and elders to greet and explain your purpose. "Honour to whom honour" (Romans 13:7).
4. **Inform local churches** and work with them, not around them.
5. **Respect local rules**: dress, timing, sacred places, permits.
6. **Accept hospitality** and build relationships.
7. **Serve** visibly and consistently.
8. **Stay** or return regularly; one-off visits rarely build churches.
## Oral and local-language communication | Acts 2:6-11; Mark 4:33-34; Nehemiah 8:8
At Pentecost people heard "every man in his own language" (Acts 2:6). Jesus taught with parables "as they were able to hear it" (Mark 4:33). Ezra's helpers "gave the sense" so people understood (Nehemiah 8:8).

Effective village communication:

- **Use the heart language**: Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Tiv, Nupe, Ijaw, Kanuri and hundreds more. Use local interpreters and Bible translations where they exist.
- **Tell Bible stories** in order, from creation to Christ (chronological Bible storying). Let listeners retell them.
- **Ask questions** after stories: What happened? What does it teach about God? About us? What will we do?
- **Use songs, proverbs and drama**.
- **Use audio**: recorded Scriptures and stories in local languages, playable on phones or solar players.
- **Use local examples**: farming, harvest, rain, market, family, chieftaincy.
- **Keep it simple and repeated**.
- **Address real fears**: show Christ's power over spirits, curses and death (Colossians 2:15).

# Holistic service and lasting churches | Serving needs and building local leadership.
## Serving the whole person | Matthew 25:35-40; James 2:15-17; Acts 10:38
Jesus "went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil" (Acts 10:38). "If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food, and one of you say unto them, Depart in peace... notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit?" (James 2:15-16).

Holistic outreach in villages may include:

- **Medical outreaches** with qualified health workers: check-ups, malaria tests, eye care, referrals.
- **Clean water** projects and hygiene teaching.
- **Literacy classes** and support for schools.
- **Agricultural training** and cooperatives.
- **Skills training** for youth and women.
- **Relief** in disasters, with dignity.

Principles:

- **Partner with the community**: ask what they need; involve them.
- **Do no harm**: avoid dependency; build local capacity.
- **Keep the gospel central**: good works open doors and show love, but people need Christ.
- **Never make aid conditional** on conversion.
## Building local, lasting churches | Acts 14:21-23; Titus 1:5; 1 Thessalonians 1:8
Paul and Barnabas returned to young churches, "Confirming the souls of the disciples" and "ordained them elders in every church" (Acts 14:22-23). Paul left Titus in Crete to "ordain elders in every city" (Titus 1:5). From Thessalonica "sounded out the word of the Lord" to the surrounding regions (1 Thessalonians 1:8).

Building lasting village churches:

- **Disciple** new believers in their language with simple, reproducible methods.
- **Raise local leaders** early; train them on-site and through short courses.
- **Meet simply**: homes, schools, under trees; buildings can come later.
- **Teach giving** and stewardship so the church supports itself.
- **Encourage reproduction**: believers reaching nearby villages.
- **Connect** with a supporting church or denomination for oversight and fellowship.
- **Visit regularly** to encourage and teach.

The aim is not a church dependent on outsiders but a church that is **self-led, self-supporting and self-reproducing**.

@assignment Practical assignment: a village ministry plan
Choose a real rural community (one you know, or one your church or STRONGS could reach). Write a 600 to 900 word plan:

1. A profile of the community: location, language(s), leadership, culture, needs and existing churches.
2. Steps for entering the community respectfully.
3. A teaching plan using five Bible stories in the local language, with discussion questions.
4. One holistic project that meets a real need, developed with the community.
5. A plan for discipling believers and raising local leaders.
