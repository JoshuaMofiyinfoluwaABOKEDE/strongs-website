# The digital mission field | Going where people are.
## Every creature, everywhere | Mark 16:15; Acts 17:17; 1 Corinthians 9:22
"Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature" (Mark 16:15). Paul reasoned "in the market daily with them that met with him" (Acts 17:17): he went where people gathered.

Today people gather on WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X and other platforms, and they listen to radio and podcasts. These are today's marketplaces.

Opportunities:

- **Reach**: content can travel to thousands at little cost.
- **Access**: people search for answers privately at night, at work, in places closed to the gospel.
- **Language**: content in Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Pidgin and more.
- **Conversation**: messaging allows personal follow-up.

Challenges:

- **Noise**: many voices compete for attention.
- **Shallowness**: likes are not conversions.
- **Misinformation** and false teaching.
- **Distraction** and time-wasting for the worker too.
- **Safety**: scams, harassment and privacy risks.

"I am made all things to all men, that I might by all means save some" (1 Corinthians 9:22).
## Online integrity | Philippians 4:8; Ephesians 4:29; Galatians 1:10
Online evangelism must look like Christ.

- **Truth**: no clickbait that misleads, no fake miracles or edited "proof", no false statistics.
- **Grace**: "Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth" (Ephesians 4:29); no insults or online fights.
- **Humility**: aim to glorify Christ, not to grow your own following (Galatians 1:10).
- **Respect**: don't post people (especially at altars or children) without consent.
- **Credit**: use music, images and clips you have rights to use.
- **Discernment**: check facts before sharing; avoid forwarding rumours.

"Whatsoever things are true... honest... just... pure... lovely... of good report... think on these things" (Philippians 4:8). Post on these things too.

# Creating gospel content | Clear, engaging and true.
## The message in short form | 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; John 3:16; Acts 2:36-38
The gospel can be told briefly: "Christ died for our sins... was buried... rose again the third day" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4). John 3:16 summarises it in one sentence.

Content types:

- **Short videos** (30 to 90 seconds): a testimony, a single truth, a question answered. Hook in the first two seconds; captions on screen; end with a next step.
- **Testimonies** (with consent): real stories of changed lives.
- **Scripture graphics**: one verse, beautifully designed, with a short reflection.
- **Carousel posts**: "5 questions people ask about Jesus", one slide each.
- **Audio**: short devotionals, Bible readings and stories in local languages, shared on WhatsApp.
- **Live sessions**: Q&A, prayer, Bible study.
- **Longer teaching**: YouTube messages, podcasts and radio programmes.

Every piece should point to Jesus, and many should invite a response: "Message us if you'd like to know more" or "Pray this with me."
## Platforms and planning | Habakkuk 2:2; Proverbs 21:5
"Write the vision, and make it plain" (Habakkuk 2:2).

Platforms (each has a different audience and style):

- **WhatsApp**: personal sharing, status updates, channels and groups; excellent for one-to-one follow-up.
- **Facebook**: broad age range; groups and longer posts.
- **Instagram / TikTok / YouTube Shorts**: short video, younger audiences.
- **YouTube**: searchable teaching and testimonies.
- **X**: short text and conversations.
- **Radio**: still powerful in many communities, especially rural areas and in local languages.

Planning:

- Choose **one or two platforms** to do well rather than many poorly.
- Make a **content calendar** with themes (Who is Jesus? Questions; Testimonies; Bible stories).
- Post **consistently**.
- Use **local languages** and contexts.
- **Measure**: which posts lead to conversations, not just views?

# From content to disciples | Conversations, decisions and follow-up.
## Online conversations | John 4:7-26; Colossians 4:6; 1 Peter 3:15
Jesus' conversation with the Samaritan woman moved from an ordinary request to "I that speak unto thee am he" (John 4:26). Online, content opens the door; conversation leads to Christ.

A simple online conversation guide:

1. **Respond quickly** and warmly to messages and comments ("Thank you for writing. I'm glad you reached out.").
2. **Ask** what prompted them: "What made you message today?"
3. **Listen** and acknowledge their situation.
4. **Share** the gospel simply, with a few verses or a short audio.
5. **Invite** a response: "Would you like to receive Christ?" Pray with them by message or call.
6. **Give assurance** (John 1:12; 1 John 5:12).
7. **Connect**: get their location and phone number (with consent) for local follow-up.

Safety: same-gender responders for private chats where possible; no requests for money; never share personal financial or private details; report and block abuse; escalate crises (such as someone in danger) to appropriate help.
## Follow-up and discipleship | Acts 2:41-42; Hebrews 10:24-25; 2 Timothy 2:2
Online decisions need offline discipleship. The first converts "continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship" (Acts 2:42), and believers should not forsake "the assembling of ourselves together" (Hebrews 10:25).

A follow-up pathway:

- **Day 1 to 7**: daily short messages or audio on assurance, prayer and reading John.
- **Week 2 to 6**: weekly online Bible study or a WhatsApp discipleship group.
- **Local connection**: introduce them to a Bible-believing church or STRONGS Bible Club near them, and ask a local believer to meet them.
- **Track**: a simple, private record of contacts, conversations and connections (protect personal data).
- **Train** new believers to share content and their testimony with friends.

Digital evangelism succeeds not when a video goes viral, but when a person who met Christ online is growing in a local church and reaching others.
