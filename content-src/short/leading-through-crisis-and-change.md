# Facing the storm | How leaders respond in crisis.
## Turning to God first | 2 Chronicles 20:1-22; Nehemiah 4:7-14; Psalm 46:1-3
When a great army came against Judah, Jehoshaphat "feared, and set himself to seek the LORD, and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah" (2 Chronicles 20:3). He prayed honestly: "we have no might against this great company... neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee" (verse 12). God answered, and Jehoshaphat led the people forward in faith.

When enemies plotted against the wall, Nehemiah responded: "we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night" (Nehemiah 4:9). **Prayer and practical action together.**

In a crisis:

- **Don't panic**; "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" (Psalm 46:1).
- **Pray** and call others to pray.
- **Get the facts** quickly and accurately.
- **Gather wise counsel**.
- **Act**: protect people, make decisions, assign responsibilities.
- **Keep leading**: people look to leaders for steadiness.
## Stages of crisis response | Acts 27:13-44; Proverbs 22:3
In Paul's shipwreck (Acts 27), he warned before the storm, encouraged in the storm ("be of good cheer", verse 22), gave practical instructions (eat, stay with the ship), and everyone reached land safely (verse 44).

Stages:

1. **Prepare** (before): identify risks (fire, accidents, insecurity, health emergencies, scandals, financial shocks), make plans, train teams. "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself" (Proverbs 22:3).
2. **Respond** (immediate): safety first; contact emergency services; secure people and facts; designate one spokesperson.
3. **Communicate**: inform those affected early, honestly and regularly.
4. **Care**: pastoral care for victims, families and team.
5. **Recover**: restore normal life, repair damage, follow up.
6. **Learn**: review what happened and improve.

# Communication and care | Leading people through difficulty.
## Honest communication | Ephesians 4:25; Proverbs 12:22; Acts 15:22-31
"Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour" (Ephesians 4:25). After the Jerusalem council, the leaders sent a clear letter carried by trusted men who would "tell you the same things by mouth" (Acts 15:27). Clear, consistent communication brought "consolation" (verse 31).

Crisis communication:

- **Be early**: silence breeds rumours.
- **Be honest**: say what you know, what you don't know, and what you're doing.
- **Be clear and calm**.
- **Be consistent**: one spokesperson, one message.
- **Be compassionate**: acknowledge pain and fear.
- **Protect privacy** of victims and those involved.
- **Update regularly**.
- **Never cover up** wrongdoing, especially abuse or financial misconduct. Cover-ups multiply damage and dishonour God.

Example opening: "Dear church family, this afternoon there was a fire in the children's wing. Everyone is safe. Here is what we know and what we are doing..."
## Caring for people | 2 Corinthians 1:3-4; Romans 12:15; Galatians 6:2
"Blessed be God... the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble" (2 Corinthians 1:3-4).

During crises, people experience fear, grief, anger and confusion. Leaders should:

- **Be present**: visit, call, attend funerals and hospitals.
- **Listen** before advising.
- **Weep with those who weep** (Romans 12:15); avoid quick spiritual clichés.
- **Meet practical needs**: food, transport, medical bills, shelter.
- **Organise care teams** so no one is forgotten.
- **Refer** to professionals for trauma, medical and legal needs.
- **Keep teaching hope** from Scripture, patiently.
- **Care for the team**: helpers also grow weary.

# Leading change and sustaining yourself | Change wisely, endure faithfully.
## Leading necessary change | Nehemiah 2:11-18; Acts 15:1-29; Ecclesiastes 3:1
Not every crisis is sudden. Sometimes leaders must lead change: new structures, new outreach methods, new locations, leadership transitions.

Nehemiah's change process:

1. **Prayed** and waited (Nehemiah 1).
2. **Assessed** the situation quietly (2:12-16).
3. **Shared the vision** and the need (2:17).
4. **Told his story** of God's hand (2:18).
5. **Gained buy-in**: "Let us rise up and build" (2:18).
6. **Organised** the people (chapter 3).
7. **Handled opposition** (chapters 4 to 6).
8. **Completed** and celebrated (chapter 12).

In Acts 15, the church changed its approach to Gentile believers through prayerful discussion, Scripture, testimony and a shared decision.

Principles: explain **why**; involve people; move at the right pace; keep the unchangeable (the gospel) unchanged while changing methods; communicate repeatedly.
## Sustaining your soul | 1 Kings 19:1-18; 2 Corinthians 12:9-10; Isaiah 40:29-31
After his victory on Carmel, Elijah ran from Jezebel, sat under a juniper tree and asked to die (1 Kings 19:4). God's care was practical: sleep, food, then a gentle word, a new assignment, and a reminder that he was not alone: seven thousand had not bowed to Baal (verses 5-18).

Leaders under pressure need:

- **Rest and food**: physical care matters.
- **Time with God**: listening, not only speaking.
- **Honesty** about weakness: "my strength is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9).
- **Companions**: you are not alone.
- **Perspective**: God is still at work.
- **Help**: counsellors, doctors or mentors when needed.

"They that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength" (Isaiah 40:31).
