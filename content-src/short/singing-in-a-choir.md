# Voice parts | Soprano, alto, tenor and bass.
## The four parts | 1 Chronicles 25:1-7; Nehemiah 12:31
David organised singers and musicians "for the service of the house of God" (1 Chronicles 25:6), each with an assigned role. Nehemiah appointed "two great companies of them that gave thanks" (Nehemiah 12:31). Choirs are a picture of the body of Christ: many members, one sound.

The four main parts (often called **SATB**):

- **Soprano**: the highest women's (and children's) part, often the melody.
- **Alto**: the lower women's part, usually harmony below the soprano.
- **Tenor**: the higher men's part, often written an octave lower than it looks in the treble clef.
- **Bass**: the lowest men's part, the foundation of the harmony.

Larger choirs may divide these (first and second soprano, baritone between tenor and bass). Every part matters. A choir with a weak bass sounds thin; a choir without altos sounds empty.
## Finding your part | Romans 12:4-6; 1 Corinthians 12:18
"God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him" (1 Corinthians 12:18). In a choir, your part should fit the voice God gave you, not your wishes or the part your friend sings.

How the choir director decides:

- **Range**: the highest and lowest notes you sing comfortably.
- **Tessitura**: the area where your voice sits most comfortably for long periods.
- **Tone colour**: lighter or darker, brighter or warmer.
- **Ear and reading ability**: some parts need stronger independence.

A short audition usually involves singing a scale, a familiar hymn, and echoing short phrases.

Your part may change as your voice develops, especially for teenagers whose voices are changing. Accept the director's guidance humbly, and give your best wherever you are placed (Romans 12:6).

# Learning a part | Practising and holding your line.
## Learning notes and words | Psalm 33:3; 2 Timothy 2:15
"Play skilfully" (Psalm 33:3). Learning your part well is respect for God, the director and fellow singers.

Ways to learn a part:

- **From the score**: follow your line, noting the notes, rhythm and words.
- **With sol-fa**: many Nigerian choirs teach parts in tonic sol-fa (*d r m f s l t d'*). Sing the sol-fa first, then the words.
- **With recordings**: part-learning recordings (your part louder) are very helpful. Make them on your phone in sectional rehearsals.
- **Repetition in small pieces**: learn four bars at a time, then join them.
- **Mark your music**: breaths (✓), dynamics, tricky intervals.

Practise at home between rehearsals. Rehearsal time is for putting parts together, not learning notes from zero.
## Holding your part | Philippians 1:27; Ephesians 4:16
Holding your part while others sing different notes is the key choir skill. Paul hoped the church would stand "fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together" (Philippians 1:27).

Tips:

- **Know it well alone first.** Confidence comes from preparation.
- **Stand near strong singers** of your section.
- **Listen to your section** more than the others, especially at first.
- **Hear the other parts** as you grow: your part makes sense within the chord.
- **Cup one ear lightly** with your hand in rehearsal to hear yourself better.
- **Don't drift to the melody.** Altos and tenors often slip into the soprano line.

Practise with one other person singing a different part, then in a quartet. Each member "according to the effectual working in the measure of every part" builds the whole (Ephesians 4:16).

# Blend and balance | Many voices, one sound.
## Blend | 2 Chronicles 5:13; Romans 15:6
"The trumpeters and singers were as one, to make one sound" (2 Chronicles 5:13), and then "the house was filled with a cloud". Paul prays that we may "with one mind and one mouth glorify God" (Romans 15:6).

**Blend** is when voices merge into one sound with no single voice sticking out.

How to blend:

- **Listen** more than you sing.
- **Match vowels**: everyone shapes "ah", "ee", "oh" the same way.
- **Match volume** to your section.
- **Match tone**: avoid heavy vibrato or an unusual sound that stands out.
- **Start and end together**: watch the director for entries and cut-offs.
- **Consonants together**, especially final ones.

Blend is a spiritual exercise as well as a musical one: it asks each singer to serve the whole rather than be heard.
## Balance and dynamics | Psalm 46:10; Psalm 150:5
**Balance** is the right volume relationship between parts. Usually the melody should be clear, the bass solid, and inner parts (alto, tenor) present but not overpowering.

**Dynamics** are changes in volume:

- *pp* (very soft), *p* (soft), *mp* (moderately soft)
- *mf* (moderately loud), *f* (loud), *ff* (very loud)
- *crescendo* (getting louder), *diminuendo* (getting softer)

Scripture has both: "Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10) and "praise him upon the high sounding cymbals" (Psalm 150:5). A choir that is loud all the time loses power; contrast makes the loud moments meaningful and the soft moments tender.

Watch the director's hands: larger gestures usually mean louder, smaller gestures softer.

# Rehearsal and ministry | Faithful service on and off the platform.
## Rehearsal discipline | 1 Corinthians 14:40; 1 Corinthians 4:2
"Let all things be done decently and in order" (1 Corinthians 14:40). "It is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:2).

Habits of a faithful chorister:

- **Arrive early**, ready to warm up when rehearsal starts.
- **Bring your music**, a pencil and water.
- **Phones away** unless recording a part with permission.
- **Watch and listen** to the director; avoid side conversations.
- **Mark corrections** immediately.
- **Practise at home** what was taught.
- **Communicate absences** in advance.
- **Respect section leaders** and fellow singers.

Rehearsal is where most of the work is done. A great performance is the fruit of many faithful, ordinary rehearsals.
## Ministry, not performance | Colossians 3:16-17; 2 Chronicles 20:21-22
In 2 Chronicles 20, Jehoshaphat "appointed singers unto the LORD... as they went out before the army", and "when they began to sing and to praise, the LORD set ambushments" against the enemies (verses 21-22). Choir singing is spiritual ministry.

So:

- **Prepare your heart** as well as your voice: pray before ministering.
- **Understand and believe** what you sing (Colossians 3:16).
- **Lead the congregation**: your face and posture help people worship.
- **Dress modestly** and as the choir agrees.
- **Live what you sing** during the week. Character matters more than talent.
- **Avoid rivalry** over solos and positions. Rejoice when others are used.
- **Give God the glory** when people praise the choir.

"Whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks" (Colossians 3:17).

@assignment Practical assignment: learn and record a part
Choose a four-part hymn or anthem your choir sings (or one suggested by your director). Learn your part thoroughly, then:

1. Record yourself singing your part alone (sol-fa or words).
2. Record yourself singing your part with a recording or with at least one other part sung by someone else.

Hand in a 250 to 400 word reflection: which part you sing and why, how you learned it, where you struggled to hold your part, and what you will practise next. Include your director's or section leader's comments if possible.
