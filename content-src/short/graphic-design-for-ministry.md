# Design basics | The principles behind every good design.
## Design that serves | Exodus 31:1-6; Habakkuk 2:2
God filled Bezaleel "with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship, to devise cunning works" (Exodus 31:3-4). Design and craftsmanship are gifts God uses.

Ministry design **serves a message**. A flyer, social media graphic or banner should help people understand quickly: What is it? When? Where? Why should I come? "Write the vision, and make it plain" (Habakkuk 2:2).

Before you design, answer:

- **Purpose**: inform, invite, inspire?
- **Audience**: students, families, professionals, children?
- **Main message** in one sentence.
- **Essential information**: title, date, time, venue, contact.
- **Where it will be seen**: WhatsApp, Instagram, a printed poster, a banner.

Good design is not decoration added at the end. It is clear communication from the start.
## Four principles | 1 Corinthians 14:33; 1 Corinthians 14:40
"God is not the author of confusion" (1 Corinthians 14:33). Four principles bring order to design (often remembered as **CRAP**, or more politely **PARC**):

- **Contrast**: make important things different (bigger, bolder, a different colour) so the eye knows where to look. Strong contrast between text and background is essential for reading.
- **Repetition**: repeat colours, fonts and shapes so the design feels unified.
- **Alignment**: line elements up with each other. Nothing should be placed randomly.
- **Proximity**: group related information together (date, time and venue in one block).

Also:

- **Hierarchy**: one main thing first (usually the title), then supporting details.
- **White space**: empty space is not wasted; it lets the design breathe.
- **Less is more**: cut anything that doesn't help the message.

# Typography and colour | Choosing fonts and colours with purpose.
## Typography | Proverbs 25:11; 1 Corinthians 14:9
Typography is how text looks. It can make a design clear or confusing.

Guidelines:

- **Use one or two fonts**, rarely three. For example, a bold heading font and a clean body font.
- **Serif fonts** (with small feet, like Georgia) feel traditional; **sans-serif** fonts (like Montserrat, Poppins) feel modern and clean, and read well on screens.
- **Avoid novelty fonts** for important information; they are hard to read.
- **Size for distance**: a poster seen from across a room needs large text. On phones, small text disappears.
- **Limit text**: say less, more clearly.
- **Don't stretch or squash** letters.
- **Watch line spacing** and letter spacing for readability.
- **Check spelling**, especially names, dates and Bible references.

"Words easy to be understood" (1 Corinthians 14:9) applies to how words look, too.
## Colour | Genesis 9:13-16; Exodus 28:5-8
God put His bow in the cloud as a sign (Genesis 9:13). The priests' garments used "gold, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen" (Exodus 28:5). Colour communicates.

Colour basics:

- **Limit your palette** to two or three main colours plus neutrals (white, black, grey).
- **Use contrast**: dark text on light backgrounds, or light text on dark. Avoid red on green or yellow on white.
- **Colour has associations**: blue (trust, calm), gold/yellow (joy, light), red (energy, urgency), green (growth), purple (royalty). These vary by culture, so think of your audience.
- **Use brand colours** consistently (Module 5). STRONGS uses navy, royal blue and gold.
- **Be careful with photos**: text over busy images is hard to read; add a dark overlay or a solid shape behind text.
- **Check accessibility**: about 1 in 12 men has some colour vision deficiency, so don't rely on colour alone to carry meaning.

# Canva and Photoshop | Tools for ministry design.
## Designing in Canva | Proverbs 22:29; Ecclesiastes 9:10
"Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might" (Ecclesiastes 9:10). Canva is a free, easy design tool on phone and computer, ideal for ministry teams.

Getting started:

1. **Choose the right size**: Instagram post (1080×1080 px), story/status (1080×1920), A4 or A3 for print, banner sizes for print shops.
2. **Start from a template** if you're new, but change it to match your message and brand. Don't use a template unchanged.
3. **Set your brand**: upload logos, choose brand colours and fonts (Canva's Brand Kit, if available).
4. **Use layers and alignment**: position, group, and align elements with guides.
5. **Use quality images**: Canva's library or your own photos, with permission from people pictured.
6. **Download correctly**: PNG for social media, PDF Print for printing.

Canva makes it easy to create quickly. Your design principles make it good.
## Photoshop basics and images | Exodus 20:15; Philippians 4:8
**Adobe Photoshop** is professional software for editing photos and creating detailed designs. Key ideas you should understand, whatever tool you use:

- **Layers**: separate elements stacked on top of each other, so you can edit one without affecting others.
- **Resolution**: for print, images should be about 300 pixels per inch at the printed size; for screens, 72 to 150 is enough. Low-resolution images look blurry when printed large.
- **Cropping and straightening** photos.
- **Adjusting** brightness, contrast and colour.
- **Masks** to hide parts of an image without deleting them.
- **File types**: PSD (editable Photoshop file), JPG (photos), PNG (graphics with transparency), PDF (print).

**Image ethics**:

- Don't use images found online without permission or a licence; use free libraries such as Unsplash or Pexels, or your own photos. "Thou shalt not steal" (Exodus 20:15).
- Don't edit photos in ways that deceive.
- Choose images that are "lovely" and "of good report" (Philippians 4:8).

# The event flyer | Designing a flyer step by step.
## Planning the flyer | Nehemiah 8:1-3; Proverbs 21:5
"The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness" (Proverbs 21:5). Plan before you design.

What every event flyer needs:

- **Event title** (biggest, clearest)
- **Theme or tagline** and Scripture (optional)
- **Date and time**
- **Venue** (with a landmark if needed)
- **Speakers or ministers** (names and titles spelled correctly; photos with permission)
- **Organiser and logo**
- **Contact** (phone, WhatsApp, social handle)
- **Call to action**: "Come with a friend", "Register at...", "Free entry"

Rank the information by importance. The title and date should be visible in two seconds on a phone screen.

Gather all information **in writing** from the organiser before designing. Many flyer mistakes come from guessing.
## Designing and checking | Luke 14:28-30; Proverbs 27:17
Design steps:

1. **Choose the format** (square for WhatsApp/Instagram, A4 or A3 for print).
2. **Set the background**: a solid colour, gradient or photo with overlay.
3. **Place the title** large, with strong contrast.
4. **Group details** (date, time, venue) in one aligned block.
5. **Add speakers' photos** cleanly cropped, the same size and style.
6. **Add logos and contact** at the bottom.
7. **Check hierarchy**: squint at it. What do you see first? It should be the title.

Before publishing, **proofread** every word, and get the organiser to approve it. Check:

- [ ] Date and day match (is the 14th really a Saturday?)
- [ ] Time, venue, phone numbers correct
- [ ] Names and titles correct
- [ ] Readable on a small phone screen
- [ ] Logo clear

Make versions for different formats (WhatsApp status, square post, print) that share the same look.

# Brand and consistency | Keeping a ministry's look recognisable.
## What a brand is | Proverbs 22:1; Matthew 5:14-16
"A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches" (Proverbs 22:1). A ministry's **brand** is how people recognise and remember it: its name, logo, colours, fonts, tone and, above all, its character.

Visual brand elements:

- **Logo**: used correctly, never stretched, recoloured or crowded.
- **Colour palette**: primary and secondary colours with exact codes (HEX for screens, CMYK for print).
- **Fonts**: chosen headings and body fonts.
- **Photography style**: real people, warm, dignified.
- **Graphic elements**: shapes, patterns or icons used regularly.
- **Tone of voice**: how the ministry speaks (warm, clear, hopeful).

When every flyer looks different, people don't recognise the ministry. When designs are consistent, each one builds trust.

A city set on a hill cannot be hidden (Matthew 5:14). Consistency helps a ministry's light be seen.
## Brand guidelines and templates | 1 Corinthians 14:40; 2 Timothy 2:2
Write a simple **brand guide** (one to three pages):

- Logo versions (full colour, white, small) and minimum size and clear space.
- Colour codes.
- Fonts and sizes for headings and body.
- Do's and don'ts with examples.
- Templates for common needs.

Create **templates** for:

- Weekly programme graphics
- Event flyers (square and status)
- Quote or Scripture graphics
- Announcement banners
- Certificates and letterheads

Templates help several designers produce consistent work quickly. Train new team members with them, as Paul passed his teaching to "faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2).

Store logos, fonts and templates in a shared folder so everyone uses the correct, latest versions.
