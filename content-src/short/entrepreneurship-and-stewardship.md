# Ideas and customers | Starting with problems worth solving.
## Seeing needs | Proverbs 31:10-31; Acts 18:1-3
The woman of Proverbs 31 "considereth a field, and buyeth it: with the fruit of her hands she planteth a vineyard" and "perceiveth that her merchandise is good" (verses 16, 18). She saw opportunities and acted. Paul, Aquila and Priscilla supported themselves as tentmakers (Acts 18:3).

Good businesses start with **problems**, not products. Ask:

- What do people around me struggle with, complain about, or waste time on?
- What do they already pay for, but get poorly?
- What skills do I have that others need?

Examples:

- Students need affordable printing and typing near campus.
- Busy parents need reliable school lunches.
- Small shops need simple social media content.
- Churches need sound, design or video services.

Write down ten problems you notice this week. The best ideas usually combine a real need, your skills, and something people will pay for.
## Testing the idea | Proverbs 15:22; Proverbs 14:15; Proverbs 18:13
"The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going" (Proverbs 14:15). Before investing much, **test** your idea.

How to test cheaply:

- **Talk to potential customers** (at least ten). Ask about the problem, not your idea first: "How do you currently get X? What frustrates you?"
- **Look at competitors**: who already serves this need? What could you do better (price, quality, location, service)?
- **Make a simple offer**: a few samples, a small batch, a pre-order, or a service for a few customers.
- **Measure**: did people buy? Did they come back? What did they say?
- **Adjust** based on feedback.

"He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him" (Proverbs 18:13). Listen to customers before you build.

If people won't pay even a small amount, rethink the idea before investing more.

# Starting small | Launching a lean business and building good habits.
## A lean plan | Luke 14:28-30; Proverbs 21:5; Proverbs 24:3-4
"Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established: And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled" (Proverbs 24:3-4).

A **lean plan** on one page:

- **Problem** you solve and **customer** you serve.
- **Solution**: your product or service.
- **Why you**: what makes you different.
- **Channels**: how customers find and buy from you.
- **Revenue**: what you charge and how often people buy.
- **Costs**: start-up and running costs.
- **Key numbers**: sales needed per month to break even.
- **Next steps** for the next 30 days.

**Break-even**: the point where income covers all costs. If your monthly costs are ₦50,000 and you make ₦1,000 profit on each sale, you need 50 sales a month to break even.

Keep start-up costs low. Use what you have. Moses' rod (Exodus 4:2) and the widow's oil (2 Kings 4:2) remind us God often starts with what is in our hands.
## Registration, habits and growth | Romans 13:1-7; Proverbs 6:6-11; Matthew 25:14-30
**Formalise** as you grow: register your business name with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), open a business bank account, and understand any taxes or permits that apply. "Render therefore to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due" (Romans 13:7).

Good habits:

- **Separate** business and personal money.
- **Record** every sale and expense.
- **Show up**: consistency builds trust (Proverbs 6:6-11).
- **Keep learning**: courses, mentors, books, other entrepreneurs.
- **Serve customers well**; ask for feedback and referrals.
- **Use simple digital tools**: WhatsApp Business, a spreadsheet, a payment app.

In the parable of the talents, the faithful servants "traded" and multiplied what they were given, and heard, "Well done, thou good and faithful servant" (Matthew 25:21). God values faithful, productive work.

# Integrity in business | Doing business God's way.
## Honest weights and fair dealing | Proverbs 11:1; Leviticus 19:13; Proverbs 16:8
"A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight" (Proverbs 11:1). "The wages of him that is hired shall not abide with thee all night until the morning" (Leviticus 19:13). "Better is a little with righteousness than great revenues without right" (Proverbs 16:8).

Integrity in business means:

- **Honest products**: no fake, expired or substandard goods; describe products truthfully.
- **Honest prices**: no hidden charges or cheating the uninformed.
- **Honest measures**: accurate scales and quantities.
- **Paying workers fairly and on time**.
- **Keeping promises**: deadlines, refunds, warranties.
- **Paying debts and taxes**.
- **Refusing bribes** and not offering them.

In a culture where shortcuts are common, honesty can cost something in the short term. But "the integrity of the upright shall guide them" (Proverbs 11:3). An honest name becomes your most valuable asset.
## Treating people well | Colossians 4:1; James 5:4; Matthew 7:12
"Masters, give unto your servants that which is just and equal; knowing that ye also have a Master in heaven" (Colossians 4:1). James warned the rich whose unpaid workers' wages "crieth" to God (James 5:4).

Treating people well:

- **Employees**: fair pay, safe conditions, respect, training, clear expectations.
- **Customers**: courtesy, listening, solving problems, honouring complaints.
- **Suppliers**: paying on time, keeping agreements.
- **Competitors**: competing fairly, never spreading lies about them.
- **Community**: not polluting, contributing to the common good.

Apply the golden rule: "all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them" (Matthew 7:12).

Business is a way to love your neighbour: by meeting needs, creating jobs, and doing excellent work.

# Stewardship | Money, success and generosity.
## Managing money faithfully | Luke 16:10-11; Proverbs 21:20; Proverbs 22:7
"He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much... If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?" (Luke 16:10-11).

Money principles for entrepreneurs:

- **Budget**: plan income and spending each month.
- **Reinvest**: put part of profit back into the business.
- **Save**: "There is treasure to be desired and oil in the dwelling of the wise; but a foolish man spendeth it up" (Proverbs 21:20). Build an emergency fund.
- **Be careful with debt**: "the borrower is servant to the lender" (Proverbs 22:7). Borrow only for things that produce income, with a clear plan to repay.
- **Avoid get-rich-quick schemes** and Ponzi-style "investments". If returns sound too good to be true, they usually are.
- **Pay yourself** a fixed amount; don't drain the business.
## Generosity and contentment | Deuteronomy 8:17-18; 1 Timothy 6:6-10; 2 Corinthians 9:6-8
"Thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth" (Deuteronomy 8:18). Success is God's gift, not ours alone.

Paul warned that "the love of money is the root of all evil" and that "godliness with contentment is great gain" (1 Timothy 6:6, 10). Money is a good servant and a terrible master.

A steward:

- **Gives** generously and cheerfully to God's work and to the poor (2 Corinthians 9:7).
- **Is content**, not driven by comparison or greed.
- **Keeps priorities**: God, family and church are not sacrificed for profit.
- **Rests**: keeps a rhythm of rest and worship.
- **Uses business for the kingdom**: creating jobs, supporting ministry, training others, and showing Christ's character in the marketplace.

"He which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully" (2 Corinthians 9:6). The goal is not only a successful business but a faithful life.
