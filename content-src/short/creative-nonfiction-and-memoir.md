# True stories, well told | What creative nonfiction is.
## Memoir, essay and testimony | Deuteronomy 8:2; Psalm 78:1-7; Galatians 1:11-24
"Thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness" (Deuteronomy 8:2). Remembering is a spiritual practice. Psalm 78 retells Israel's history "that the generation to come might know them" (verse 6). Paul writes his own story in Galatians 1:11-24: his zeal, his conversion, his years in Arabia, his visits to Jerusalem.

Forms of creative nonfiction:

- **Memoir**: a true story from part of a life, focused on a theme (a season of illness, growing up in a village, a call to ministry). Not your whole life.
- **Autobiography**: a whole-life account, usually chronological.
- **Personal essay**: a shorter piece that explores an idea through experience ("What my grandmother's farm taught me about waiting").
- **Testimony**: a story of God's work in a life, usually shorter and more direct.
- **Literary journalism**: true stories about others, reported with care.

All share one rule: **it must be true**.
## Finding your stories | Psalm 77:11-12; Mark 5:19
"I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old. I will meditate also of all thy work" (Psalm 77:11-12).

Exercises for finding stories:

- **Memory map**: draw the house, street or village you grew up in. Mark places where things happened. Each mark is a story.
- **Turning points**: list ten moments that changed you (a loss, a move, a conversation, a failure, a conversion).
- **Objects**: a photograph, a Bible, a letter, a piece of cloth. What stories do they hold?
- **People**: who shaped you, for good or ill?
- **Questions**: what do you still not understand about your past?

The best stories often carry a **tension**: something you struggled with, wondered about, or had to learn. A story where everything was easy rarely moves readers.

Jesus told the healed man to "tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee" (Mark 5:19). Your ordinary story may be extraordinary to someone else.

# The craft | Scene, detail, voice and reflection.
## Scene and detail | 2 Samuel 18:24-33; John 20:1-8
The Bible is full of vivid scenes. David waiting at the gate for news of Absalom, the watchman calling out runners, and his cry, "O my son Absalom... would God I had died for thee" (2 Samuel 18:33). John running to the tomb, outrunning Peter, stooping to see "the linen clothes lying", and the napkin "wrapped together in a place by itself" (John 20:4-7). The details make us believe.

Scene-writing tools:

- **Place and time**: where exactly? What season, what hour?
- **Senses**: what did you see, hear, smell, taste, touch? (The smell of kerosene, the sound of rain on zinc.)
- **Action**: what happened, moment by moment?
- **Dialogue**: what was said? Use memory honestly; if you aren't sure of exact words, signal it ("My father said something like...").
- **Specific details**: not "a car" but "my uncle's dented blue Peugeot 504".

Alternate **scene** (showing a moment) with **summary** (covering time quickly).
## Voice and reflection | Ecclesiastes 1:12-14; Psalm 73:16-17
Memoir has two narrators: the **younger you** who lived it, and the **present you** who understands it now. Ecclesiastes is full of reflection: "I gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom" (1:13). In Psalm 73 the writer describes envying the wicked until "I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end" (73:17). The story moves from experience to insight.

**Reflection** is where meaning happens:

- What did I believe then? What do I see now?
- What did this teach me about God, myself or others?
- What questions remain?

Balance: too much reflection and the story disappears; too little and it's just events.

**Voice** is the sound of you on the page: your vocabulary, humour, rhythm, honesty. Write the way you think when you are being most honest. Don't try to sound impressive.

Avoid **preaching** the lesson. Trust the reader. A brief, honest reflection is stronger than a long moral.

# Ethics and shaping | Writing about real people, and finishing the piece.
## Writing about real people | Exodus 20:16; Ephesians 4:15; Proverbs 10:12
Memoir always involves others: parents, siblings, friends, pastors, enemies. "Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour" (Exodus 20:16). "Speaking the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15). "Love covereth all sins" (Proverbs 10:12).

Guidelines:

- **Tell the truth**, but you don't have to tell everything. Ask: is this detail necessary for the story?
- **Be fair**: show people's complexity, including their good sides. Avoid writing to settle scores.
- **Write about yourself most harshly**: your own faults belong in the story.
- **Protect the vulnerable**: children, victims, people who could be harmed.
- **Change names and identifying details** where needed, and say so in a note.
- **Consider showing** sensitive passages to the people involved before publishing.
- **Don't invent** composite characters, events or dialogue and present them as fact. If you compress time, say so.

Before publishing, pray and ask: does this honour God and love my neighbour?
## Shaping and publishing | Habakkuk 2:2; Psalm 102:18
"This shall be written for the generation to come" (Psalm 102:18).

Shaping a piece:

- **Find the theme**: what is this story really about? (Not "my father's farm" but "learning to trust slow growth".)
- **Choose a structure**: chronological, braided (two timelines woven together), or circular (ending where it began, changed).
- **Start in a scene**, not with background.
- **Cut** what doesn't serve the theme, even if it's true and interesting.
- **End with resonance**: a final image or quiet insight, not a sermon.

Publishing options:

- Magazines and literary journals that publish personal essays (read their guidelines).
- Church and ministry magazines; anthologies; writing competitions.
- Your blog or a newsletter.
- A full memoir manuscript, if your story warrants a book (see Book Writing and Self-Publishing).

Keep a **journal**. Today's notes are tomorrow's memoir.
