# Word-centred growth | The power of systematic Bible study.
## From a Bible study to a movement | Acts 17:11; Acts 19:8-10; 2 Timothy 3:16-17
William Kumuyi began holding a weekly Bible study in Lagos in 1973 with a handful of university students. The focus was simple: study the Bible carefully and obey it, especially its call to repentance and holiness. As people were converted and grew, the study became the Deeper Christian Life Ministry and then the Deeper Life Bible Church, with congregations across Nigeria and many other nations.

That history illustrates biblical principles:

- **The Word builds believers**: Scripture is "profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness" (2 Timothy 3:16).
- **Searching the Scriptures** produces stable faith (Acts 17:11).
- **Regular teaching multiplies**: Paul's daily teaching in Ephesus reached all Asia (Acts 19:10).

Systematic Bible study (working through books and doctrines in order, with questions and memory verses) gives believers roots that sermons alone often cannot.
## Leading a systematic Bible study | Nehemiah 8:8; Luke 24:27; James 1:22
Ezra's helpers "read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand" (Nehemiah 8:8). Jesus explained "in all the scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27).

A study outline (like the weekly outlines many churches use) usually includes:

- **Topic and key text**.
- **Memory verse**.
- **Introduction**: why this matters.
- **Main points**, each with Bible references to look up.
- **Questions** for discussion.
- **Application**: what we will do.
- **Prayer points**.

Leading it:

- **Prepare** by studying the passage and the outline early.
- **Let members read** the references aloud.
- **Ask questions**; let people discover.
- **Keep to the text**; avoid side debates.
- **Apply** specifically: "be ye doers of the word" (James 1:22).
- **Review** memory verses each week.

# Home caring fellowships | Care and community in neighbourhoods.
## Caring in homes | Acts 2:44-47; Acts 20:20; Romans 12:10-15
Paul taught "publickly, and from house to house" (Acts 20:20). The early church met "from house to house... with gladness and singleness of heart" (Acts 2:46).

Home fellowships meet weekly in members' homes, grouped by neighbourhood. Their focus is **care**:

- **Knowing** each member and family by name.
- **Praying** for one another's needs.
- **Visiting** the sick, bereaved and absent.
- **Practical help**: food, rent support, job leads, school fees, within wisdom and the church's welfare structures.
- **Encouraging** holiness and growth.
- **Celebrating** together.

"Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love... Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep" (Romans 12:10, 15).

A fellowship leader should know the state of each member, like a shepherd who counts his sheep.
## Reaching the neighbourhood | Acts 16:31-34; Matthew 5:14-16; Luke 14:23
"Ye are the light of the world... Let your light so shine before men" (Matthew 5:14, 16). A home fellowship is a light on a street.

Neighbourhood evangelism through home fellowships:

- **Pray** for every house on your street by name.
- **Invite** neighbours to an open fellowship evening with simple refreshments.
- **Hold occasional evangelistic meetings** in the home with a clear gospel message.
- **Serve** visibly: help in emergencies, clean-up days, care for the elderly.
- **Follow up** visitors within a week.
- **Bring whole households**: the jailer "believed in God with all his house" (Acts 16:34).
- **Connect** new believers to the church's new converts' class.

Many people who would never attend a church building will enter a neighbour's living room.

# Leaders and multiplication | Structure that keeps groups healthy.
## Structure and reporting | Exodus 18:21-26; 1 Corinthians 14:40; Proverbs 27:23
Moses appointed "rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens" (Exodus 18:21). Large churches stay caring by organising in small units with leaders who report upward.

A simple structure:

- **Fellowship leader** for each home group (with an assistant).
- **Zone or area coordinator** over several groups.
- **Church pastor or coordinator** over the zones.

Weekly or monthly **reports** (attendance, visitors, new converts, needs, prayer requests) help leaders know "the state of thy flocks" (Proverbs 27:23) and respond quickly to problems.

Keep reporting **simple**, use it to **serve people** (not to pressure leaders), and protect confidential information.
## Training leaders and multiplying | 2 Timothy 2:2; Acts 6:3; Titus 1:5-9
Groups multiply only when leaders are trained.

- **Choose** faithful, mature members (Acts 6:3).
- **Train** them regularly: Bible study methods, pastoral care, evangelism, handling problems.
- **Apprentice**: each leader trains an assistant who leads parts of meetings.
- **Equip** with study outlines and resources.
- **Multiply** when groups become too large (about 12 to 15 regular adults): the assistant leads a new group nearby.
- **Pray** for and care for leaders; visit them.

Kumuyi's ministry is known for regular, systematic training of workers so that growth is supported by teaching. Without trained leaders, growth becomes crowds without care.
