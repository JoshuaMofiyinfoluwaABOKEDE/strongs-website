# God makes His servants | Calling and the process of preparation.
## The Potter and the clay | Jeremiah 18:1-6; Isaiah 64:8; Ephesians 2:10
God sent Jeremiah to the potter's house. The vessel "was marred in the hand of the potter: so he made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter to make it" (Jeremiah 18:4). "Cannot I do with you as this potter?" (verse 6).

Ministry begins with God's making, not with our doing. Gbile Akanni has stressed in his teaching that what God makes of the minister matters more than what the minister makes of his ministry. Gifts can be displayed quickly; character is formed slowly.

God's making involves:

- **A call**: He chooses and sets apart (Galatians 1:15).
- **A process**: years of preparation, often hidden.
- **Breaking**: removing self-reliance and pride.
- **Filling**: His Spirit, His Word, His character.
- **Sending**: in His timing, to His work.

"We are the clay, and thou our potter; and we all are the work of thy hand" (Isaiah 64:8). A minister's first task is to stay on the wheel.
## The hidden years | Exodus 3:1-10; Psalm 78:70-72; Galatians 1:15-18
Moses spent forty years tending sheep in Midian before the burning bush (Exodus 3:1; Acts 7:30). David was taken "from the sheepfolds" and "fed them according to the integrity of his heart" (Psalm 78:70-72). After his conversion Paul went into Arabia and only after three years went up to Jerusalem (Galatians 1:17-18). Even Jesus lived thirty years in Nazareth before His public ministry.

Lessons of the hidden years:

- God is **not in a hurry**; He is thorough.
- **Small faithfulness** prepares for large responsibility (Luke 16:10).
- Hidden years train **character**: humility, patience, dependence.
- They teach us to **hear God** without crowds.
- They expose **wrong motives** before they can do public harm.

If you feel unseen, don't despise the season. Sheep, caves and deserts were God's classrooms.

# Breaking and testing | How God deals with self.
## Brokenness | Genesis 32:24-31; Psalm 51:17; John 12:24
Jacob was a schemer until he wrestled all night with God. At daybreak his thigh was out of joint; he limped, but he was blessed and renamed Israel (Genesis 32:24-31). God broke his self-reliance to make him a prince with God.

"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise" (Psalm 51:17). "Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit" (John 12:24).

Brokenness is not weakness of personality. It is the end of **self-will**, self-confidence and self-glory, so that Christ's life can flow. Gbile Akanni's teaching has repeatedly called ministers to the way of the cross: death to self as the path to fruitful ministry.

Signs of brokenness:

- Quick to repent, slow to defend yourself.
- Able to receive correction.
- No need to be seen or praised.
- Dependence on God in prayer.
- Gentleness with others' failures.
## Tested and proved | Deuteronomy 8:2-3; James 1:2-4; 1 Peter 1:6-7
"Thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness, to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart" (Deuteronomy 8:2).

God tests ministers in many ways:

- **Obscurity**: will you serve when no one notices?
- **Delay**: will you wait for God's timing?
- **Money**: will you remain honest and content in lack and in plenty?
- **Opposition and false accusation**: will you respond in love?
- **Success**: will you stay humble?
- **Temptation**: will you flee like Joseph?

"The trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing" (James 1:3-4). Tests reveal and refine. A minister who has passed through fire is trusted by God and by people.

# The inner life of the minister | Self-watch and private disciplines.
## Take heed to yourself | Acts 20:28; 1 Timothy 4:16; Proverbs 4:23
Paul told the Ephesian elders, "Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock" (Acts 20:28): yourselves first. "Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine" (1 Timothy 4:16).

Spurgeon's first lecture to his students was on **the minister's self-watch**. He urged that the preacher must be a saved man, a growing man, and a holy man, because his life preaches as loudly as his sermons. A minister who is careless about his own soul cannot care well for others.

Self-watch means asking regularly:

- Is my love for Christ fresh, or am I running on routine?
- Am I practising what I preach?
- What sins am I tolerating?
- Are my motives pure, or am I seeking applause, money or influence?
- How is my family?

"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life" (Proverbs 4:23).
## Private disciplines | Mark 1:35; Psalm 119:11; Matthew 6:6
Public power flows from private devotion. Jesus rose "a great while before day" to pray alone (Mark 1:35). David hid God's word in his heart (Psalm 119:11). Jesus taught, "when thou prayest, enter into thy closet" (Matthew 6:6).

Disciplines that sustain ministers:

- **Daily prayer**, not only for the work, but for communion with God.
- **Personal Bible reading** for your own soul, not only sermon preparation.
- **Fasting** at times, to humble yourself and seek God.
- **Solitude and silence**: time away from crowds.
- **Study**: Spurgeon read widely and urged his students to keep learning.
- **Confession and accountability** with trusted brethren.
- **Sabbath rest**: regular rest is obedience, not laziness.
- **Simplicity**: guarding against the love of money and display.

These disciplines are not merit badges. They are the means by which God keeps making the minister long after the hidden years end.

2. Which season of preparation you believe you are in (hidden, testing, serving) and what God seems to be teaching you.
3. One way God has broken or tested you, and what it produced.
4. Your self-watch questions and a weekly plan for at least five private disciplines.
5. One person who will walk with you in this formation.
