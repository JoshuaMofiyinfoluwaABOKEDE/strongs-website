# The writer's calling | Why Christians write and who we write for.
## Writing as service | Habakkuk 2:2; Luke 1:1-4; John 20:31
"Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it" (Habakkuk 2:2). God has always used writing. Much of the Bible came to us because people wrote carefully. Luke researched "from the very first" and wrote "in order" so that Theophilus might "know the certainty" of what he had been taught (Luke 1:3-4). John wrote "that ye might believe" (John 20:31).

Christian writing is **service**. We write to help readers know God, understand truth, find hope and act wisely. That means:

- **Truthfulness**: never inventing facts, quotes or testimonies.
- **Clarity**: "make it plain", so readers can run with it.
- **Love for readers**: thinking about their needs, not just our ideas.
- **Excellence**: our work represents Christ.

You do not need to be famous to write well. You need something true to say, a reader to serve, and the patience to learn the craft.
## Habits of a writer | Proverbs 22:29; Ecclesiastes 12:9-12; Colossians 3:23
The Preacher "sought to find out acceptable words: and that which was written was upright, even words of truth" (Ecclesiastes 12:10). Good writing is sought out; it takes work.

Habits that grow writers:

- **Read widely and well.** Read the Bible, good books, and writers you admire. Notice how they write.
- **Write regularly.** Even 300 words a day builds skill.
- **Keep an idea notebook.** Note sermons, conversations, questions and observations.
- **Protect writing time.** Treat it like an appointment.
- **Finish things.** A finished imperfect piece teaches more than ten unfinished perfect ones.
- **Seek feedback** humbly.

"Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings" (Proverbs 22:29). "Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord" (Colossians 3:23). Writing well is part of serving well.

# From idea to outline | Finding your main point and planning a piece.
## One big idea | Proverbs 25:11; 1 Corinthians 14:8-9
"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver" (Proverbs 25:11). Before you write, decide what you are trying to say.

Write your **main idea in one sentence**. For example: "Waiting on God is active trust, not passive delay." If you cannot state your point in one sentence, you are not ready to write.

Then ask:

- **Who is my reader?** Students, new believers, parents, professionals?
- **What do they need?** Information, encouragement, warning, steps?
- **What should they do or believe** after reading?
- **How long** should it be, and where will it appear (blog, newsletter, magazine, booklet)?

Paul said that if the trumpet gives an uncertain sound, no one prepares for battle (1 Corinthians 14:8). Unclear writing is the uncertain trumpet. A clear main idea is the first cure.
## Building an outline | Luke 1:3; Nehemiah 8:8
Luke wrote "in order" (Luke 1:3). An **outline** puts your ideas in order before you draft.

A simple outline:

1. **Opening**: a hook (story, question, surprising fact) and your main idea.
2. **Point one** with explanation and an example.
3. **Point two** with explanation and an example.
4. **Point three** (optional) with explanation and an example.
5. **Closing**: summarise, apply, and give the reader a next step.

Tips:

- Each point should **support the main idea**. Cut points that don't.
- Order points logically: problem to solution, simple to complex, or first to last in time.
- Note where Scripture, quotations or facts will go, and **check every source**.
- Write the outline in short phrases; it is a map, not the journey.

Ezra's helpers "read in the book... distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand" (Nehemiah 8:8). Order helps understanding.

# Clear sentences | Writing that readers understand at first reading.
## Plain and concrete | Matthew 5:37; 1 Corinthians 2:1-4
Jesus spoke plainly and used concrete pictures: salt, light, seeds, coins, lamps. Paul did not come "with excellency of speech" but to make Christ known (1 Corinthians 2:1-2). Clear writing is not shallow; it respects the reader.

Guidelines:

- **Prefer short, familiar words**: *use* not *utilise*, *help* not *facilitate*.
- **Keep most sentences short** (15 to 20 words), with some variety.
- **Use active voice**: "The pastor prayed" not "Prayer was offered by the pastor."
- **Be concrete**: not "She faced challenges" but "She lost her job in March and could not pay her rent."
- **Cut empty words**: *very*, *really*, *basically*, *in order to*.
- **Avoid jargon and church clichés** that outsiders won't understand, or explain them.

Read your sentence aloud. If you stumble, your reader will too.
## Strong paragraphs and flow | Ecclesiastes 12:10; Colossians 4:6
A **paragraph** develops one idea. Start with a clear topic sentence, support it with explanation, example or evidence, and end by linking to the next idea.

Online and on phones, keep paragraphs short: two to four sentences. Use headings and lists when they help readers scan.

**Flow** comes from connecting words and logical order:

- To add: *also, in addition, furthermore*.
- To contrast: *but, however, yet*.
- To show result: *so, therefore, as a result*.
- To give an example: *for example, for instance*.

Use **Scripture accurately**. Quote the exact words, give the reference and version, and make sure your use fits the passage's meaning in context.

"Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt" (Colossians 4:6). Grace in writing means clarity, warmth and care for the reader.

# Revising and sharing | Turning a draft into finished work.
## The craft of revision | Proverbs 15:22; Jeremiah 36:27-32
Jeremiah's scroll was burned by the king, and he wrote it again, adding "many like words" (Jeremiah 36:32). Rewriting is part of the work.

Revise in **layers**:

1. **Big picture**: Is the main idea clear? Does every section support it? Is anything missing or repeated?
2. **Structure**: Is the order logical? Does the opening hook the reader? Does the ending give a next step?
3. **Paragraphs and sentences**: Is each paragraph about one thing? Are sentences clear and active?
4. **Words**: Cut empty words. Replace vague words with precise ones.
5. **Facts**: Check names, dates, figures, Bible references and quotations.

Leave the draft for a day before revising if you can. Fresh eyes see more. Read it aloud.
## Feedback and publishing | Proverbs 27:17; Proverbs 12:15
"Iron sharpeneth iron" (Proverbs 27:17), and "he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise" (Proverbs 12:15).

**Getting feedback**:

- Ask one or two trusted readers, ideally someone like your intended reader.
- Ask specific questions: "Where did you get lost? What was the main point? What should I cut?"
- Listen without defending yourself. Then decide what to change.

**Sharing your work**:

- Church bulletins, newsletters and magazines.
- Blogs and social media (keep it short and clear).
- STRONGS Writers' community publications.
- Competitions and anthologies.

Before publishing: follow the publication's guidelines, give credit to sources, and get **permission** before telling another person's story. Then publish, learn, and write the next piece.

@assignment Practical assignment: write and revise a short article
Write a 600 to 900 word article for a Christian magazine or blog on a topic of your choice. Hand in:

1. Your one-sentence main idea and a description of your reader.
2. Your outline.
3. Your first draft.
4. Your revised draft, with a short note (100 to 200 words) explaining the three most important changes you made and why, including any feedback you received.
