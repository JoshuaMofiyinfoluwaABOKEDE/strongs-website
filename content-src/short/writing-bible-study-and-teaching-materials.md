# The inductive Bible study | Questions that lead people into the text.
## Faithful to the text | Nehemiah 8:8; 2 Timothy 2:15; Acts 8:30-35
Philip asked the Ethiopian, "Understandest thou what thou readest?", and the man replied, "How can I, except some man should guide me?" (Acts 8:30-31). Study materials are guides that help people understand.

Faithful materials:

- **Start with the text**, not with a topic you want to push.
- **Study the passage yourself** first, using observation, interpretation and application.
- **Find the main point** of the passage in one sentence; build the study around it.
- **Respect context**: the chapter, the book, and the whole Bible.
- **Check doctrine** with trusted resources and a pastor.
- **Point to Christ** where the passage leads there (Luke 24:27).
- **Be humble** on disputed questions; present them fairly.

Daily Bible study guides have long shaped believers' habits. Many Nigerian Christians grew up with daily devotional and study guides from their churches and ministries, a reminder of how much a well-written guide can help people stay in the Word every day.
## Writing good questions | Luke 2:46-47; Matthew 16:13-16; Luke 10:26
Jesus taught with questions: "Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?... But whom say ye that I am?" (Matthew 16:13-15). "What is written in the law? how readest thou?" (Luke 10:26).

The inductive pattern uses three kinds of questions:

- **Observation** (What does it say?): "What does Paul command in verse 6? What reason does he give in verse 7?"
- **Interpretation** (What does it mean?): "Why do you think Paul connects prayer with peace here? What does 'guard your hearts' suggest?"
- **Application** (What will we do?): "What worry will you bring to God in prayer this week? How?"

Good questions are:

- **Open** (not yes/no).
- **Clear** and **short**.
- **Answerable from the text** (especially observation questions).
- **In a logical order**, building toward the main point.

Avoid: leading questions ("Isn't it wonderful that...?"), questions that only experts can answer, and vague questions ("What do you think about this passage?" alone).

# Lessons for different ages | Lesson plans for children, youth and adults.
## The lesson plan | Deuteronomy 6:6-9; Proverbs 22:6; Luke 2:52
"Train up a child in the way he should go" (Proverbs 22:6). Teaching is planned work.

A simple lesson plan:

1. **Aim**: what learners should know, feel and do by the end. ("Children will know that God hears prayer, feel safe to talk to Him, and pray a short prayer.")
2. **Bible passage** and **memory verse**.
3. **Hook** (3 to 5 minutes): a question, object, story or game that connects to the theme.
4. **Bible teaching** (10 to 20 minutes): tell or study the passage.
5. **Activity**: craft, drama, discussion, worksheet or game that reinforces the point.
6. **Application**: what will we do this week?
7. **Prayer** and **review**.
8. **Materials** needed.

Write a **leader's guide** with background notes, suggested answers and tips, and a **learner's sheet** with the text and questions.
## Age and learning styles | 1 Corinthians 13:11; Hebrews 5:12-14; 1 Corinthians 9:22
"When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child" (1 Corinthians 13:11). Materials must fit their learners.

- **Young children (3 to 7)**: short attention spans; stories, songs, pictures, movement, repetition, one simple truth.
- **Older children (8 to 12)**: love facts, challenges, quizzes and memory work; can look up verses.
- **Teenagers**: want honesty, discussion, real-life issues (identity, relationships, purpose), and respect for their questions.
- **Adults**: bring experience; value discussion, depth and practical application.
- **New believers**: basic truths, clear language, assurance.

Different people learn in different ways: some by **listening**, some by **seeing** (charts, pictures), some by **doing** (activities, drama). Vary methods.

Use **local examples** and languages. Keep reading levels appropriate; test with a typical learner. Move learners from "milk" to "meat" over time (Hebrews 5:12-14).

# Series and publishing | Designing a series and preparing materials for use.
## Designing a study series | Acts 20:27; Colossians 2:6-7
Paul "shunned not to declare... all the counsel of God" (Acts 20:27). A series helps people grow steadily, "rooted and built up in him" (Colossians 2:7).

Types of series:

- **Book study**: work through a book of the Bible (e.g. Philippians in four sessions).
- **Topical**: a theme across Scripture (prayer, holiness, money, evangelism), each session based on a main passage.
- **Discipleship**: foundations for new believers (assurance, prayer, the Word, the church, witness).
- **Character study**: lives of Bible people (Joseph, Ruth, Daniel, Peter).

Planning a series:

1. **Overall aim** of the series.
2. **Number of sessions** and **length** of each (45 to 60 minutes is common for groups).
3. **Session titles**, passages and main points in a table.
4. A **consistent format** for every session (opening question, read, observe, interpret, apply, pray).
5. **Progression**: each session builds on the last.
6. **Review** at the end.
## Editing, testing and publishing | Proverbs 15:22; 2 Timothy 2:2
"The things that thou hast heard of me... commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2). Good materials help ordinary leaders teach.

Before publishing:

- **Test** each session with a real group; note where people got confused or bored.
- **Revise** questions that didn't work.
- **Check** every Bible reference and quotation.
- **Doctrinal review** by a pastor or theologian.
- **Proofread** carefully.
- **Design** for readability: clear headings, space to write answers, large enough font, a simple layout that photocopies well.
- **Name the translation** used and follow its copyright guidelines for quotations.
- **Credit** sources and contributors.

Publishing options: printed booklets, PDF downloads, WhatsApp-friendly daily guides, a church website, or a STRONGS course. Include a short **"How to use this guide"** page for leaders.

@assignment Practical assignment: a four-session study series
Write a four-session Bible study series on a book or theme. For each session include: the passage, the main point in one sentence, an opening question, six to ten observation, interpretation and application questions, a closing prayer prompt, and leader's notes with suggested answers.

Add one children's lesson plan (for ages 6 to 10) based on one of the four passages.

Test at least one session with a group and hand in a 200 to 300 word report on what you revised afterwards.
