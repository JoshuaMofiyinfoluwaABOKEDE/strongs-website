# Mentoring | Walking alongside others.
## What mentoring is | Ruth 1:16-18; Titus 2:3-5; Proverbs 13:20
Ruth clung to Naomi: "whither thou goest, I will go" (Ruth 1:16). Naomi later guided Ruth wisely (Ruth 3). Titus 2 describes older women teaching younger women, and older men as examples. "He that walketh with wise men shall be wise" (Proverbs 13:20).

**Mentoring** is a relationship in which a more experienced person helps another grow in character, faith and skill. It differs from:

- **Teaching** (passing on information to many).
- **Coaching** (developing specific skills or goals).
- **Counselling** (helping with problems or wounds).

Mentoring may include all of these, but its heart is **relationship and example**.

Types of mentors:

- **Spiritual mentors** for walking with God.
- **Ministry mentors** for leadership and service.
- **Career mentors** in professions.
- **Peer mentors** at a similar stage.
- **Historical mentors**: biographies of faithful believers.

Most people need several mentors at different times.
## How to mentor well | Proverbs 20:5; 1 Thessalonians 2:11-12; Hebrews 13:7
Paul dealt with the Thessalonians "as a father doth his children", exhorting, comforting and charging them (1 Thessalonians 2:11).

Practical mentoring:

- **Agree on purpose and rhythm**: e.g. meet monthly for a year, focused on prayer life and leadership.
- **Let the mentee lead the agenda** with questions and needs.
- **Ask good questions** more than giving speeches:
  - "How is your walk with God?"
  - "What are you learning? Where are you struggling?"
  - "What decision are you facing? What options do you see?"
  - "What will you do before we next meet?"
- **Share honestly** from your own life, including failures.
- **Use Scripture** and good books.
- **Pray together**.
- **Keep confidences**.
- **Respect boundaries**: mentoring across genders needs special wisdom and openness.
- **End or review** at the agreed time.

# Succession | Preparing the next leader.
## Biblical succession | Numbers 27:12-23; 1 Kings 19:16-21; Acts 20:28-32
God told Moses he would die and not enter Canaan. Moses' first concern was succession: "Let the LORD... set a man over the congregation" (Numbers 27:16). God chose Joshua; Moses commissioned him publicly and "put some of [his] honour upon him, that all the congregation... may be obedient" (verse 20).

God told Elijah to anoint Elisha "to be prophet in thy room" (1 Kings 19:16). Elisha served Elijah for years before succeeding him.

Paul prepared the Ephesian elders for his absence: "I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in... Therefore watch" (Acts 20:29, 31).

David's succession, by contrast, was left unclear until a crisis (1 Kings 1), and it brought conflict. Unplanned succession often leads to division.

Lesson: **plan succession while you are strong**, not when you are dying or in crisis.
## Planning succession | Proverbs 15:22; 2 Timothy 2:2; Deuteronomy 31:7-8
A **succession plan** answers:

- **Which roles** need successors (senior pastor, key department heads, board members)?
- **What qualities** must the successor have (character, calling, gifts, doctrine, vision fit)?
- **Who are potential successors**? Develop several, not just one.
- **How will they be prepared**? Mentoring, responsibilities, training, exposure.
- **Who decides**? Board, elders, denomination or congregation, according to your constitution.
- **When and how** will the transition happen? Timeline, announcement, handover.
- **Emergency plan**: who leads if the leader is suddenly unable to?

Write the plan down and review it every year. Avoid **family entitlement**: a leader's child may be called, but should be chosen by the same standards as anyone else.

# Transitions | Handing over and moving on with honour.
## The handover | Deuteronomy 34:9; Joshua 1:1-9; 2 Kings 2:9-15
After Moses, "Joshua... was full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands upon him: and the children of Israel hearkened unto him" (Deuteronomy 34:9). God then spoke directly to Joshua: "as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee" (Joshua 1:5).

When Elijah was taken up, Elisha picked up his mantle and struck the Jordan, and the sons of the prophets said, "The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha" (2 Kings 2:15).

A good handover:

- **Public recognition** and prayer for the new leader.
- **Full transfer** of authority, information, relationships and resources.
- **Overlap period** where helpful, with clear roles.
- **Honour** for the outgoing leader.
- **Freedom** for the new leader to lead differently.
- **Clear communication** to members and partners.
## Life after leadership | 2 Timothy 4:6-8; Psalm 92:12-15; Acts 20:32
"They shall still bring forth fruit in old age" (Psalm 92:14). Leaving a position is not leaving God's service.

For outgoing leaders:

- **Let go**: don't undermine or second-guess your successor publicly.
- **Be available** as a counsellor when asked.
- **Find new service**: mentoring, writing, prayer, teaching, missions.
- **Prepare financially** and emotionally for transition.
- **Commend** the work to God: "I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace" (Acts 20:32).

For incoming leaders:

- **Honour** those before you.
- **Learn** the history before changing things.
- **Seek counsel**, but lead with your own God-given vision.

Paul ended well: "I have finished my course" (2 Timothy 4:7). A leader's legacy is people who carry on faithfully.
