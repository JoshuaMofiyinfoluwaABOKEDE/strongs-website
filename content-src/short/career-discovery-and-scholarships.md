# Your work as a calling | Seeing careers through God's eyes.
## Work is God's idea | Genesis 2:15; Colossians 3:23-24; Ephesians 2:10
Before sin entered the world, "the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:15). Work is not a curse; it is part of God's good design. Sin made work hard, but did not make it meaningless.

"We are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them" (Ephesians 2:10). God has good works prepared for you, in church **and** in the classroom, clinic, farm, court, studio, office and workshop.

A **calling** is not only for pastors. Joseph served in government, Daniel in public administration, Lydia in business, Luke in medicine, Paul in tent-making. "Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord... for ye serve the Lord Christ" (Colossians 3:23-24).

So ask not only "What will pay well?" but also "Where can I serve God and people well with what He has given me?"
## Seeking guidance | Proverbs 3:5-6; Proverbs 15:22; James 1:5
"Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths" (Proverbs 3:5-6).

God guides through:

- **His Word**: principles about honesty, service and stewardship.
- **Prayer**: "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God" (James 1:5).
- **Your gifts and interests**: what you do well and love doing.
- **Counsel**: "in the multitude of counsellors they are established" (Proverbs 15:22). Talk to parents, teachers, mentors and people working in fields that interest you.
- **Open and closed doors**: opportunities, results and circumstances.

Career choice is rarely one dramatic moment. It is a series of faithful steps. Take the next right step with the light you have, and stay open to God's leading.

# Gifts and careers | Discovering who you are and what fits.
## Knowing yourself | Romans 12:3-8; 1 Peter 4:10; Psalm 139:14
"Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, let us use them" (Romans 12:6). "As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards" (1 Peter 4:10).

Think honestly about (Romans 12:3, "not to think of himself more highly than he ought"):

- **Strengths and skills**: subjects you do well in; things people ask your help with.
- **Interests**: what you enjoy reading, watching or doing in free time.
- **Personality**: do you enjoy people or quiet work? Ideas or practical tasks? Structure or variety?
- **Values**: what matters most to you? Helping people, creativity, security, justice, discovery?
- **Burdens**: what problems in your community move you (health, education, poverty, injustice)?

Write these down. Ask two people who know you well what they see in you. Patterns will emerge.
## Exploring careers | Proverbs 22:29; Proverbs 24:27; 2 Timothy 2:15
Once you know yourself better, explore careers:

- **Research** jobs online: what they do, qualifications needed, typical pay, and future demand.
- **Talk to professionals**: ask about their daily work, the training path, and what they wish they had known.
- **Shadow or volunteer**: spend a day in a hospital, law office, farm, studio or tech hub.
- **Look at courses** that lead there: JAMB subject combinations, polytechnic and university programmes, vocational training, apprenticeships.
- **Consider growing fields**: technology, healthcare, agriculture and agribusiness, renewable energy, education, creative industries.
- **Keep options** open: many skills (writing, numbers, communication, digital skills) transfer across careers.

"Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house" (Proverbs 24:27). Prepare well before you commit.

Make a shortlist of three careers and the steps toward each.

# Scholarships and applications | Finding opportunities and applying well.
## Finding scholarships | Proverbs 21:5; Ecclesiastes 11:6
"The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness" (Proverbs 21:5). "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand" (Ecclesiastes 11:6): apply widely and keep sowing.

Where to look:

- **Government**: federal and state scholarship boards, education trust funds, and student loan schemes (check current official websites).
- **Companies and foundations**: many Nigerian banks, oil and gas companies and foundations run undergraduate scholarships.
- **Universities**: merit and need-based awards at your school.
- **International**: Commonwealth, Chevening, Mastercard Foundation, DAAD and others, mostly for postgraduate study (check eligibility).
- **Churches and ministries**, including STRONGS opportunities shared in our communities.

Keep a **scholarship tracker** (a spreadsheet): name, link, eligibility, deadline, documents needed, status.

**Avoid scams**: genuine scholarships do not ask you to pay to apply or to "process" an award. Check the official website.
## Applying well: CV and personal statement | Proverbs 22:1; Proverbs 12:22; Daniel 1:4
Daniel and his friends were chosen for being "well favoured, and skilful in all wisdom" (Daniel 1:4). Your application should show who you are, honestly.

**CV** (one to two pages):

- Name and contact details.
- Education (most recent first) with results.
- Achievements and awards.
- Leadership and volunteering (fellowship, clubs, outreach).
- Skills (languages, digital, other).
- Referees (with permission).

**Personal statement or essay**:

- **Answer the question** asked.
- Start with a **specific story** or moment that shows your motivation.
- Show your **goals** and how this opportunity helps you reach them.
- Give **evidence**: achievements, responsibilities, lessons learned.
- Show how you will **give back**.
- **Be honest**. "Lying lips are abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 12:22). Never exaggerate or pay someone to write it for you.
- **Proofread** and ask a teacher or mentor to review it.

Meet deadlines; submit early.
