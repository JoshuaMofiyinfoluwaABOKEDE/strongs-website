# Before you write | Funders, fit and the concept note.
## Finding the right funder | Nehemiah 2:1-8; Philippians 4:14-19
When Nehemiah approached King Artaxerxes, he had prayed, he knew exactly what he needed (time, letters for safe passage, timber), and he asked clearly (Nehemiah 2:4-8). "And the king granted me, according to the good hand of my God upon me" (verse 8).

Funders include:

- **Churches and denominations**, mission agencies and Christian foundations.
- **Local and international foundations** and NGOs.
- **Government agencies** and development partners.
- **Companies** through corporate social responsibility (CSR) programmes.
- **Individual partners** and diaspora networks.

Find a **fit**: read the funder's mission, priorities, eligible countries and organisations, grant sizes and past grants. Don't twist your project to chase money that doesn't fit your calling.

Read the **call for proposals** (or guidelines) at least twice. Highlight requirements, deadlines, word limits, formats and scoring criteria. Many proposals are rejected for not following instructions.
## The concept note | Habakkuk 2:2; Proverbs 16:3
"Write the vision, and make it plain" (Habakkuk 2:2). Many funders first ask for a **concept note** (one to three pages). Even if they don't, writing one clarifies your project.

A concept note includes:

- **Organisation**: who you are, your track record, legal status.
- **Problem**: what need you address, with evidence.
- **Solution**: what you will do, for whom, where.
- **Results**: what will change.
- **Budget**: total amount requested and other contributions.
- **Timeframe**.
- **Contact**.

Write it so a busy reader understands your project in two minutes. If you can't explain the project simply, it isn't ready.

"Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established" (Proverbs 16:3). Pray, plan, and seek counsel from people who have won grants before.

# Building the proposal | Problem, objectives, plan and budget.
## Problem, goal and objectives | Nehemiah 1:3-4; Luke 14:28-30
Nehemiah's problem was concrete: "the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire" (Nehemiah 1:3).

**Problem statement**:

- Describe the need **specifically**, with **evidence**: statistics, surveys, reports, local observations, stories (with consent).
- Explain **causes** and **effects**.
- Show **who** is affected and **where**.
- Explain why now and why your organisation.

**Goal**: the long-term change you contribute to ("Improved literacy among primary pupils in Ogbomoso North").

**Objectives**: specific results the project will achieve. Make them **SMART**:

- **S**pecific
- **M**easurable
- **A**chievable
- **R**elevant
- **T**ime-bound

Example: "By July 2027, 200 Primary 4 pupils in five schools will improve their reading scores by at least 20%."
## Activities, logframe and budget | 1 Chronicles 22:14-16; 2 Corinthians 8:20-21
David prepared materials for the temple in detail: gold, silver, brass, iron, timber, stone, and workmen of every kind (1 Chronicles 22:14-15). A good plan is detailed.

**Activities**: what you will do to achieve each objective (train 10 volunteer tutors; run twice-weekly reading clubs; provide 500 books).

A **logical framework (logframe)** links:

- **Inputs** (money, staff, materials) → **Activities** → **Outputs** (things produced: 40 sessions held, 500 books distributed) → **Outcomes** (changes: improved reading scores) → **Impact** (long-term goal).
- For each level: **indicators**, **means of verification** (how you'll prove it), and **assumptions/risks**.

**Budget**:

- List every cost: personnel, materials, travel, venue, equipment, administration, monitoring and evaluation.
- Show **unit cost × quantity**.
- Include a **budget narrative** justifying major items.
- Be **realistic**, neither inflated nor too low to deliver.
- Show **other contributions** (your own, partners', in-kind).

**Timeline**: a simple table or Gantt chart of activities by month.

# Writing, submitting and accountability | Finishing well and reporting honestly.
## Writing and submitting | Proverbs 21:5; Ecclesiastes 9:10
A typical full proposal:

1. **Cover page** and **executive summary** (written last, one page).
2. **Organisation background** and capacity.
3. **Problem statement**.
4. **Goal, objectives** and **target beneficiaries**.
5. **Activities and methodology**.
6. **Logframe** and **monitoring and evaluation (M&E) plan**: what you'll measure, how, when, and who.
7. **Timeline**.
8. **Budget** and narrative.
9. **Sustainability**: how the work continues after funding ends.
10. **Risks** and how you'll manage them.
11. **Attachments**: registration certificate, accounts, CVs, letters of support.

Writing tips:

- Use the **funder's headings and words**.
- **Short paragraphs**, clear headings, tables.
- **Specific, evidence-based** claims.
- **Stay within word limits**.
- **Proofread** and have someone else review it against the guidelines.
- **Submit early**; portals often crash near deadlines.
## Integrity and reporting | 2 Corinthians 8:20-21; Luke 16:10-12; Proverbs 11:3
Paul handled the collection for Jerusalem carefully, "Avoiding this, that no man should blame us in this abundance which is administered by us: Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men" (2 Corinthians 8:20-21).

Integrity in grants:

- **Honest claims**: don't exaggerate needs, capacity or results.
- **No double-funding**: don't charge two funders for the same costs without disclosure.
- **Use funds as agreed**; ask permission before changing the budget significantly.
- **Keep records**: receipts, attendance lists, photos (with consent), reports.
- **Separate accounts** for project funds where possible; two signatories.
- **Report on time**, honestly, including **problems and lessons**. Funders respect honesty about challenges.
- **Thank** funders and partners.

"He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much" (Luke 16:10). Faithful reporting builds trust for future partnership.
