# Integrity before God | The foundation of ministry ethics.
## Above reproach | 1 Timothy 3:2; Psalm 15:1-5; 2 Corinthians 8:21
"A bishop then must be blameless" (1 Timothy 3:2), literally "above reproach": no legitimate charge can stick.

Psalm 15 asks who may dwell in God's holy hill and answers: one who "walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart", who "sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not", and who does not take bribes (verses 2, 4-5).

**Integrity** means wholeness: the same person in private and in public, in the pulpit and at home, online and offline.

Paul aimed to provide "for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men" (2 Corinthians 8:21). Ethics in ministry involves both **being** right before God and **being seen** to be right, so that the gospel is not discredited.
## Truthfulness | Ephesians 4:25; 2 Corinthians 4:2; Acts 5:3-4
"Putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour" (Ephesians 4:25). Paul renounced "the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully" (2 Corinthians 4:2).

Areas where ministers are tempted to bend the truth:

- **Preaching**: misusing texts, claiming revelations that are really opinions, borrowing others' sermons and stories as one's own.
- **Testimonies and miracles**: exaggerating or staging healings and results.
- **Numbers**: inflating attendance, converts or finances.
- **Credentials**: claiming degrees or titles not earned.
- **Prophecy**: predicting what God has not said (Deuteronomy 18:22), or using "God told me" to control people.
- **Excuses** and broken promises.

Ananias and Sapphira's sin was a lie about giving that sought spiritual reputation (Acts 5). Truthfulness in small things protects credibility in great things.

# Purity, money and power | Three great tests of ministers.
## Sexual integrity and boundaries | 1 Thessalonians 4:3-7; 2 Timothy 2:22; Genesis 39:7-12
"This is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication" (1 Thessalonians 4:3). "Flee also youthful lusts" (2 Timothy 2:22). Joseph "fled, and got him out" (Genesis 39:12).

Ministers often have access to people in vulnerable moments. Boundaries protect everyone:

- **Avoid being alone** with someone of the opposite sex in private settings; keep doors open or meet in visible places; involve a colleague.
- **Counsel** with care; refer long-term counselling of the opposite sex to a same-sex counsellor where possible.
- **Messages and calls**: keep them professional; avoid late-night private chats.
- **Physical contact**: appropriate and public only.
- **Guard your eyes and mind** online.
- **Invest in your marriage** if married; stay accountable if single.
- **Never** use spiritual authority to pursue a relationship with someone under your care. This is an abuse of power.

If a leader falls, the church must act with truth and care: protect victims, follow proper discipline, and do not hide it.
## Money and power | 1 Peter 5:2-3; Mark 10:42-45; 3 John 1:9-10
Peter warned elders to serve "not for filthy lucre" and not as "lords over God's heritage" (1 Peter 5:2-3). John rebuked Diotrephes, "who loveth to have the preeminence", spreading malicious words and putting people out of the church (3 John 1:9-10).

**Money**: transparent accounts, no personal use of ministry funds, fair pay decided by others, no manipulative fundraising or "pay for prayer" schemes (see Acts 8:18-20, where Peter rebuked Simon for thinking God's gift could be bought).

**Power**: spiritual authority is for serving and building up (2 Corinthians 10:8), never for control.

Signs of **spiritual abuse**:

- Using Scripture or "God told me" to control decisions about marriage, money, jobs or relationships.
- Demanding unquestioning loyalty; punishing questions.
- Shaming, threatening or cursing members who leave.
- Secrecy and lack of accountability for leaders.
- Exploiting members' time, money or bodies.

Healthy leaders welcome questions, accountability and people's freedom.

# Confidentiality, safeguarding and accountability | Protecting people and leaders.
## Confidentiality and safeguarding | Proverbs 11:13; Matthew 18:6; Proverbs 31:8-9
"A talebearer revealeth secrets: but he that is of a faithful spirit concealeth the matter" (Proverbs 11:13).

**Confidentiality**:

- Keep what people share in counselling and confession private.
- Don't use people's stories in sermons without permission.
- Share only on a **need-to-know** basis with other leaders, and tell the person when you must.
- **Limits**: confidentiality does not cover situations where someone is in danger of harming themselves or others, or where a child or vulnerable adult is being abused. Explain this limit at the start of counselling.

**Safeguarding**: Jesus' warning about causing little ones to stumble (Matthew 18:6) applies to protecting children and vulnerable adults.

- Screen workers; use the two-adult rule; train staff.
- Have a written policy and a designated safeguarding lead.
- Report abuse to appropriate authorities; never cover it up to protect the ministry's image.
- Support victims and take allegations seriously.
## Accountability structures | Proverbs 27:5-6; Galatians 2:11-14; Hebrews 13:17
Paul publicly corrected Peter when he behaved hypocritically (Galatians 2:11-14). Even apostles needed accountability. "Faithful are the wounds of a friend" (Proverbs 27:6).

Structures that protect:

- **Plural leadership**: elders, boards or councils with real authority, not just yes-men.
- **Personal accountability partners** who ask hard questions about purity, money, family and devotion.
- **Financial controls** and annual audits.
- **A code of conduct** signed by all leaders and workers.
- **Complaints procedure**: a safe way for members to raise concerns, including about the senior leader.
- **Denominational or network oversight** where available.
- **Regular review** of the leader's life and ministry.

Leaders who refuse accountability are in danger. Leaders who welcome it are protected, and so are their people.
