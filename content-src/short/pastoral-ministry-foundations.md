# The shepherd's calling | What pastors are and do.
## Shepherds under the Chief Shepherd | 1 Peter 5:1-4; Acts 20:28; Jeremiah 3:15
The word **pastor** means shepherd. God promised, "I will give you pastors according to mine heart, which shall feed you with knowledge and understanding" (Jeremiah 3:15).

Peter writes to elders: "Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind; Neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock. And when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory" (1 Peter 5:2-4).

Key truths:

- The flock is **God's**, not the pastor's.
- It was **purchased with his own blood** (Acts 20:28). Every member is precious.
- Pastors serve **under** the Chief Shepherd and will give account.
- The New Testament uses **elder**, **overseer (bishop)** and **pastor** for the same office, usually exercised by a team (Acts 14:23; Titus 1:5-7).
## The core tasks | Ezekiel 34:2-16; John 21:15-17; Acts 6:4
Ezekiel condemned shepherds who fed themselves and listed what they failed to do: strengthen the diseased, heal the sick, bind up the broken, bring back the driven away, seek the lost (Ezekiel 34:4). Then God promised, "I will feed my flock" (34:15). That list describes the pastor's work.

Jesus restored Peter with three commands: "Feed my lambs... Feed my sheep... Feed my sheep" (John 21:15-17).

Core tasks:

- **Feeding**: preaching and teaching the Word.
- **Praying** for the flock.
- **Leading**: vision, direction and decisions with other leaders.
- **Protecting** from false teaching and harm (Acts 20:29-31).
- **Caring**: visiting, counselling, comforting.
- **Seeking**: the lost and the straying.
- **Equipping** members for ministry (Ephesians 4:11-12).
- **Modelling** a godly life.

# Feeding the flock | Preaching, teaching and prayer.
## A teaching ministry | 2 Timothy 4:1-5; Acts 20:20, 27; Nehemiah 8:8
Paul taught the Ephesians "publickly, and from house to house" and did not shun "to declare unto you all the counsel of God" (Acts 20:20, 27).

A healthy teaching ministry:

- **Expository preaching**: working through books of the Bible so the church hears the whole counsel of God, not only favourite topics.
- **Balanced diet**: Old and New Testaments; doctrine and practice; comfort and challenge.
- **Planned**: a preaching calendar for the year, with series.
- **Accessible**: in the people's languages, with clear application.
- **Multi-level**: Sunday preaching, midweek Bible study, house fellowships, new believers' classes, children and youth teaching.
- **Shared**: train other teachers.

Spurgeon urged preachers to labour over their sermons, saying that people deserve the best fruit of the pastor's study. Feed the sheep, not just entertain the goats.
## Praying for the flock | Colossians 4:12; Job 1:5; Luke 22:31-32
Epaphras was "always labouring fervently for you in prayers, that ye may stand perfect and complete in all the will of God" (Colossians 4:12). Job rose early to offer for each of his children (Job 1:5). Jesus told Peter, "I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not" (Luke 22:32).

Practical pastoral prayer:

- Keep a **membership list** and pray through it regularly (for example, a set of names each day).
- Pray for **specific needs**: the sick, bereaved, jobless, students in exams, new believers.
- Pray for **leaders** and workers.
- Pray **with** people when you visit.
- Hold regular **church prayer meetings**.
- Fast and pray at critical times.

A pastor who prays for his people learns to love them, and they sense it.

# Caring and leading | Visiting, counselling, occasions and team leadership.
## Visiting and counselling | James 1:27; Galatians 6:1-2; Proverbs 20:5
"Pure religion... is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction" (James 1:27).

**Visiting**:

- Visit new members, the sick, the bereaved, the elderly, and those who stop attending.
- Go with another leader where appropriate (especially when visiting someone of the opposite sex).
- Listen more than you speak; read a short Scripture; pray.
- Keep visits short and respectful; follow up.

**Counselling** basics:

- **Listen** carefully; "counsel in the heart of man is like deep water; but a man of understanding will draw it out" (Proverbs 20:5).
- **Use Scripture** gently and wisely.
- **Keep confidences**, except where someone is in danger or a crime involving harm must be reported.
- **Know your limits**: refer to doctors, mental health professionals, lawyers or experienced counsellors when needed.
- **Restore gently** those who have fallen (Galatians 6:1).
## Occasions, ordinances and leading with others | 1 Corinthians 11:23-26; Titus 1:5; Acts 15:6
Pastors lead the church at life's key moments:

- **Baptism** and the **Lord's Supper** (1 Corinthians 11:23-26), taught clearly and administered reverently according to your church's practice.
- **Child dedications** and naming ceremonies.
- **Weddings**: premarital counselling, legal requirements and Christ-centred ceremonies.
- **Funerals**: comfort the bereaved, preach the hope of the resurrection, and care for the family afterwards.

**Leading with others**:

- Paul appointed **elders** in every church (Titus 1:5). Pastoral ministry is a team effort.
- In Acts 15 "the apostles and elders came together for to consider of this matter": decisions were made together, with Scripture and the Spirit's guidance.
- Develop **deacons and workers** to share practical tasks (Acts 6).
- Guard **unity** and **doctrine**.
- Keep transparent **finances** and good records.
- Submit to **accountability**: your denomination, fellow pastors or a board.

@assignment Practical assignment: a pastoral ministry plan
Write a 500 to 800 word plan for a congregation or fellowship you serve (or imagine one you may serve):

1. A six-month preaching and teaching plan with at least one book series.
2. A system for praying for members by name.
3. A visiting plan: who, how often, and with whom.
4. Your approach to one pastoral occasion (wedding, funeral or dedication).
5. How you will share leadership and remain accountable.

If possible, carry out one pastoral visit with a senior leader and add a 150-word reflection.
