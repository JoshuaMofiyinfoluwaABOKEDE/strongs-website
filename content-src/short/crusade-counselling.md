# The counsellor's heart and conduct | Who a counsellor is and how he or she behaves.
## A servant at the harvest | Matthew 9:37-38; 2 Timothy 2:24-26; 1 Peter 3:15
When the appeal is given at a crusade, people come forward to give their lives to Christ. **Counsellors** are the trained believers who meet them, explain the gospel personally, pray with them and take their details for follow-up. The preacher casts the net; counsellors help draw it in.

A counsellor must be:

- **Born again and walking with God.** You cannot lead someone where you have not been.
- **Gentle and patient**: "the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient" (2 Timothy 2:24).
- **Prepared**: "ready always to give an answer... with meekness and fear" (1 Peter 3:15).
- **Prayerful**: pray before and during the meeting.
- **Teachable**: follow the crusade team's instructions.

Counselling is not a stage role. It is quiet, humble service, often in dust, heat or rain. Its reward is seeing souls born into the kingdom.
## Conduct and appearance | 1 Corinthians 9:22; Colossians 4:5-6; 1 Thessalonians 5:22
How a counsellor behaves can open or close a heart.

- **Dress modestly and neatly**, wearing your counsellor badge or tag.
- **Counsel people of the same sex** where possible: men with men, women with women. Children are counselled with another worker present.
- **Keep good hygiene**; carry mints, not gum.
- **Be warm but respectful.** Smile. Use their name. Do not touch beyond a handshake or a hand on the shoulder when praying, and ask first.
- **Do not argue, preach a second sermon, or discuss denominations.** Point to Christ.
- **Speak simply**, in the person's language where possible. Use an interpreter if needed.
- **Keep confidences.** What people share is private.
- **Never ask for money** or promise healing, jobs or visas.

"Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt" (Colossians 4:6). Your manner should make Christ attractive.

# Counselling at the altar | A simple order for leading someone to Christ and preparing for follow-up.
## The five steps | Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Romans 10:9-10; John 1:12
A simple order that works at the altar:

1. **Welcome and ask.** "My name is ___. What is your name? Why did you come forward today?" Listen. Some come for salvation, some to rededicate, some for prayer.
2. **Explain the gospel briefly** with a few verses:
   - We have all sinned (Romans 3:23).
   - Sin's wage is death, but God's gift is eternal life (Romans 6:23).
   - Christ died for us (Romans 5:8).
   - We must believe and confess Jesus as Lord (Romans 10:9-10).
3. **Check understanding.** "Do you understand? Is this what you want?" Never pressure.
4. **Pray together.** Let them pray in their own words, or repeat a simple prayer of repentance and faith after you.
5. **Assure and record.** Show them John 1:12 and 1 John 5:12. Fill in the decision card fully and clearly.

Spend about five to ten minutes. If many are waiting, stay focused and kind.
## Decision cards and difficult moments | 1 John 5:11-13; James 5:14-16; Acts 8:30-31
The **decision card** is how follow-up happens. Record: full name, phone number (ask them to check it), area or address, age group, church (if any), the type of decision and any prayer need. A wrong phone number can mean a lost convert. Hand the cards to the follow-up coordinator the same day.

Common situations:

- **Someone wants prayer for sickness.** Pray with faith and compassion, but do not promise results. Make sure they also hear the gospel.
- **Someone is distressed or manifesting.** Stay calm, call a team leader, and pray quietly. Do not create a spectacle.
- **Someone is already a believer.** Encourage them, pray for rededication, and record it as such.
- **Someone has questions you cannot answer.** Be honest: "That's a good question; let us get you connected with someone who can help." Record it.
- **A child responds.** Counsel simply with another worker present and record a parent's contact.

Like Philip with the Ethiopian (Acts 8:30-31), your job is to explain and guide, then to connect.
