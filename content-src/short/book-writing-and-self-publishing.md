# From idea to plan | Shaping your book.
## Why this book? | Habakkuk 2:2-3; Ecclesiastes 12:12; John 21:25
"Write the vision, and make it plain... For the vision is yet for an appointed time" (Habakkuk 2:2-3). "Of making many books there is no end" (Ecclesiastes 12:12). Many books exist, so ask: why **this** book, by **me**, for **these readers**?

Answer in a few sentences:

- **Reader**: who exactly is it for? ("Young Christian professionals in Nigerian cities trying to keep their faith alive in busy jobs.")
- **Promise**: what will they gain? ("Practical, biblical habits for a steady walk with God on a full schedule.")
- **Why you**: your experience, calling, knowledge or story.
- **Different**: how is it different from books already available?
- **Type**: devotional, teaching, testimony, biography, novel, workbook.
- **Length**: many non-fiction books are 30,000 to 60,000 words; devotionals vary; children's books are much shorter.

Write a **working title** and subtitle. "Rooted: Growing a Strong Faith in a Busy City" tells readers what they'll get.
## Outline and proposal | Luke 1:1-4; Proverbs 16:9
Luke wrote "in order" (Luke 1:3). An outline turns a big idea into manageable pieces.

**Non-fiction outline**:

- Introduction: the problem, the promise, how to use the book.
- 8 to 15 chapters, each with one main idea, in logical order (problem → principles → practice, or chronological).
- For each chapter: a title, a one-sentence summary, key points, Scriptures, stories and application.
- Conclusion: summary and call to action.

**Fiction**: a synopsis, main characters, and a chapter-by-chapter plot outline.

A **book proposal** (needed for traditional publishers and useful for everyone):

- Title and subtitle, a one-paragraph summary, target readers.
- Author bio and platform (who already listens to you).
- Comparable books.
- Chapter outline and one or two sample chapters.

"A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps" (Proverbs 16:9). Plan, and stay open to change as you write.

# Writing the draft | Habits that finish books.
## The writing habit | Proverbs 6:6-8; Nehemiah 6:3; Galatians 6:9
Books are finished by steady work. Nehemiah refused distractions: "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down" (Nehemiah 6:3).

Building the habit:

- **Set a goal**: e.g. 500 words a day, five days a week = 50,000 words in about 20 weeks.
- **Schedule** writing like an appointment: early morning, lunch, or late evening.
- **Protect** the time: phone off, door closed.
- **Track** progress in a spreadsheet or chart.
- **Write first, edit later**: the first draft is for getting it down. Don't polish chapter one forever.
- **Leave notes** like [CHECK REFERENCE] and keep moving.
- **Keep an idea file** for stories and illustrations.
- **Pray** before each session.

"Let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not" (Galatians 6:9). Most unfinished books stop in the middle. Keep going.
## Writing chapters that work | Matthew 7:24-29; Nehemiah 8:8
Jesus' teaching ended with a story that made the point unforgettable (Matthew 7:24-27), and "the people were astonished at his doctrine" (verse 28).

A strong non-fiction chapter often has:

1. **Opening hook**: a story, question or scene.
2. **The main idea** stated clearly.
3. **Teaching**: explanation from Scripture or research, in two to four sections with subheadings.
4. **Illustrations**: true stories (with permission), examples, analogies.
5. **Application**: practical steps, reflection questions, prayers or exercises.
6. **Closing**: a short summary and a bridge to the next chapter.

Tips:

- Write to **one reader**.
- Keep chapters **similar in length** (3,000 to 5,000 words is common).
- Quote Scripture **accurately**, naming the translation; check copyright rules for modern translations (most allow limited quotation with a notice).
- **Credit** every quotation and source.
- Back up your manuscript in at least **two places**.

# Revising and editing | From draft to professional manuscript.
## Self-revision | Jeremiah 36:28-32; Proverbs 27:17
After the king burned Jeremiah's scroll, it was written again with "many like words" added (Jeremiah 36:32). Rewriting is normal.

Revise in stages:

1. **Rest**: leave the draft for two to four weeks.
2. **Read through** the whole book quickly, noting big problems.
3. **Structural revision**: Does the book keep its promise? Are chapters in the right order? Is anything missing, repeated or unnecessary?
4. **Chapter and paragraph revision**: clarity, flow, examples.
5. **Line revision**: sentences and words.
6. **Fact-check**: Scripture references, quotations, names, dates, statistics.

**Beta readers**: give the manuscript to three to five people like your target readers. Ask specific questions: Where did you get bored or confused? What helped most? What should I cut?

"Iron sharpeneth iron" (Proverbs 27:17).
## Working with editors | Proverbs 12:15; Proverbs 15:31-32
"The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise" (Proverbs 12:15).

Types of editing (in order):

- **Developmental (structural) editing**: big-picture content and structure.
- **Copyediting (line editing)**: sentences, clarity, consistency, grammar.
- **Proofreading**: final check of typeset pages for errors.

Every serious book needs at least a copyedit and a proofread by someone other than the author.

Hiring an editor:

- Ask for **samples** and **references**.
- Agree on **scope, fee, deadlines** and **rights** in writing.
- Send a **clean manuscript**: double-spaced, page numbers, consistent formatting.
- **Receive feedback humbly**. You decide, but take every comment seriously (Proverbs 15:31-32).

For theological books, ask a **pastor or theologian** to review doctrine. For memoirs, ensure people in the story have been treated fairly.

# Publishing and reaching readers | ISBN, design, printing, ebooks and launch.
## Publishing routes and preparation | Psalm 68:11; Romans 13:7
"The Lord gave the word: great was the company of those that published it" (Psalm 68:11).

**Routes**:

- **Traditional publishing**: a publisher pays for editing, design, printing and distribution and pays you royalties. Competitive; requires a proposal.
- **Self-publishing**: you pay for and control everything, and keep more of the income.
- **Hybrid / assisted publishing**: you pay a company for services. Check carefully what you get; avoid companies that charge large fees and deliver little ("vanity presses").

**Preparing to self-publish in Nigeria**:

- **ISBN**: obtain from the **National Library of Nigeria** (the ISBN agency for Nigeria). Each format (paperback, hardback, ebook) needs its own ISBN.
- **Legal deposit**: Nigerian law requires publishers to deposit copies of new books with the National Library.
- **Copyright**: your work is protected automatically; the Nigerian Copyright Commission offers voluntary notification for extra evidence.
- **Cover design**: hire a professional; the cover sells the book. Include title, subtitle, author, back-cover blurb, ISBN barcode and price.
- **Interior layout (typesetting)**: readable font, margins, headers, page numbers, front matter (title page, copyright page, contents) and back matter.
## Printing, ebooks and launch | Luke 14:28-30; Ecclesiastes 11:1
"Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days" (Ecclesiastes 11:1).

**Printing**:

- Get **quotes** from several printers; compare paper, binding, cover finish and delivery.
- Request a **proof copy** and check it carefully before the full print run.
- **Print-on-demand** services (e.g. Amazon KDP, IngramSpark) print copies as they're ordered, including internationally.
- Start with a **modest print run** you can realistically sell; "count the cost" (Luke 14:28).

**Ebooks and audiobooks**: publish on Amazon Kindle, Google Play Books, Apple Books and African platforms (e.g. Okadabooks). Format as EPUB.

**Launch and marketing**:

- Build interest before launch: share the journey, a cover reveal, sample chapters.
- Plan a **launch event** (church, campus, bookstore, online).
- Ask early readers for **honest reviews**.
- Use your **platforms**: social media, newsletter, speaking engagements, ministries.
- Place books in **bookshops**, churches and conferences; set sensible wholesale prices.
- Keep records of sales and stock.

Above all, pray that the book will reach the readers God intends.
