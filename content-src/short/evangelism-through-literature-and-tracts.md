# The power of the written word | Literature in God's mission.
## God's word in writing | John 20:30-31; Colossians 4:16; Acts 8:27-35
"These are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name" (John 20:31). John's Gospel is itself an evangelistic book.

Paul's letters were read and passed between churches (Colossians 4:16). The Ethiopian official was reading Isaiah in his chariot when Philip met him (Acts 8:28): a written text prepared his heart.

Why literature works:

- It **reaches** where preachers cannot: homes, offices, villages, prisons, other cities.
- It **stays**: people can read it again, privately, at their own pace.
- It **multiplies**: one copy can be read by many and passed on.
- It **crosses barriers**: shy people and busy people may read what they would not hear.
- It **opens conversations**.

Many believers in Nigeria first heard the gospel through a tract handed to them in a bus, market or hospital. A small paper can carry eternal truth.
## Kinds of gospel literature | Habakkuk 2:2; Isaiah 55:10-11
"So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void" (Isaiah 55:11).

Types:

- **Tracts**: short (one sheet or a small folded leaflet) with a single gospel message.
- **Gospel portions**: a Gospel of John or Mark, often with reading notes.
- **Booklets**: for new believers (assurance, prayer, Bible reading) or for seekers (answers to questions).
- **Testimony leaflets**: real stories of changed lives (with consent).
- **Magazines and newsletters** with gospel content.
- **Children's literature**: illustrated Bible stories.
- **Digital literature**: PDFs, e-tracts and shareable images for WhatsApp and social media.

Choose literature for the **audience**: language, reading level, faith background and age.

# Writing a good tract | Clear, true and attractive.
## Content of a tract | 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; Romans 10:9-13; 2 Corinthians 5:20
A good tract has one clear purpose: to help a reader understand the gospel and respond.

Structure (300 to 500 words):

1. **Title and hook**: a question, story or need the reader feels ("Are you tired of carrying guilt?").
2. **The problem**: sin separates us from God (Romans 3:23).
3. **God's love and Christ's work**: Christ died for our sins and rose again (1 Corinthians 15:3-4; Romans 5:8).
4. **The response**: repent and believe; confess Jesus as Lord (Romans 10:9-10).
5. **A simple prayer** the reader can pray.
6. **Assurance**: a promise (John 1:12; 1 John 5:12).
7. **Next steps**: read the Bible (start with John), pray, find a Bible-believing church.
8. **Contact**: phone, WhatsApp, email or address for follow-up.

Write plainly, kindly and truthfully. Use Scripture accurately with references. Paul's spirit is right: "we pray you in Christ's stead, be ye reconciled to God" (2 Corinthians 5:20).
## Design, language and printing | Proverbs 25:11; Acts 2:8-11
"How hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?" (Acts 2:8).

**Language**: produce tracts in English and in local languages (Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Pidgin and others). Use fluent speakers to translate and check.

**Design**:

- An attractive cover that invites reading.
- Large, readable font; short paragraphs; plenty of white space.
- Relevant, respectful images (with permission or licences).
- Your ministry name and contact details clearly shown.
- A QR code or link for digital follow-up.

**Printing**: get quotes; choose sizes that fit pockets; print a test batch; proofread carefully. Check facts and references.

**Copyright**: write your own text, or get permission to reprint others' tracts. Many ministries freely permit reprints; ask.

# Distribution and follow-up | Getting literature into hands and connecting readers.
## Distributing wisely | Ecclesiastes 11:1, 6; Colossians 4:5-6; Romans 10:14-15
"Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days... In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thine hand" (Ecclesiastes 11:1, 6).

Where to distribute:

- Personal encounters: give a tract after a conversation.
- Markets, motor parks, bus stops (with permission where needed).
- Hospitals (visit and pray, with permission), prisons (through chaplaincy).
- Door to door, in teams.
- Campuses and schools (within rules).
- Events: crusades, concerts, outreaches.
- With purchases or services, for Christian businesses.
- Digitally: WhatsApp status, social media, email.

How to distribute:

- **Smile, greet, and offer**: "Good morning, this is for you. It's good news."
- **Don't force** it; respect refusals.
- **Don't litter**: avoid leaving piles in public places or on cars where they become rubbish.
- **Pray** for every person who receives one.
- **Keep some with you** always.
## Literature with personal witness and follow-up | Acts 8:30-35; 2 Timothy 4:13; Philemon 1:6
Philip asked the Ethiopian, "Understandest thou what thou readest?" and then explained (Acts 8:30-35). Literature works best with **conversation**.

Using tracts as conversation starters:

- "Have you ever thought about this question?" (pointing to the title).
- "Can I show you the main idea in one minute?"
- After someone reads: "What did you think?"

**Follow-up**:

- Include a **clear contact** on every piece.
- Have a **team ready** to answer calls and messages promptly.
- Offer a **free Gospel of John** or a Bible study booklet.
- Connect respondents with **local churches** or STRONGS Bible Clubs.
- **Record** responses (with consent) to follow up.

Even Paul valued books: "bring with thee... the books, but especially the parchments" (2 Timothy 4:13). Faithful literature, faithfully distributed and followed up, keeps bearing fruit.

@assignment Practical assignment: write and distribute a tract
1. Write an original gospel tract (300 to 500 words) in English or a local language, following the structure in Module 2, with Scripture references and a contact for follow-up.
2. Design it (a simple layout is fine) and print or share digitally at least 20 copies.
3. Distribute them over two weeks, using at least five as conversation starters.
4. Hand in the tract and a 250 to 400 word report: where and how you distributed, conversations that followed, responses, and follow-up arranged.
