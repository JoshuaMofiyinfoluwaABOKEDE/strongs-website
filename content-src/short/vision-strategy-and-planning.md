# Vision | Seeing what God wants to do.
## Receiving vision | Habakkuk 2:1-3; Nehemiah 1:1-11; Proverbs 29:18
Habakkuk said, "I will stand upon my watch... and will watch to see what he will say unto me" (Habakkuk 2:1). God answered, "Write the vision, and make it plain... though it tarry, wait for it" (verses 2-3).

Nehemiah's vision began with a **burden**: hearing that the wall was broken, "I sat down and wept, and mourned certain days, and fasted, and prayed" (Nehemiah 1:4).

Vision is a **clear picture of a preferred future** that God wants to bring about through you. It usually grows from:

- **Burden**: what breaks your heart?
- **Scripture**: what does God care about?
- **Prayer**: waiting to hear Him.
- **Gifts and opportunities** God has given.
- **Counsel** from mature believers.

"Where there is no vision, the people perish" (Proverbs 29:18): they become unrestrained and scattered. Vision gives direction and energy.
## Mission and values | Matthew 28:18-20; Acts 1:8; Micah 6:8
The church's **mission** is given by Christ: make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19-20), as witnesses "in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth" (Acts 1:8). Each ministry expresses that mission in its own context.

Three statements:

- **Vision**: where we are going (the future). "A generation of Nigerian students who know Christ and transform their world."
- **Mission**: what we do to get there (purpose). "We win, build and send students through Bible clubs, outreach and training."
- **Values**: how we behave on the way (character). E.g. Scripture, prayer, integrity, excellence, love, service.

Good statements are **short, clear, memorable and used**: they guide decisions, budgets and programmes. If a proposed activity doesn't serve your mission, consider saying no.

# Strategy | Choosing the best path.
## Assessing the situation | Nehemiah 2:11-16; Luke 14:28-32; Proverbs 27:23
Before sharing his vision, Nehemiah inspected the walls by night, "and viewed the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down" (Nehemiah 2:13). He assessed reality first.

Jesus spoke of a king who "sitteth not down first, and consulteth whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that cometh against him with twenty thousand" (Luke 14:31).

A **SWOT analysis** helps:

- **Strengths** (internal): what we do well; resources and people.
- **Weaknesses** (internal): what limits us.
- **Opportunities** (external): open doors, needs, trends.
- **Threats** (external): risks, opposition, challenges.

Gather facts: attendance trends, finances, member surveys, community needs, feedback. Be honest; wishful thinking leads to poor plans.
## Choosing priorities | Acts 6:2-4; Acts 16:6-10; Philippians 3:13-14
The apostles chose priorities: "It is not reason that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables... we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word" (Acts 6:2, 4). Paul focused: "this one thing I do... I press toward the mark" (Philippians 3:13-14). And he remained open to the Spirit redirecting him to Macedonia (Acts 16:6-10).

**Strategy** is choosing the few priorities that will most advance the mission, given your situation.

Steps:

1. From the SWOT, list possible strategic directions.
2. Pray and discuss: which will make the biggest difference?
3. Choose **three to five priorities** for the next one to three years (e.g. strengthen discipleship groups; launch campus outreach in two new schools; train 20 new leaders).
4. Decide what you will **stop** doing to make room.
5. Hold plans humbly: "ye ought to say, If the Lord will" (James 4:15).

# Planning and review | From priorities to action.
## Goals and action plans | Nehemiah 3:1-32; Proverbs 21:5; Proverbs 16:3, 9
Nehemiah 3 is an action plan: who builds which section, next to whom. "The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness" (Proverbs 21:5). "A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps" (Proverbs 16:9).

Turn each priority into **SMART goals** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound): "By December, start 10 new discipleship groups with trained leaders."

For each goal, an **action plan**:

| Action | Who | By when | Resources | Done? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Select and train 10 group leaders | Discipleship coordinator | March | ₦50,000, venue | |
| Launch groups | Group leaders | April | Study guides | |

Then a **budget** aligned with priorities and a **calendar** for the year.

Communicate the plan: people support what they understand.
## Monitoring, review and adjusting | Nehemiah 6:15-16; Acts 14:26-28; Revelation 2:1-5
The wall was finished in 52 days, and even enemies "perceived that this work was wrought of our God" (Nehemiah 6:15-16). Paul and Barnabas returned to Antioch and reported "all that God had done with them" (Acts 14:27).

Jesus reviewed the church at Ephesus: commending their works but warning, "thou hast left thy first love... repent, and do the first works" (Revelation 2:4-5). Review includes spiritual health, not only activity.

Review rhythm:

- **Monthly**: check action plans; solve problems.
- **Quarterly**: review progress on goals; adjust plans.
- **Annually**: evaluate results against vision and mission; celebrate; set new goals.

Review questions:

- What did we achieve? What didn't we, and why?
- What did we learn?
- Is our spiritual life healthy?
- What should we start, stop or continue?
- Where is God leading next?

@assignment Practical assignment: a strategic plan
For a ministry, department or fellowship you serve, write a short strategic plan (800 to 1,200 words):

1. Vision, mission and values statements.
2. A SWOT analysis with evidence.
3. Three strategic priorities for the next one to two years, and one thing you will stop doing.
4. SMART goals and an action plan table for one priority.
5. A review rhythm and the questions you will use.
