# Set apart for God | What consecration means.
## Wholly the Lord's | Romans 12:1-2; Leviticus 8:22-24; 2 Timothy 2:20-21
At the consecration of Aaron's sons, the blood of the ram was put on the right ear, the right thumb and the great toe of the right foot (Leviticus 8:23-24): ears to hear God, hands to serve Him, feet to walk in His ways. The whole person was set apart.

To **consecrate** means to **set apart for God's exclusive use**. In the New Testament every believer is called to this: "present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service" (Romans 12:1).

Paul pictures a great house with many vessels. "If a man therefore purge himself from these, he shall be a vessel unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master's use, and prepared unto every good work" (2 Timothy 2:21). God uses clean, available vessels, not necessarily gold ones.

Gbile Akanni's teaching has often called believers to a consecration that is total, not partial: all of life laid on the altar, not merely Sundays or spare time.
## The cost and the joy | Luke 9:23-25; Philippians 3:7-10; Matthew 13:44
"If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me" (Luke 9:23). Consecration costs: plans, comfort, reputation, sometimes relationships and money.

Paul counted all his former advantages as "loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord" (Philippians 3:8). In Jesus' parable, a man found treasure in a field, "and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field" (Matthew 13:44). The cost is real, but the treasure is greater.

Consecration is not a one-time emotional moment only. It is a decision renewed daily: "take up his cross **daily**". Many have consecrated themselves at a camp or convention and then slowly taken back what they gave. Keep returning to the altar.

# The servant heart | Learning from Christ the Servant.
## The mind of Christ | Philippians 2:5-8; Isaiah 42:1-4; Matthew 20:28
"Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: Who, being in the form of God... made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant" (Philippians 2:5-7). Isaiah prophesied the Servant who "shall not cry, nor lift up... A bruised reed shall he not break" (Isaiah 42:2-3).

"The Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many" (Matthew 20:28).

Marks of a servant heart:

- **Humility**: no task is beneath you.
- **Availability**: ready when needed.
- **Gentleness** with the weak and bruised.
- **Faithfulness** in small, unseen duties.
- **Joy** in others' success.
- **Hiddenness**: content to be unknown.

A servant's question is not "What is my position?" but "What does my Master want done?"
## Serving with right motives | Matthew 6:1-4; Galatians 1:10; 1 Corinthians 4:5
Jesus warned: "Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them: otherwise ye have no reward of your Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 6:1).

Wrong motives in service:

- **Recognition**: wanting to be seen and praised.
- **Position**: using service as a ladder to titles.
- **Money**: serving for material gain (1 Peter 5:2, "not for filthy lucre").
- **Guilt or fear** rather than love.
- **Competition** with other workers.

Right motives: love for God, love for people, gratitude for grace, and obedience.

"Judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who... will make manifest the counsels of the hearts" (1 Corinthians 4:5). God sees motives. Examine yours regularly: Would I still serve if no one thanked me? If someone else got the credit?

# Living consecrated | Daily surrender in ministry and life.
## Areas of consecration | 1 Corinthians 6:19-20; Colossians 3:17; Malachi 3:8-10
"Ye are not your own... glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's" (1 Corinthians 6:19-20).

Consecration reaches every area:

- **Body**: purity, health, rest; avoiding what defiles.
- **Mind**: what you watch, read and think about (Philippians 4:8).
- **Time**: priorities, Sabbath, time for God and people.
- **Money**: giving, contentment, honesty (Malachi 3:10).
- **Relationships**: friendships, courtship and marriage under God.
- **Gifts and career**: used for His glory (Colossians 3:17).
- **Ambitions**: surrendered to His will.
- **Possessions**: held loosely, available to God.

Partial consecration leaves corners of life where self still reigns. Ask God to show you any area you have withheld.
## Faithful to the end | Revelation 2:10; Luke 17:7-10; Matthew 25:21
Jesus told a parable of a servant who comes in from the field and still serves his master's meal. "So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all those things which are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do" (Luke 17:10).

Servants don't serve for applause. Yet the Master Himself will reward: "Well done, thou good and faithful servant... enter thou into the joy of thy lord" (Matthew 25:21). "Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life" (Revelation 2:10).

Staying consecrated:

- Begin each day by offering yourself to God.
- Confess quickly when you take back control.
- Keep company with consecrated believers.
- Remember the cross: Christ gave everything for you.
- Look to the reward and the Master's joy.

@assignment Practical assignment: an act of consecration
Write a personal act of consecration (400 to 600 words), covering each area in Module 3 (body, mind, time, money, relationships, gifts, ambitions, possessions). For each, name honestly where you have held back and what you now surrender.

Then choose one hidden act of service you will do weekly for a month without telling anyone what you are doing. Hand in a 150 to 250 word reflection on how it shaped your motives (without boasting about what you did).
