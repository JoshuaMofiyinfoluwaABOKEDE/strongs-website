# Power for witness | The Spirit and the mission.
## Wait for power | Luke 24:49; Acts 1:4-8; Acts 2:1-4
After His resurrection Jesus told the disciples, "tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high" (Luke 24:49). "Ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me" (Acts 1:8).

Notice the purpose of the power: **witness**. The Spirit's coming at Pentecost (Acts 2:1-4) turned fearful disciples into bold preachers. Peter, who had denied Jesus to a servant girl, stood before thousands and proclaimed Christ.

The Spirit's work in evangelism:

- **Empowers** witnesses with boldness (Acts 4:31).
- **Convicts** the world "of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment" (John 16:8).
- **Glorifies Christ** (John 16:14).
- **Guides** to people and places (Acts 8:29; 16:6-10).
- **Opens hearts** (Acts 16:14).
- **Confirms the Word** with signs (Mark 16:20; Hebrews 2:4).

Reinhard Bonnke often stressed that evangelism without the Holy Spirit is like a car without fuel: the machinery is there but nothing moves.
## Being filled and walking in the Spirit | Ephesians 5:18; Acts 4:29-31; Galatians 5:16, 22-25
"Be filled with the Spirit" (Ephesians 5:18) is a continuing command. In Acts, the same disciples were filled again when they prayed for boldness: "they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness" (Acts 4:31).

Christians differ on some details of the Spirit's baptism and gifts, but all agree that believers must depend on the Holy Spirit and be filled with Him for effective witness.

How to walk in the Spirit's power:

- **Ask**: "how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?" (Luke 11:13).
- **Yield**: surrender areas of life to His control.
- **Obey** promptings to speak and serve.
- **Pray** for boldness, as the early church did (Acts 4:29).
- **Feed** on the Word.
- **Bear fruit**: love, joy, peace... (Galatians 5:22-23). Power without love is noise (1 Corinthians 13:1-2).

The Spirit-filled witness is marked by both **boldness** and **love**.

# Spirit-led encounters | Guidance, boldness and the needs of people.
## Led by the Spirit | Acts 8:26-40; Acts 10:19-20; Acts 16:6-10
"The Spirit said unto Philip, Go near, and join thyself to this chariot" (Acts 8:29). Philip met an Ethiopian official reading Isaiah, explained the gospel from that very passage, and baptised him.

The Spirit told Peter to go with Cornelius' messengers "doubting nothing" (Acts 10:20), opening the door to the Gentiles. The Spirit stopped Paul from preaching in Asia and Bithynia and led him to Macedonia (Acts 16:6-10).

Practising Spirit-led witness:

- **Pray each day** for divine appointments.
- **Notice people**: who seems open, troubled or searching?
- **Respond to gentle promptings** to speak, help or pray, testing them by Scripture and wisdom.
- **Start where people are**, as Philip did with the Ethiopian's reading.
- **Accept closed doors** too; the Spirit sometimes says "not here".

Guidance is not a substitute for faithfulness. Most witnessing happens in ordinary relationships as we go.
## Praying for the sick and needy | James 5:14-16; Acts 3:1-10; Matthew 9:35-36
Peter told the lame man, "Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk" (Acts 3:6). The healing opened a door to preach Christ (Acts 3:12-26).

Jesus healed out of **compassion** (Matthew 9:36), and healing often accompanied the preaching of the kingdom.

Praying for the sick on outreach:

- **Ask permission**: "Can I pray for you?"
- **Ask about the need** briefly.
- **Pray simply** in Jesus' name, with faith and love; a hand on the shoulder only with permission.
- **Ask afterwards** how they are, without pressure.
- **Point to Jesus**, whether or not healing is seen.
- **Never** tell people to stop medicine, never promise healing, never blame the sick for lack of faith, and never charge money.
- **Report honestly**; don't exaggerate.

God heals in many ways, including through medicine and over time. Our part is to pray in faith and love; the results are His.

# The cross at the centre | Power that serves the gospel.
## Power and the message | 1 Corinthians 1:17-18; 1 Corinthians 2:1-5; Acts 8:18-23
"The preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God" (1 Corinthians 1:18). Paul's preaching came "in demonstration of the Spirit and of power: That your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God" (1 Corinthians 2:4-5).

Signs and wonders are never the message; they **point to** the message. In Samaria, when Simon tried to buy the Spirit's power, Peter rebuked him: "Thy money perish with thee... thy heart is not right in the sight of God" (Acts 8:20-21).

Keep the cross central:

- Preach **sin, the cross, the resurrection and repentance** every time.
- Give glory to **Jesus**, not to the minister.
- Value **conversions** above sensational experiences.
- Make **disciples**, not fans of miracles.
## Integrity in Spirit-empowered ministry | Matthew 7:21-23; 2 Corinthians 4:2; 1 John 4:1
Jesus warned that some would say, "have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works?", yet He would answer, "I never knew you" (Matthew 7:22-23). Gifts are not proof of godliness.

"Believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God" (1 John 4:1).

Guard against:

- **Staged or exaggerated** miracles and testimonies.
- **Manipulation**: pushing people over, emotional pressure, long repeated appeals.
- **Money for ministry**: selling anointing oil, "prayer seeds" or special blessings.
- **Fear-based teaching** that makes people dependent on the minister.
- **Pride** in gifts.

Walk "not in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully" (2 Corinthians 4:2). Real power and real integrity belong together.

@assignment Practical assignment: Spirit-led witness journal
For three weeks, pray daily for boldness and divine appointments, and keep a witness journal.

Hand in (400 to 600 words): the conversations you had and how they began, any promptings you followed or didn't, any times you prayed for someone's need (with how you asked and what happened, honestly reported), what you learned about depending on the Holy Spirit, and how you kept the cross at the centre.
