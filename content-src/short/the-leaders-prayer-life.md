# Prayer as the leader's first work | Why leaders must pray.
## Leaders who prayed | 1 Samuel 12:23; Acts 6:4; Luke 6:12-13
Samuel told Israel, "God forbid that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you" (1 Samuel 12:23). For Samuel, prayerlessness was sin.

When the church grew and needs multiplied, the apostles said, "we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word" (Acts 6:4). Prayer was listed **first**.

Before choosing the twelve apostles, Jesus "went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God. And when it was day... he chose twelve" (Luke 6:12-13). His most important leadership decision was born in prayer.

Sanders observed that the leader must lead in prayer above all else, because a leader can take people only as far as he has gone with God. Prayer is not a preliminary to the work; it **is** the work.
## Prayer and spiritual authority | Exodus 17:8-13; Exodus 32:11-14; James 5:16
When Israel fought Amalek, "when Moses held up his hand... Israel prevailed: and when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed" (Exodus 17:11). Aaron and Hur held up his hands until the sun went down. Victory in the valley depended on prayer on the hill.

After the golden calf, Moses pleaded for the people, and "the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people" (Exodus 32:14). His intercession saved a nation.

"The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much" (James 5:16). Spiritual authority comes not from titles but from a life of communion with God.

Note also: Moses needed **Aaron and Hur**. Leaders need others to pray with and for them. Build a team that holds up your hands.

# Praying for people and decisions | Intercession and guidance.
## Praying for the people you lead | Ephesians 1:15-19; Colossians 1:9-12; Philippians 1:3-11
Paul's letters are full of prayers for the churches. They show what to ask for people:

- That they would **know God** better (Ephesians 1:17).
- That their understanding would be **enlightened** to know their hope, inheritance and His power (Ephesians 1:18-19).
- That they would be **filled with the knowledge of his will** and walk worthy, fruitful and strong (Colossians 1:9-11).
- That their **love would abound** in knowledge and judgment (Philippians 1:9).
- With **thanksgiving** for them (Philippians 1:3).

Notice: Paul prayed mostly for **spiritual growth**, not only for problems to disappear.

Practical habits:

- Keep a list of the people you lead and pray for a few by name each day.
- Use Paul's prayers, inserting names.
- Pray for specific needs people share, and follow up.
- Thank God for them; it changes how you see them.
## Seeking guidance in prayer | Proverbs 3:5-6; Acts 13:1-3; Nehemiah 2:4-5
In Antioch, as the leaders "ministered to the Lord, and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul" (Acts 13:2). Guidance came while they were worshipping and fasting. Even then, "when they had fasted and prayed", they laid hands on them (verse 3).

When Artaxerxes asked Nehemiah, "For what dost thou make request?", Nehemiah says, "So I prayed to the God of heaven. And I said unto the king..." (Nehemiah 2:4-5). An arrow prayer in the moment came from months of prior prayer (Nehemiah 1).

Seeking guidance:

- **Pray before deciding**, not after.
- **Search the Scriptures** for principles.
- **Seek counsel** from godly people (Proverbs 15:22).
- **Wait** for peace and clarity; avoid rushing when pressured.
- **Pray together** with your team on major decisions.
- **Act in faith**, and keep praying as you go.

# Building a prayer rhythm | Personal and team practice.
## Personal rhythm | Daniel 6:10; Psalm 55:17; Luke 5:16
"Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray, and cry aloud: and he shall hear my voice" (Psalm 55:17). Daniel kept his rhythm of prayer three times a day "as he did aforetime" (Daniel 6:10). Even as crowds grew, Jesus "withdrew himself into the wilderness, and prayed" (Luke 5:16).

Building a rhythm:

- **Daily time**: a fixed, protected time and place. Early morning works for many leaders before demands begin.
- **Structure**: worship, confession, thanksgiving, the Word, intercession, listening.
- **Prayer list**: family, people you lead, leaders, ministry needs, the lost, the nation.
- **Weekly**: a longer time, perhaps with fasting.
- **Retreats**: half-days or days of prayer several times a year.
- **Arrow prayers** throughout the day.
- **Journal**: record requests and answers.

Spurgeon urged his students to pray constantly, in their study and as they preached. Prayer should run through the whole day, not only the morning.
## Team and church prayer | Acts 1:14; Acts 4:23-31; Acts 12:5
The early church was born in prayer: "These all continued with one accord in prayer" (Acts 1:14). When threatened, they "lifted up their voice to God with one accord", and "the place was shaken... and they spake the word of God with boldness" (Acts 4:24, 31). When Peter was in prison, "prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him" (Acts 12:5).

Leading a team in prayer:

- **Begin every meeting with real prayer**, not a quick formality.
- **Hold regular team prayer times** (weekly or monthly) focused only on prayer.
- **Pray Scripture together**, as in Acts 4:24-30 (they prayed Psalm 2).
- **Share requests and answers** to build faith.
- **Mobilise intercessors** for outreaches, crusades and key decisions.
- **Model it**: people pray when leaders pray.

A praying team is a powerful team.

@assignment Practical assignment: a prayer plan for leaders
Write and follow for three weeks a prayer plan (400 to 600 words):

1. Your daily prayer time, place and structure.
2. A prayer list of the people you lead, with one of Paul's prayers you will pray for them.
3. One decision you are facing and how you will seek God's guidance.
4. A plan for team prayer in your ministry or fellowship.

At the end, add a 200-word reflection with any answers to prayer and changes you noticed.
