# Drama and the gospel | Why and how the church uses drama.
## Showing the truth | Ezekiel 4:1-3; Jeremiah 13:1-11; 2 Samuel 12:1-7
God sometimes told prophets to **act out** His message. Ezekiel drew Jerusalem on a tile and laid siege to it as "a sign to the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 4:1-3). Jeremiah buried a linen girdle and dug it up ruined to show Judah's pride (Jeremiah 13:1-11). Nathan's story made David see his sin (2 Samuel 12). Jesus told parables full of characters and drama.

Drama works because people **see** truth in action. It can:

- Draw crowds at outreaches who would not enter a church.
- Show the consequences of sin and the hope of the gospel.
- Make Bible stories come alive for children and youth.
- Raise questions before a preacher answers them.

Drama is a servant of the message, not a replacement for it. Usually it prepares hearts for a clear gospel word or appeal.
## Kinds of gospel drama | Mark 4:33-34; 1 Corinthians 9:22
Jesus spoke "with many such parables... as they were able to hear it" (Mark 4:33). Choose the form that fits your audience.

- **Short sketch** (3 to 10 minutes): one situation, one point. Good for services and outreaches.
- **Mime**: no words, movement to music. Crosses language barriers; powerful at crusades.
- **Street drama**: loud, simple, energetic, to gather a crowd in a market or motor park.
- **Bible drama**: acting a Bible story faithfully.
- **Full play** (30 to 90 minutes): for Easter, Christmas, campus programmes or evangelistic nights.
- **Dance drama**: movement and music telling a story.

Nigeria has a rich tradition of Christian drama on stage and film. Learn from it, and aim for excellence: poor drama can distract from the gospel, while good drama opens hearts.

# Script writing | Writing short, clear drama scripts.
## Structure of a script | Luke 15:11-32; Genesis 37:3-4
The parable of the prodigal son (Luke 15:11-32) is a perfect short drama: a clear **character** (the younger son), a **desire** (freedom and money), **conflict** (famine, hunger, shame), a **turning point** ("he came to himself"), and a **resolution** (the father's embrace), plus a twist (the angry older brother).

A short script structure:

1. **Opening**: set the scene quickly. Who, where, what's normal?
2. **Inciting incident**: something happens that creates a problem.
3. **Rising conflict**: things get harder; choices matter.
4. **Climax/turning point**: the moment of decision or revelation.
5. **Resolution**: what happens as a result, pointing to the message.

Keep it focused: **one main message**, few characters (two to six), one or two locations.
## Writing dialogue and format | Proverbs 15:23; Colossians 4:6
"A word spoken in due season, how good is it!" (Proverbs 15:23).

Dialogue tips:

- **Make it sound real**: people speak in short sentences, interrupt, and use everyday language (Pidgin or local languages where appropriate).
- **Show character** through what people say and how.
- **Avoid preaching** in dialogue. Let the story make the point; a character can speak truth naturally at the climax.
- **Use humour** wisely: it relaxes the audience and makes serious moments stronger.
- **Cut** anything that doesn't move the story.

Script format:

```
SCENE 1. A BUS STOP. EVENING.
(TUNDE paces, checking his phone.)
TUNDE: Where is this man? I can't be late again.
(MAMA BOLA enters with a heavy basket.)
```

Character names in capitals, directions in brackets. Number scenes and pages. Read the script aloud with others before rehearsal.

# Acting | Voice, movement and character.
## Voice and body | Nehemiah 8:3-8; 1 Corinthians 14:8
When Ezra read the law, "the ears of all the people were attentive" (Nehemiah 8:3). Actors must be heard and understood.

**Voice**:

- **Project**: speak from the breath, aiming your voice at the back row, without shouting.
- **Articulate**: clear consonants, especially outdoors.
- **Vary**: pitch, pace and volume show emotion.
- **Pause**: silence before an important line draws attention.

**Body**:

- **Face the audience** ("cheat out"): even when talking to another actor, angle your body toward the audience.
- **Move with purpose**: every movement should mean something.
- **Use your whole body** to show emotion: posture, gestures, facial expression.
- **Stay in character** even when not speaking; react to others.

Warm up voice and body before every rehearsal and performance.
## Building a character | 1 Samuel 16:7; Hebrews 4:15
Good actors understand **who** their character is and **why** they act as they do.

Ask about your character:

- What do they **want** in this scene?
- What **stops** them getting it?
- What do they **believe** about God, themselves and others?
- How do they **walk, talk and dress**?
- How do they **change** by the end?

Even villains and sinners should be played truthfully, not as cartoons. Audiences recognise themselves in honest characters.

Christian actors must keep **integrity**:

- Do not act out sin in ways that cause stumbling (sexual content, real drunkenness, vulgar language). Suggest it instead.
- Separate role from life: playing a thief doesn't make you one, but guard your heart.
- Pray before performances; you are ministering.

# Staging | Rehearsing and presenting a production.
## Rehearsals and roles | 1 Corinthians 14:40; Nehemiah 4:6
"So built we the wall... for the people had a mind to work" (Nehemiah 4:6). A production is teamwork.

Key roles:

- **Director**: shapes the whole production, guides actors.
- **Stage manager**: runs rehearsals and the show, keeps time, manages cues.
- **Actors**.
- **Costumes, props and set**.
- **Sound and music**, **lighting** (if available).

Rehearsal plan for a short drama:

1. **Read-through**: everyone reads the script together.
2. **Blocking**: decide where actors stand and move (write it in scripts).
3. **Scene work**: rehearse scene by scene for meaning and emotion.
4. **Off-book**: lines learned by a set date.
5. **Run-throughs**: the whole piece without stopping.
6. **Technical rehearsal**: with sound, props and costumes.
7. **Dress rehearsal**: exactly like the performance.

Pray at every rehearsal. Unity in the team matters more than a perfect show.
## Performing outdoors and safely | Proverbs 22:3; Acts 19:23-41
Much gospel drama is performed outdoors: crusades, markets, campuses, villages.

Practical staging:

- **Simple sets and props** that are easy to carry.
- **Bold costumes** that show character at a distance.
- **Sound**: use microphones if possible; otherwise strong projection and fewer, louder lines.
- **Sightlines**: make sure the audience can see; use a raised area or form a circle.
- **Music** to gather a crowd and to cover scene changes.

Safety:

- **Get permission** from local authorities, market leaders or school management.
- **Know the area**: avoid conflict zones; have a team leader watching the crowd.
- **Avoid realistic weapons** or anything that could cause panic.
- **Plan for heat and rain**: water, shade, covers for equipment.
- **Have a follow-up team** ready for those who respond.

"A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself" (Proverbs 22:3). Wise planning protects people and the message.
