# The biblical pattern | God's work passes from one generation to the next.
## Fathers and sons in the work | Numbers 27:15-23; 2 Kings 2:1-15; 2 Timothy 2:1-2
Moses asked God to "set a man over the congregation... that the congregation of the LORD be not as sheep which have no shepherd" (Numbers 27:16-17). God chose Joshua, and Moses laid hands on him before the people.

Elisha followed Elijah faithfully, refusing to leave him ("As the LORD liveth... I will not leave thee", 2 Kings 2:2), and received a double portion of his spirit.

Paul called Timothy "my own son in the faith" (1 Timothy 1:2) and told him to commit the truth "to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2).

These were **relationships**, not programmes. Spiritual fathers and mothers invested time, example, correction and blessing.

Sanders taught that the leader who fails to train successors has failed in part of his task. Gbile Akanni's ministry has given special attention to raising young people who carry God's burden with integrity.
## Jesus and the twelve | Mark 3:13-15; John 17:6-20; Matthew 10:1-8
Jesus "ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach, And to have power" (Mark 3:14-15). In three years He shaped ordinary fishermen and a tax collector into apostles who turned the world upside down.

His method:

- **Selection**: He prayed and chose a few.
- **Association**: they lived with Him.
- **Instruction**: He taught them privately as well as publicly.
- **Demonstration**: they saw Him pray, heal, preach and serve.
- **Delegation**: He sent them out two by two (Matthew 10).
- **Supervision**: they reported back, and He corrected them (Luke 10:17-20).
- **Reproduction**: He commissioned them to make disciples (Matthew 28:19-20).

In His prayer in John 17, Jesus said, "I have given them the words which thou gavest me" (verse 8), and He prayed "for them also which shall believe on me through their word" (verse 20): He thought generations ahead.

# Spotting and training | Identifying and developing faithful people.
## Whom to invest in | 2 Timothy 2:2; Acts 16:1-3; Luke 16:10
Paul chose Timothy because he "was well reported of by the brethren" (Acts 16:2). Paul told Timothy to look for **faithful** people (2 Timothy 2:2).

Qualities to look for (often summarised as **FAT**):

- **Faithful**: they keep commitments and serve in small things (Luke 16:10).
- **Available**: they make time to learn and serve.
- **Teachable**: they receive correction and want to grow.

Also look for:

- **Hunger for God**: they pray and love the Word.
- **Good report** from others.
- **Servant heart**: they do unglamorous tasks willingly.
- **Some gifting** that can be developed.

Don't choose only by talent, popularity or friendship. Gifted but unfaithful people often cause damage. Faithful people with modest gifts often grow into great servants.

Pray, as Jesus did, before choosing.
## Life-on-life training | 1 Thessalonians 2:7-12; Philippians 4:9; Acts 20:17-21
Paul reminded the Thessalonians how he lived among them "as a father doth his children", sharing "not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls" (1 Thessalonians 2:8, 11). "Those things, which ye have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do" (Philippians 4:9).

Training methods:

- **Be together**: take emerging leaders with you to visits, meetings, outreaches and prayer.
- **Teach** them the Word and principles of ministry.
- **Model**: let them watch you serve, decide and handle problems.
- **Give real responsibilities**: start small, then increase.
- **Debrief**: after each task, ask what went well, what was hard, what they learned.
- **Correct privately and lovingly**.
- **Pray** with and for them.
- **Read together**: good books and biographies.

A useful pattern: *I do, you watch; I do, you help; you do, I help; you do, I watch; you do, someone else watches.*

# Releasing and supporting | Letting go and standing behind.
## Releasing others | Deuteronomy 31:7-8; Deuteronomy 34:9; John 3:30
Moses called Joshua "in the sight of all Israel" and said, "Be strong and of a good courage... the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee" (Deuteronomy 31:7-8). After Moses' death, "Joshua... was full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands upon him: and the children of Israel hearkened unto him" (Deuteronomy 34:9).

John the Baptist said of Jesus, "He must increase, but I must decrease" (John 3:30).

Releasing well means:

- **Public affirmation** so people accept the new leader.
- **Clear handover**: responsibilities, authority, information and relationships.
- **Letting them lead differently**: they won't do everything your way.
- **Not taking back control** at the first mistake.
- **Rejoicing** when they surpass you.

Insecure leaders hold on; secure leaders release.
## Supporting emerging leaders | 2 Timothy 1:6-7; Acts 15:36-41; 2 Timothy 4:11
Paul kept writing to Timothy after releasing him: "stir up the gift of God, which is in thee... For God hath not given us the spirit of fear" (2 Timothy 1:6-7).

Paul and Barnabas disagreed about John Mark, who had left them (Acts 15:37-39). Barnabas gave Mark a second chance. Years later Paul wrote, "Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry" (2 Timothy 4:11). Emerging leaders who fail can be restored.

Ongoing support:

- **Regular check-ins** and availability.
- **Encouragement** in discouragement.
- **Honest counsel** without interference.
- **Prayer** for them by name.
- **Second chances** after genuine repentance.
- **Connection** to other mentors and peers.

The fruit of leadership is seen in the second and third generations.

@assignment Practical assignment: a leader development plan
Identify one to three people you could help raise (or are already raising). Write a 500 to 700 word plan:

1. Why you chose them, using the FAT criteria and other qualities.
2. A six-month training pathway: time together, teaching topics, books, and real responsibilities that increase step by step.
3. How you will debrief, correct and encourage.
4. How and when you will release responsibilities, and how you will keep supporting them.

Begin the plan and add a 200-word report after one month.
