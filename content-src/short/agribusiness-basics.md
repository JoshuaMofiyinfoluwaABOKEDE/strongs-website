# Opportunities in agriculture | Seeing farming as a calling and a business.
## God the first farmer | Genesis 2:8-15; Genesis 8:22; Proverbs 12:11
"The LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden" and put man there "to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:8, 15). Farming is one of the oldest callings. God promised, "While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest... shall not cease" (Genesis 8:22).

"He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread" (Proverbs 12:11). Agriculture feeds nations, employs millions of Nigerians, and offers real opportunities for young people with skill and diligence.

**Agribusiness** means farming and the businesses around it as a profitable enterprise, not only subsistence. The **value chain** includes:

- **Inputs**: seeds, seedlings, feed, fertiliser, tools.
- **Production**: crops, poultry, fish, livestock, snails, bees, mushrooms.
- **Processing**: garri, palm oil, smoked fish, plantain chips, packaged pepper, feed milling.
- **Storage and transport**.
- **Marketing and sales**: markets, shops, restaurants, online.
- **Services**: veterinary, mechanisation, training, consulting.

You don't have to own land to be in agribusiness.
## Choosing an opportunity | Proverbs 27:23-27; Luke 14:28
"Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds" (Proverbs 27:23).

Questions for choosing an enterprise:

- **Demand**: what do people in your area buy regularly? Who will buy from you?
- **Your resources**: land, water, money, time, skills.
- **Start-up cost** and **time to first income**: vegetables or broilers may pay in weeks; tree crops take years.
- **Risks**: disease, weather, theft, price changes. How can you reduce them?
- **Climate and soil** suitability.
- **Learning**: can you get training or mentorship?

Starter options often suitable for young people:

- Vegetables (pepper, tomato, leafy greens) in small plots or containers.
- Poultry (broilers or layers) at small scale.
- Catfish in tanks.
- Processing and packaging (e.g. pepper mix, plantain chips).
- Selling inputs or produce as a trader.

Visit successful farmers before you start. Learn from their mistakes.

# Planning and starting small | Building a simple plan and beginning well.
## A simple business plan | Proverbs 24:27; Proverbs 21:5; Luke 14:28-30
"Which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?" (Luke 14:28).

A one-page agribusiness plan:

1. **What**: the product (e.g. 200 broilers per batch).
2. **Who**: customers (households, eateries, market women) and how you'll reach them.
3. **How**: production steps, location, equipment, labour.
4. **When**: a timeline from preparation to sale.
5. **Costs**: start-up costs (housing, equipment) and running costs per batch (chicks, feed, drugs, transport).
6. **Income**: expected sales × price, with a realistic estimate of losses.
7. **Profit**: income minus all costs.
8. **Risks** and how you'll manage them.

Use realistic local prices. Ask sellers and farmers. Assume some losses (for example, mortality in poultry).
## Starting small and growing | Zechariah 4:10; Proverbs 13:11; Ecclesiastes 11:4
"For who hath despised the day of small things?" (Zechariah 4:10). "Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase" (Proverbs 13:11).

Principles:

- **Start small** enough to learn without losing everything. A first batch of 50 birds teaches as much as 500, with less risk.
- **Learn the skill** first: attend training (Agricultural Development Programmes, extension officers, farmers' groups, online courses).
- **Avoid debt** for a first venture where possible; use savings or small contributions.
- **Get good inputs**: certified seeds, healthy chicks or fingerlings from reliable sources.
- **Follow good practice**: vaccination schedules, clean water, proper spacing, pest control.
- **Grow from profit**, reinvesting step by step.
- **Join a cooperative** or farmers' group for shared learning, bulk buying and better prices.

"He that observeth the wind shall not sow" (Ecclesiastes 11:4). Don't wait for perfect conditions. Prepare wisely, then begin.

# Records and selling | Keeping accounts and reaching customers.
## Keeping records | Luke 16:1-2; Proverbs 27:23; 1 Corinthians 4:2
The steward in Luke 16 was told, "give an account of thy stewardship" (Luke 16:2). Good records show whether your business is truly making money.

Keep simple records in a notebook or spreadsheet:

- **Expenses**: date, item, quantity, cost (feed, seeds, labour, transport, drugs).
- **Sales**: date, customer, quantity, price, total, paid or owed.
- **Production**: planting dates, harvest quantities, eggs per day, bird mortality, weights.
- **Stock**: inputs on hand.

At the end of each batch or month:

- **Total income** − **total expenses** = **profit or loss**.
- **Cost per unit**: total cost ÷ number sold (e.g. cost per bird). Make sure your price is above this.

**Separate** business money from personal money. Pay yourself a set amount rather than spending business money freely.
## Marketing honestly | Proverbs 11:1; Proverbs 20:23; Proverbs 22:1
"A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight" (Proverbs 11:1). Honesty is good business and good faith.

Marketing basics:

- **Know your customers**: households, restaurants, schools, hotels, market traders.
- **Quality and consistency**: fresh, clean, well-sorted produce brings repeat buyers.
- **Packaging and branding**: simple labels with your name and phone number build recognition.
- **Pricing**: cover your costs and a fair profit; check market prices.
- **Channels**: local markets, direct to customers, WhatsApp status and groups, Instagram and Facebook, aggregators and off-takers.
- **Timing**: plan production for seasons of high demand (festive seasons) and avoid gluts.
- **Customer service**: deliver on time, answer calls, keep promises.

**Integrity**: use accurate scales and measures; don't hide bad produce under good; don't use dangerous chemicals to ripen or preserve food. "A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches" (Proverbs 22:1).
