# The shape of a story | How stories work and why they move people.
## Why stories matter | Psalm 107:2; Mark 5:18-20; 2 Samuel 12:1-7
"Let the redeemed of the LORD say so" (Psalm 107:2). God's people have always told stories of what He has done. Jesus sent the healed man home: "Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee" (Mark 5:19). Nathan confronted King David not with a lecture but with a story of a poor man's lamb (2 Samuel 12:1-7).

Stories reach the heart because they show truth in a real life. Readers remember stories long after they forget arguments.

A **testimony** tells how God worked in a person's life. A **ministry report** tells what God did through an outreach, crusade or project. Both are true stories, so they must be told truthfully and well. Exaggeration dishonours God, who does not need our help to look great.
## The story arc | Genesis 37:1-36; Genesis 50:20
Most stories follow an **arc**:

1. **Setting and character**: who, where, when. "In 2019, Grace was a final-year student in Ogbomoso."
2. **Desire or problem**: what the person wants or faces. "She had failed the same course twice."
3. **Struggle**: attempts, obstacles, rising tension.
4. **Turning point**: a moment of change, often where God intervenes or the person decides.
5. **Resolution**: what happened as a result.
6. **Meaning**: what it shows about God or life (often stated briefly, or left for the reader to see).

Joseph's story (Genesis 37 to 50) has every element: a favoured son, betrayal, slavery, prison, a turning point before Pharaoh, and the meaning stated at the end: "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good" (Genesis 50:20).

**Show, don't just tell**: "She cried in the bus all the way home" shows sadness better than "She was very sad."

# Interviewing and listening | Gathering stories from other people.
## Preparing and asking | Proverbs 18:13; Proverbs 20:5; James 1:19
"Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water; but a man of understanding will draw it out" (Proverbs 20:5). Interviewing is drawing out a story.

Before the interview:

- **Explain your purpose**: what you are writing and where it will appear.
- **Ask permission** to record or take notes.
- **Prepare open questions**.

Good questions:

- "Tell me about your life before ___."
- "What happened then? What did you feel?"
- "What was the turning point?"
- "What has changed since?"
- "Can you describe that moment: where you were, what you saw?"

Avoid yes/no questions and questions that suggest the answer ("Wasn't it amazing?"). Ask for **specifics**: names (with permission), places, dates, numbers, exact words spoken.

"Be swift to hear, slow to speak" (James 1:19). Let silences sit; people often share the most important thing after a pause.
## Listening, notes and checking | Luke 1:1-4; Proverbs 18:17
Luke's Gospel was drawn from "eyewitnesses" (Luke 1:2), carefully researched. Careful listening produces accurate writing.

During the interview:

- **Listen more than you write.** Note key words, phrases and quotes.
- **Record exact quotes** carefully; if you aren't sure of the wording, ask them to repeat it.
- **Ask follow-up questions**: "What do you mean by that?"
- **Notice details**: setting, gestures, emotions.

After the interview:

- **Write up your notes the same day**, while memory is fresh.
- **Check facts**: dates, names, places. "He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him" (Proverbs 18:17). Where possible, confirm with another source.
- **Read sensitive parts back** to the person, or show them the draft, to make sure it is accurate and that they are comfortable with it.

# Reports and consent | Ministry reports, privacy and honesty.
## Writing ministry reports | Acts 14:27; Acts 15:3-4; Acts 21:19
When Paul and Barnabas returned to Antioch, "they had gathered the church together, they rehearsed all that God had done with them, and how he had opened the door of faith unto the Gentiles" (Acts 14:27). Paul later "declared particularly what things God had wrought among the Gentiles by his ministry" (Acts 21:19).

A good **ministry report** (for a crusade, outreach or project) includes:

- **What, where, when, who**: the event, location, dates, team.
- **What happened**: activities, attendance, decisions, follow-up arrangements.
- **Stories**: one or two short, true accounts that show the impact.
- **Numbers**, honestly counted and clearly explained ("312 decision cards were collected").
- **Challenges and lessons**: what went wrong and what will change.
- **Thanks**: partners, volunteers, hosts.
- **Next steps** and prayer points.
- **Photos** with captions (and consent).

Give God the glory. Report numbers accurately; inflated figures are lies, however good the motive.
## Consent, privacy and dignity | Exodus 20:16; Proverbs 11:13; Matthew 7:12
"Thou shalt not bear false witness" (Exodus 20:16). "A talebearer revealeth secrets: but he that is of a faithful spirit concealeth the matter" (Proverbs 11:13).

Rules for responsible storytelling:

- **Get consent** before publishing anyone's story, name or photo. Explain where it will appear. Written consent is best.
- **Children**: get a parent's or guardian's consent; don't publish a child's full name, school or location.
- **Change names and details** when needed for safety, and say so ("names have been changed").
- **Protect dignity**: don't use photos of people in distress, sickness or poverty in ways that shame them. Ask: would I want my picture used this way?
- **Don't exaggerate** healings, numbers or results. Report what you know, and say what you don't.
- **Let people withdraw** their story if they change their minds.
- **Avoid revealing** sensitive matters (health, family conflict, past sins) unless the person clearly wants them shared.

"Whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them" (Matthew 7:12).
