# Why small groups | The church in the home.
## House to house | Acts 2:46-47; Acts 5:42; Romans 16:5
The first believers met "daily with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread from house to house" (Acts 2:46). Paul greeted "the church that is in their house" (Romans 16:5). Large gatherings and small groups worked together.

Small groups help believers:

- **Know and be known**: in a crowd you can hide; in a group of ten you are missed.
- **Study the Word** with discussion and questions.
- **Pray** for one another by name.
- **Care** practically: visits, meals, help in crisis.
- **Grow** through accountability and encouragement.
- **Reach** neighbours who may never enter a church building.
- **Develop leaders**: many leaders first lead a small group.

House fellowships are not rivals to Sunday church; they are part of it, under the oversight of church leaders.
## The leader's role | 1 Peter 5:2-3; John 10:3-4; Hebrews 13:17
A small group leader is a **shepherd of a few**. Jesus "calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out" (John 10:3).

The leader's role:

- **Pray** for each member.
- **Prepare** the meeting.
- **Facilitate**, not dominate: help others discover and share.
- **Care**: notice who is absent, sick or struggling, and follow up.
- **Communicate** with church leadership; report needs and concerns.
- **Model** a growing life with God.
- **Raise others** to lead.

You don't need to be a Bible scholar. You need to love God, love people, prepare faithfully and be teachable. "Feed the flock of God which is among you... being ensamples to the flock" (1 Peter 5:2-3).

# Leading the meeting | Preparation, flow and discussion.
## A meeting plan | 1 Corinthians 14:26; Colossians 3:16; Acts 2:42
"When ye come together, every one of you hath a psalm, hath a doctrine... Let all things be done unto edifying" (1 Corinthians 14:26).

A typical 60 to 90 minute meeting:

1. **Welcome** (10 min): greet everyone, light refreshment, an icebreaker question ("What was the best part of your week?").
2. **Worship** (10 min): a few songs or a psalm read together.
3. **Word** (30 to 40 min): read the passage; discuss with observation, interpretation and application questions.
4. **Prayer** (15 min): share requests; pray for each other, often in pairs or small clusters.
5. **Outreach and care** (5 min): who are we praying for and reaching? Who needs a visit?
6. **Close** on time.

Preparation: study the passage early in the week, write or adapt questions, pray for members by name, and confirm the venue.
## Facilitating discussion | Proverbs 18:2; James 1:19; Luke 24:17
Jesus asked the travellers on the road to Emmaus, "What manner of communications are these that ye have one to another?" (Luke 24:17) and let them speak first.

Good facilitation:

- **Ask open questions**: "What do you notice in verse 3?", "How might this apply at work?"
- **Wait** after asking; silence gives people time to think.
- **Affirm** contributions: "Thank you, that's helpful."
- **Redirect** to the text: "Where do you see that in the passage?"
- **Draw in quiet members** gently: "Ada, what do you think?" (without forcing).
- **Manage talkative members** kindly: "Let's hear from someone who hasn't shared yet."
- **Handle wrong answers** gracefully: "Interesting. Let's look at verse 5 together."
- **Park side issues**: "Good question; let's talk after the meeting."
- **Summarise** the main point before prayer.

"Be swift to hear, slow to speak" (James 1:19).

# Care, outreach and multiplication | A group that grows outward.
## Caring for one another | Galatians 6:2; Romans 12:10-13; James 5:14-16
"Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ" (Galatians 6:2). The "one another" commands of the New Testament are lived out best in small groups: love, honour, serve, pray for, encourage, forgive one another.

Practical care:

- **Follow up absences** with a call or visit.
- **Celebrate**: birthdays, births, graduations, new jobs.
- **Support in crisis**: bereavement, illness, job loss; organise meals, visits and practical help.
- **Pray** together and for each other during the week.
- **Share resources** generously where there is need (Acts 2:44-45), with wisdom.
- **Refer** serious matters to church leaders: marital crisis, abuse, severe illness, discipline issues.
- **Keep confidences**: what is shared in the group stays in the group, unless someone is at risk.
## Reaching out and multiplying | Acts 16:13-15, 31-34; 2 Timothy 2:2
In Philippi the gospel spread through households: Lydia "and her household" (Acts 16:15) and the jailer "and all his house" (16:31-34).

**Reaching neighbours**:

- Each member prays for three to five people by name.
- Invite neighbours to social events, special meetings or Bible discussions.
- Serve the street: help the elderly, clean-up days, support in emergencies.
- Welcome newcomers warmly; avoid insider language.

**Multiplying**:

- When a group grows beyond about 12 to 15, discussion and care suffer.
- From the start, train an **apprentice leader** (2 Timothy 2:2): let them lead parts of meetings, then whole meetings.
- When ready, **birth a new group** with the apprentice leading, ideally with a few members going with them.
- Celebrate multiplication as success, not loss.
