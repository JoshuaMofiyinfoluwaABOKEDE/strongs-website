# Biblical foundations | Why and how churches were planted.
## The church planting mandate | Matthew 16:18; Matthew 28:18-20; Acts 1:8
Jesus said, "I will build my church" (Matthew 16:18) and sent His disciples to "teach all nations, baptizing them... Teaching them to observe all things" (Matthew 28:19-20). As the gospel spread from Jerusalem to Judaea, Samaria and beyond (Acts 1:8), it produced **churches**: communities of baptised disciples meeting together under leaders.

Why plant new churches?

- **Reach new people**: new churches often reach those whom established churches do not.
- **Reach new places**: neighbourhoods, estates, campuses, villages and people groups without a gospel witness.
- **Disciple effectively** in reachable communities.
- **Raise new leaders**.
- **Multiply**: healthy churches plant churches.

Church planting is not about competing with existing churches or building personal empires. It is about extending Christ's kingdom where it is needed.
## Paul's pattern | Acts 13:1-4; Acts 14:21-28; Acts 19:8-10
Paul's journeys show a pattern (sometimes called the **Pauline cycle**):

1. **Sent** by a praying church (Acts 13:1-4).
2. **Entered** a city, often starting where people already gathered (synagogues, Acts 13:14).
3. **Preached** the gospel.
4. **Gathered** believers (Acts 14:21; 16:15, 40).
5. **Taught** and confirmed them (Acts 14:22; 19:9-10).
6. **Appointed elders** (Acts 14:23).
7. **Commended** them to the Lord and moved on, while staying connected through visits and letters (Acts 14:23; 15:36).
8. **Reported** back to the sending church (Acts 14:26-27).

Notice: **teams** (Paul with Barnabas, Silas, Timothy, Luke, Priscilla and Aquila), **local leaders** appointed quickly, and **ongoing relationship**. Paul planted churches that could stand without him.

# Preparation | Call, team, research and plan.
## Call, team and sending church | Acts 13:2-3; Acts 16:9-10; Romans 15:20
Paul aimed "to preach the gospel, not where Christ was named, lest I should build upon another man's foundation" (Romans 15:20). The Holy Spirit directed him to Macedonia through a vision (Acts 16:9-10).

**Calling**: confirm the call through prayer, Scripture, the counsel of leaders and a track record of making disciples. Planting is demanding; a clear call sustains you.

**Sending church**: plant with a church or network that prays, commissions, supports and holds you accountable (Acts 13:3; 14:26-27).

**Team**: plant with others where possible: a leader, an evangelist, someone gifted in administration, worship, children's ministry, and prayer. Team members should share the vision, theology and values, and be willing to work hard and sacrificially.

**Planter's family**: the spouse must share the call; children need care through the transition.
## Research and plan | Numbers 13:17-20; Luke 14:28-30; Nehemiah 2:11-16
Moses sent spies to "see the land, what it is; and the people that dwelleth therein, whether they be strong or weak, few or many" (Numbers 13:18). Nehemiah inspected the walls before starting (Nehemiah 2:13).

**Community research**:

- Population, languages, age groups, economy, education, religion.
- Existing churches and their reach.
- Felt needs and problems.
- Gathering places: markets, schools, motor parks, estates.
- Key people: traditional rulers, community leaders, "persons of peace" (Luke 10:6).
- Security and legal considerations.

**Plant proposal**:

- Vision, mission and values.
- Target community and why.
- Team and sending church.
- Strategy and timeline (first contacts → small groups → public launch → organised church).
- Budget and funding (including how the church will become self-supporting).
- Prayer plan.

# Gathering and growing | Evangelism, discipleship and launch.
## Evangelism and first disciples | Acts 16:13-15, 25-34; Luke 10:5-7
In Philippi, Paul began by a riverside where women gathered to pray. "Lydia... whose heart the Lord opened" was baptised "and her household" and opened her home (Acts 16:14-15). Then the jailer "and all his" believed (16:33). The church started in homes.

Early evangelism:

- **Pray** intensely for the community.
- **Meet people** where they are; serve visible needs.
- **Find persons of peace** and their households.
- **Share the gospel** personally and through events (outreaches, Bible discussions, film nights, health days).
- **Start discovery Bible groups** with seekers.
- **Baptise** new believers and **gather them** into small groups quickly.

Focus on **making disciples**, not merely gathering a crowd. A small group of genuine disciples is a stronger foundation than a large crowd of spectators.
## Launching and organising | Acts 2:41-47; 1 Corinthians 14:40; Hebrews 10:24-25
The Jerusalem church shows the marks of a healthy congregation: teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, prayer, generosity, worship and daily growth (Acts 2:42-47).

From small groups to a congregation:

- Start **regular worship** when there are enough committed disciples to sustain it (some planters wait until several small groups are healthy).
- Choose a **simple, accessible venue**: a school hall, home, rented room or community centre.
- Prepare for a **public launch**: invite contacts, serve the community, keep it simple and focused on Christ.
- **Organise basics**: children's ministry, worship, hospitality, follow-up, finances with controls.
- **Register** legally when appropriate (in Nigeria, often under the sending church's incorporated trustees, or separately via the CAC).
- **Teach** consistently through books of the Bible.
- **Keep evangelising**: launches often attract believers from other churches; stay focused on reaching the lost.

# Leaders, sustainability and multiplication | Building to last and to reproduce.
## Leadership and sustainability | Acts 14:23; Titus 1:5-9; 2 Corinthians 8:1-5
Paul appointed elders "in every church" within months (Acts 14:23) and listed qualities in Titus 1:5-9. The Macedonian churches, though poor, "first gave their own selves to the Lord" and gave generously beyond their means (2 Corinthians 8:1-5).

**Leadership pipeline**:

- Identify faithful people early (2 Timothy 2:2).
- Give real responsibilities: small group leaders, ushers, children's teachers, worship.
- Train continuously: Bible, character, ministry skills.
- Appoint elders and deacons by biblical qualifications, not just availability.
- Prepare a successor for the planter.

**Sustainability**:

- Teach **generous, joyful giving** from the start.
- Plan for the church to cover its own costs within an agreed time (e.g. two to four years).
- Keep **costs low**; avoid expensive commitments too early.
- Develop **bivocational** leaders where helpful (Paul made tents, Acts 18:3).
- Maintain **financial transparency**.
## Multiplication | Acts 19:10; 1 Thessalonians 1:6-8; 2 Timothy 2:2
From Ephesus, "all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word" (Acts 19:10): churches like Colossae were planted by Paul's disciples such as Epaphras (Colossians 1:7). The Thessalonian church became an example, and "from you sounded out the word of the Lord" (1 Thessalonians 1:8).

Healthy churches **reproduce**:

- Build the **DNA of multiplication** from the beginning: every disciple makes disciples; every group raises a new leader; the church plans to plant.
- **Identify** the next community to reach.
- **Train** future planters in the current church.
- **Send** people and resources generously, even when it feels costly.
- **Partner** with other churches and networks.
- **Celebrate** each new plant.

The goal is not one church but a **movement** of churches making disciples who make disciples, until the whole community, city and nation hears.
