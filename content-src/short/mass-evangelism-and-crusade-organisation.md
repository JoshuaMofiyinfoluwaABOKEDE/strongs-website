# Why mass evangelism | Preaching to multitudes.
## Public proclamation in Scripture | Acts 2:14-41; Acts 8:5-8; Mark 16:15-20
At Pentecost, Peter "standing up with the eleven, lifted up his voice" to a crowd in Jerusalem, and "about three thousand souls" were added that day (Acts 2:14, 41). Philip "preached Christ" in Samaria; "the people with one accord gave heed", miracles followed, "And there was great joy in that city" (Acts 8:5-8). Jesus sent His disciples to "preach the gospel to every creature", and "the Lord working with them, and confirming the word with signs following" (Mark 16:15, 20).

Public, open-air preaching to crowds is a biblical method, used alongside personal evangelism and teaching in homes.

Reinhard Bonnke built his ministry on this conviction. He taught that the evangelist's task is to preach the cross clearly, call people to repentance and faith, and trust the Holy Spirit to confirm the Word. Across Africa, including many Nigerian cities, his campaigns drew enormous crowds and recorded millions of decisions for Christ. Whatever the size, the principle is the same: **Christ crucified, preached with power, to as many as possible.**
## Purpose and limits | 1 Corinthians 2:1-5; 1 Corinthians 3:6-9; Matthew 28:19-20
"I determined not to know any thing among you, save Jesus Christ, and him crucified" (1 Corinthians 2:2).

A crusade's purpose:

- **Proclaim the gospel** to many who would never enter a church.
- **Call for decisions**: repentance and faith in Christ.
- **Demonstrate** God's love and power.
- **Mobilise** and unite local churches in evangelism.
- **Feed new believers into local churches** for discipleship.

Limits to remember:

- A crusade is **sowing and reaping**, not the whole harvest process. "I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6).
- **Decisions are not disciples.** Without follow-up, much fruit is lost.
- **The evangelist is not the star.** Christ is.
- **Integrity matters**: honest reporting of numbers and healings; no manipulation.

# Planning the crusade | From vision to the eve of the meetings.
## Prayer, partners and committees | Acts 13:1-3; Nehemiah 2:17-18; Philippians 1:3-5
Every crusade begins in prayer. The Antioch church fasted and prayed before sending Paul and Barnabas (Acts 13:2-3). Large campaigns often hold months of prayer meetings in the host city.

**Partners**: invite local churches across denominations to own the crusade. Meet pastors early; agree on the statement of faith, follow-up and counselling. Paul thanked the Philippians "for your fellowship in the gospel" (Philippians 1:5).

**Committees** (scaled to the event):

- **Prayer**
- **Venue and logistics**: ground, stage, sound, lighting, power, toilets, water, seating.
- **Security and safety**: police liaison, stewards, first aid, crowd management.
- **Publicity**: posters, banners, radio, social media, invitation teams.
- **Counselling**: training and organising counsellors.
- **Follow-up**: data, churches, visitation.
- **Finance**: budget, giving, accounts.
- **Protocol and hospitality**: speakers, guests, traditional leaders.
- **Music and programme**.

Appoint a **coordinator** to bring the committees together weekly.
## Venue, permits, budget and publicity | Luke 14:28-30; Romans 13:1-7; Habakkuk 2:2
"Count the cost" (Luke 14:28).

**Timeline** (for a medium crusade, about six months):

- 6 months: vision, prayer, partners, date, venue options.
- 4 months: committees, budget, permits, counsellor recruitment.
- 2 months: publicity begins, counsellor training, prayer walks.
- 1 month: invitation campaigns, equipment booked, security plan final.
- 1 week: site set-up plan, rehearsals, final briefings.

**Permits**: approval from the land owner or council, the police (and other security agencies where required), and courtesy visits to traditional rulers. "Render... to all their dues" (Romans 13:7).

**Budget**: venue, stage and sound, power (generators, fuel), publicity, transport, materials (Bibles, booklets, decision cards), security, first aid, hospitality, contingency.

**Publicity**: clear, honest messages; local languages; posters, banners, radio, WhatsApp, door-to-door invitation teams. "Make it plain" (Habakkuk 2:2). Never promise guaranteed miracles.

# The event and follow-up | Running the meetings and keeping the fruit.
## On the ground | Acts 2:40-42; 1 Corinthians 14:40; Proverbs 22:3
"Let all things be done decently and in order" (1 Corinthians 14:40). "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself" (Proverbs 22:3).

**Programme** (typical evening):

- Gathering music and prayer.
- Welcome and short testimonies.
- Worship that the crowd can join, in local languages.
- **The gospel message**: clear, Christ-centred, 30 to 45 minutes.
- **The appeal**: explained clearly; respondents come forward.
- **Prayer** with those who respond; counsellors meet them.
- Prayer for the sick and needy, with integrity and care.
- Closing and safe dispersal.

**Safety**: crowd barriers, clear exits, stewards in visible vests, first aid points, lost-children point, police coordination, safe electrical installations, and a plan for rain or emergencies.

**Counselling area**: well-lit, organised by language and gender, with enough trained counsellors (at least one for every few respondents expected), decision cards, pens, Gospels and booklets.
## Follow-up: the real harvest | Acts 14:21-23; Acts 15:36; Colossians 1:28
Paul returned to new converts "confirming the souls of the disciples" (Acts 14:22), and later said, "Let us go again and visit our brethren in every city... and see how they do" (Acts 15:36).

Bonnke's ministry stressed that converts must be handed to local churches; a crusade without follow-up wastes the harvest.

Follow-up system:

1. **Decision cards** collected and entered into a database each night.
2. **Sorted** by area and language, and assigned to partner churches near each convert.
3. **Contact within 48 hours**: call or visit.
4. **New believers' classes** in partner churches and through STRONGS Bible Clubs.
5. **Tracking**: churches report contacts made and people attending.
6. **Review** after one, three and six months.

**Report honestly**: attendance estimates, decisions recorded, contacts reached, people now in churches. Thank partners. Celebrate what God has done.

The aim is Paul's: "that we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus" (Colossians 1:28).

1. Vision, purpose and partner churches.
2. Committee structure with responsibilities.
3. A six-month timeline.
4. A budget outline.
5. A site, safety and counselling plan.
6. A follow-up system with responsibilities and review points.
